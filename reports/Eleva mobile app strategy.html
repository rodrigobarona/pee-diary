One app or two? Start with the diary, and earn the rest.

Six researchers gathered the evidence. Five judges with different briefs scored four ways to structure Eleva's native apps. All five ranked the same option first.

Verdict

Ship Eleva Diary first as its own small native app. Do not build the all-in-one app. Decide on a member app later, using data from the diary and from the web launch.

Is it worth having two separate apps?
A standalone diary is worth it. Building two apps at the same time is not. Build the diary as a reusable module so a second app stays a packaging choice, not a rewrite.
Should one app carry members, experts, lectures, video, messaging and the diary?
No. Every judge ranked it last or second to last. Expert tools, clinic admin and the Academy stay on the web, which is how comparable platforms run them.
What is the catch?
Nobody has published how many free-diary users go on to book paid care. Four cheap tests can tell you before you write Swift or Kotlin. They are in the plan.

The options

You asked about two shapes. The panel scored four.

A and B are the two you described. C and D were added because the research showed the choice is less binary than it looks: a diary can work without an account inside a larger app, and a diary built as a module can live in more than one app.

AYour option 1

Two apps, built in parallel

Eleva Diary (free, no account needed, optional Eleva sign-in) and Eleva Care (members, experts, Academy, video, messaging). Linked by single sign-on and deep links.

BYour option 2

One all-in-one app

A single Eleva Care app for members, experts, lectures, video, messaging, with the diary as a section. No standalone diary.

CRecommended

Diary first, member app later

Ship Eleva Diary alone, with a no-account mode. Booking and video open on the existing web. A member app may follow and reuse the same diary module. Experts and Academy stay on web.

DRunner-up

One member app with a guest diary

A single Eleva Care member app where anyone can use the diary without an account. Diary-specific store pages target diary searches. Experts stay on web.

How firm is the result?

  • Weights barely matter. Doubling growth criteria, doubling cost criteria, or doubling compliance criteria all keep C first by 9 to 14 points.
  • D closes the gap when you look only at the long run. Ignore build cost and triple maintenance, and C leads 67 to 64. Score only cross-sell and maintenance, and D wins 74 to 54.
  • Two facts could flip C to D: near-zero search demand for the diary terms together with clinicians who would hand out an "Eleva Care" app just as readily, or Apple treating a diary in two apps as a duplicate.
  • The first step is the same either way. C and D both begin with a local-first diary module. The engineering judge noted that the Diary listing can later grow into the member app without moving user data.
Your two hypotheses

Both are partly right, for different reasons than expected.

"Two apps help marketing, because people can find the diary without wanting Eleva."

True for clinicians, weak for store search.

  • App store search for this term is small. The category leader, iUFlow, has 50K+ Android installs after about ten years. On Google Play Portugal, only 3 of the first 12 results for "diário miccional" are bladder apps.
  • People keep a bladder diary because a clinician asks them to. The European urology guideline strongly recommends a diary of at least 3 days.
  • Clinicians hand out single-purpose, easy, private tools. A neutral diary is something a urologist outside Eleva can recommend without endorsing a marketplace. This is reasoning from an Irish survey and the Squeezy precedent in the UK, not a Portuguese measurement.
  • There is a real gap: no free, no-account diary in European Portuguese with a free PDF export. The main multilingual rival charges $1.99 per PDF.

"One app makes cross-selling easier."

True mechanically, but the loss is avoidable and the gain is smaller than it looks.

  • Asking someone to install a second app loses roughly a quarter to a half of the people who tap. That is the one measured cost of two apps.
  • You can remove that step. The "book a specialist" button in the diary opens Eleva booking on the web, with the diary PDF attached by the user's choice. No second install sits before the first booking.
  • Prompts based on what the user logged are restricted in any structure. They need explicit consent under GDPR Article 9, Apple limits use of health data for marketing, and personalised prompts push the diary toward medical-device status. Only a static prompt, the same for everyone, is safe. That shrinks the advantage of having everything in one app.
  • Many diary users already have a clinician. The funnel into paid Eleva care may be thin however the apps are arranged. This is the weakest assumption in every option.
Research panel

What six researchers found

Each block gives the main findings and what could not be found. Some figures came from search summaries, so check a number at its source before you build on it. The full notes and a cited synthesis are in the repository.

Most satellite apps get folded back

  • Of 19 documented cases of a company splitting a feature into its own app, 16 were shut or merged back: Dropbox Carousel, Instagram Boomerang, LinkedIn Job Search, Google Duo, and Fitbit into Google Health in 2026.
  • Digital health has consolidated: Headspace and Ginger, Teladoc, and Hinge Health, which put pelvic care inside its existing app.
  • Where two health apps persist, the split follows who pays or who the audience is, not a feature.
  • Counter-example in this exact field: Squeezy, a narrow pelvic floor app built by NHS physiotherapists, is the top paid medical app in the UK.

Not found: any small health company that ran a free tracker app to feed a care marketplace and published the results.

The diary niche is small, weakly served and clinician-driven

  • iUFlow is English-only and was last updated on iOS in November 2024. Bladderly has Portuguese and Spanish but sells a $4.99 "3-Day Pass" and rates 3.5 on Google Play.
  • An ICS 2024 review of ten diary apps scored them 7.6 out of 19 on average and found none with a second language.
  • App diaries work once prescribed: in a Spanish validation, 90.4% of 136 patients completed the app diary and 88.2% would choose it over paper.
  • The need is large: 9.9% of adult women in Portugal report urinary incontinence. The EAU guideline strongly recommends a diary of at least 3 days.

Not found: search volume for any diary keyword in Portuguese or Spanish, and how many diaries clinicians hand out each year.

A second install is the measurable cost of two apps

  • The app title carries the most ranking weight in both stores, so a focused app has an edge on its own keyword. Since July 2025 one app can show a diary-specific store page for diary searches, but the name and icon stay "Eleva Care".
  • Vendor data puts web-to-app completion at 40 to 60% of people who tap. Even brand-search ads convert only 73.5% of taps to installs.
  • Among 158 Irish physiotherapists, 54.5% had prescribed apps. They chose on ease of use (96%), clear purpose (77%) and privacy (76%).
  • A paid install costs about $1.01 in Portugal, so a real demand test costs a few hundred euros.

Not found: any benchmark for how many users of a free companion app install or pay for the publisher's main product.

The proven split is member versus expert, and experts stay mostly on web

  • Doctolib, Doctoralia, knok and Teladoc all ship a separate provider app. None was found merging it back into the customer app.
  • Provider apps are costly for a small audience: Maven's practitioner app has 6 ratings, 171.5 MB and ships monthly. BetterHelp therapists ran video from a computer.
  • Sharetribe's 2026 guidance: web first, native for one side after validation, never both sides at launch.
  • Android holds about 71% of mobile usage in Portugal, so iOS and Android must ship together.

Not found: how many users of an all-in-one health app use its secondary tracker, and what share of sessions on comparable platforms are joined from phones.

Better Auth has no Swift or Kotlin SDK

  • The only official mobile integration is Expo. Two server-supported routes exist: REST with the bearer plugin, or the OAuth 2.1 provider plugin with PKCE in the system browser.
  • Offering Google login on iOS makes Sign in with Apple (or an equivalent) mandatory under guideline 4.8.
  • Sharing a session between two apps is silent on iOS through a shared Keychain group. Android has no clean equivalent.
  • Daily's native SDKs ship no prebuilt call screen, so a native call UI is built twice. App Clips are too limited and Google ended Instant Apps in December 2025, so there is no light substitute for a second listing.

Not found: a neutral figure for the cost of two native codebases, or for how much coding agents reduce it.

App scope moves the regulatory footprint, not the law

  • Under MDCG 2019-11 Rev.1, software that only stores and displays data is not a medical device. Software that interprets an individual's data is, usually Class IIa.
  • Apple's "Data Not Collected" label is available only to a diary that sends nothing off the device. It disappears when optional sync ships.
  • Paid 1:1 live sessions may use your own payments on both stores. Lectures and courses are digital goods: Apple's EU terms from 1 October 2026 charge 26% for in-app purchase and 15% for link-outs.
  • In Portugal, remote care must be registered with ERS before operating. This applies to the web platform today and is the same in every option.

Not found: any ruling on bladder diaries specifically, or an ERS position on marketplaces.

Plan

Five steps, each one earned by the step before

Durations assume the small team you have and heavy use of coding agents. They are estimates. The thresholds come from the judges and are judgment calls, so adjust them, but write them down before you see the data.

1Now · about 4 weeks

Measure before native code

  • Publish a "diário miccional" page on eleva.care in PT, ES and EN with a free printable 3-day diary and a QR code. Give it to your experts as their hand-out. Paper is the real competitor.
  • Pull keyword demand for the diary terms in the PT, ES and BR stores (Apple Ads popularity) and on the web (Keyword Planner). Under €50.
  • Ask 10 Eleva experts and 10 outside clinicians which they would hand out this week: a neutral "Eleva Diary" or an "Eleva Care" app with a diary inside.
  • Put the existing Expo app in front of 30 to 50 real patients through TestFlight and a Play closed test, after removing iCloud storage and Sentry. Use it as a measuring tool only.
  • Send three questions in writing: to INFARMED on whether logging and charts are outside the device definition, to a Portuguese health lawyer on who registers with ERS in a Stripe Connect marketplace, and to App Review on iCloud backup and on the same diary in two apps.
  • Run two technical spikes: sign-in through the system browser against Better Auth, and porting one diary screen from SwiftUI to Compose with a coding agent.

GateGo if demand is at least 500 monthly searches in Portugal or at least 5 of 10 outside clinicians pick the neutral diary, and at least 70% of pilot patients complete 3 days. If search is at the floor and clinicians see no difference between the two names, switch to option D. If nothing moves, keep the web page and PDF only.

2About 10 weeks, time-boxed

Eleva Diary 1.0

  • Native on iOS and Android together. Strictly on the device: no account, no sync, no third-party analytics or crash SDK, local reminders, PDF export through the share sheet.
  • Built as a module on each platform (DiaryKit Swift package, :feature:diary Gradle module) inside a thin app shell.
  • One static link, the same for every user, on the completion and export screens: "Discuss this diary with a pelvic health specialist". It opens Eleva booking on the web. Eleva's name and a booking QR code go on the PDF.
  • Store title carries the literal keyword per language. Home-screen name, icon and lock-screen text stay discreet.
  • Record-keeping wording only. Leave out trend labels, goals against a norm and any "supports diagnosis" claim.

Gate90 days after launch: under 300 installs across both stores, or under 5% of installs arriving on eleva.care through the tagged link, means the diary goes to maintenance only.

3After the web marketplace has paid sessions

Diary 1.x: sign-in, sync, share

  • Add diary endpoints to apps/api (batch upsert keyed by client UUID, changes-since cursor, share grants behind ff.diary_share) and publish them in the OpenAPI spec.
  • Optional Eleva sign-in through the system browser, Sign in with Apple, sync, and sharing with one named expert for a set period. Three separate opt-ins.
  • Build the expert's side on the web: request a diary, read the shared diary before the session, ask for a repeat at 6 to 12 weeks.
  • Before this release: complete the data protection impact assessment, add in-app account deletion, and change the Apple privacy label to "Health, linked to you".

Gate180 days after the diary launch: decide on a member app. Under 30 diary-attributed first bookings, or under 1% of installs once installs pass 1,000, means no member app for acquisition reasons.

4Conditional

Eleva Care member app

  • Build it when your own analytics show the need: at least 60% of session joins on phones with more than 10% failing or starting late in the mobile browser, or a rebooking interval of two weeks or less.
  • Scope: session reminders, joining a call, messages, rebooking, and the diary module. Nothing for experts.
  • Choose then between a second listing and growing the Diary listing into the member app. Take App Review's answer on duplicate apps into that choice. If the clinician channel has not proved that a neutral standalone earns its place, grow the existing listing.
5Only on evidence

Expert companion, native video

  • A thin expert app (agenda and new-booking alerts) only if experts ask for it and session logs show they work from phones.
  • Native video only if browser calls are costing bookings or call quality. Academy purchases stay on the web to avoid store commission.

Leave out of native in the first year

  • Expert, clinic and staff tools. Desk work, tiny audience.
  • Academy purchase and playback. Store commission and a second payment regime, for a product that does not exist on the web yet.
  • A native call screen and in-app chat. Each is built twice.
  • Any interpretation of diary data: colour-coded normal ranges, "improving" labels, AI summaries, "you should see a specialist".
  • HealthKit, Health Connect, widgets, watch apps. The platforms only have a water-intake type for this use.
Architecture

Thin app shells over shared modules

The structure of the code is what keeps the app-count decision reversible. Each app is a thin shell. Everything that matters lives in modules that any shell can include.

Sign-in

  • Use the web login inside the system browser sheet (ASWebAuthenticationSession on iOS, Auth Tab on Android). Passkeys, magic link, Google and two-factor all work with no native code per method.
  • Your server already has bearer, jwt, passkey, magicLink, twoFactor, Google and Microsoft. It does not have the OAuth provider plugin or Apple sign-in.
  • Your trusted-origins filter accepts only https eleva.care hosts, so use an https universal-link redirect, not a custom URL scheme.
  • Pin the Better Auth version and add contract tests. It shipped six patches in ten days and 1.8 has breaking changes.
  • If the spike fails: web login ending in a one-time token that the app exchanges for a bearer session.

Diary data and sync

  • Local SQLite on both platforms, client-generated UUIDs, a server version per record, deletions kept as tombstones.
  • With no account, rows have no owner and nothing leaves the device. Backup is an export file, not iCloud.
  • On first sign-in the app pushes its local entries through the normal batch endpoint. The server removes duplicates by UUID.
  • Sync is a hand-written REST delta. A sync vendor would add another processor of health data for a single-user, append-mostly diary.
  • Keep iOS and Android honest with shared JSON test vectors and golden PDFs. Revisit Kotlin Multiplatform only if the two drift.

If a second app ships

  • iOS: a shared Keychain access group signs the second app in with no prompt.
  • Android: decide before the second app's first upload whether both share one Play app-signing key. Without it, expect a short browser sheet or a passkey prompt.
  • Serve the passkey association files from the apex eleva.care with no redirect, listing every app.

Changes to your existing plan

  • docs/eleva-v3/mobile-integration-spec.md assumes Expo and Expo push. Amend ADR-010 for native clients and direct APNs and FCM.
  • The spec's default of "synced to the Eleva account" should become local by default, with sync as an explicit opt-in.
  • The spec's three visibility states, time-boxed sharing and ff.diary_share all still hold.
  • The Android package name shows in the Play URL. Pick a neutral one before the first production upload; it cannot change later.

Rules and risks

Rules that hold whichever option you choose

Three things in the proof of concept cannot carry over

  • iCloud storage. Apple guideline 5.1.3(ii) says apps may not store personal health information in iCloud.
  • Sentry with screenshots and replays. For a bladder diary, the fact that someone uses the app is itself health data. Any telemetry ends the "Data Not Collected" label.
  • Trend labels, goals and "support medical diagnosis" wording. Interpreting an individual's data for a medical purpose is what makes software a medical device.

Product rules

  • No flags, thresholds, normal-range colours, personalised advice or AI summaries of diary data unless you choose the CE-marking route on purpose.
  • Store listings and onboarding use record-keeping words. Never "monitor", "detect", "diagnose" or "treat" next to a condition name.
  • Promotion of Eleva Care inside the diary is static and identical for everyone. Nothing is triggered by what a user logged or by the fact that she uses the diary.
  • Account, sync and sharing are three separate opt-ins. Sharing is with one named expert for a set period.
  • Paid sessions stay one expert to one member, live and unrecorded.
  • Add a release gate, also in the instructions you give coding agents: any feature that calculates an index, compares to a norm, flags or summarises diary data needs a written device assessment before merge.

The strongest case against the recommendation

Every judge was asked to argue against their own pick. They converged on three points.

  • The prize may be small. Organic store installs for a new diary are likely hundreds to low thousands a year. A web page with a printable PDF and a few hundred euros of ads might test the same idea for far less.
  • The end state repeats a pattern companies undo. A diary in two apps means four store binaries, every diary fix shipped four times, and a possible duplicate-app question from Apple. Headspace was still merging two apps years after deciding to.
  • The privacy advantage is partly temporary. The "Data Not Collected" label disappears the day optional sync ships.

The skeptic's conclusion: commit only to steps 1 and 2, and size the diary so it cannot delay the web marketplace launch, which is the better use of the next two quarters.

Questions that need a professional answer

  • INFARMED or a regulatory adviser: are charts, averages and goal progress outside the device definition?
  • Portuguese health lawyer: under Stripe Connect, who registers with ERS, Eleva or each expert?
  • Data protection lawyer: is Eleva a controller for a strictly local diary, and what are the roles between Eleva and experts for shared diaries?
  • App Review: does 5.1.3(ii) cover ordinary iCloud device backups, and is the same diary module in two apps a duplicate under 4.3(a)?
  • Lawyer and tax adviser: are 1:1 session payments outside all store commissions in the EU?
Gaps and method

What this report cannot tell you

  • Tracker-to-care conversion. No company has published it. The nearest figures are a 2.18% median for freemium apps and 25 to 31% for US symptom checkers that lead to same-day visits. Neither is your case.
  • Keyword demand. No volume for any diary term in Portuguese or Spanish was accessible. You can pull it yourself in an afternoon.
  • Clinician behaviour in Portugal. The evidence is from Ireland, the UK, Norway and Spain.
  • Native cost. Every effort figure here is a judge's estimate.

How the panel worked

Six research agents each covered one topic with web sources: app portfolio precedents, the bladder diary market, growth and store mechanics, marketplace and telehealth app structures, native architecture with Better Auth, and EU regulation and store policy. They produced about 50,000 words of sourced notes. Five judge agents then read all the notes, plus your existing mobile spec and the proof-of-concept README, and scored the options without seeing each other's work: growth, engineering, compliance, product, and a designated skeptic.

Read the agreement with care. The judges are AI reviewers reading the same evidence, so five matching rankings are less independent than five people would be. Options C and D were written by me when briefing the panel. The scores are structured judgment, not measurement. The tests in step 1 are what turn this from an argued position into a measured one.

# Make Eleva reflow before the folds force it

Eleva Diary does not fit foldables today. It still works on them, but in two different broken ways. On Android the app targets API 36 and has no opt-out property in its manifest. On any unfolded book foldable (smallest width ≥ 600dp) the system already ignores the `portrait` lock, so the phone layout gets stretched to roughly 700–1000dp: a full-width bottom bar, single-column cards, charts spanning the whole screen and edge-to-edge sheets. On iOS the app is built with Xcode 26.5, so on Apple's iPhone Duo (on sale since 23 October 2026) it shows in a letterboxed "legacy" iPhone-sized window with black borders, the exact situation Duo owners are publicly complaining about. Both stopgaps run out in 2027. Google Play requires API 37 from August 2027, and the large-screen opt-out disappears with it. Moving to the iOS 27.1 SDK (Expo SDK 58, Xcode 27.1) makes the app resizable and side-by-side capable on the Duo's inner display, and it makes Duo screenshots mandatory from April 2027. The redesign is not a new app. It is one width-driven layout system with three breakpoints (compact < 600, medium 600–839, expanded ≥ 840 dp/pt) and four screen changes:
- Today becomes a two-column card grid, then a 70/30 dashboard with a side pane.
- History becomes a calendar-and-list beside an inline entry detail.
- Forms and sheets get a maximum width.
- The bottom bar becomes a leading-edge rail with the + at its top.

Foldables are still only about 1.5–2% of European phone sales, so the case rests less on audience size than on platform rules and store quality tiers. Those rules now treat a stretched or letterboxed phone layout as a failure.

## Two foldable shapes now matter, and the wide one is new

The 2026 lineup splits into two geometries, and Eleva has to handle both. The "square" book foldables keep a near-1:1 inner panel and a tall, narrow cover screen. The Galaxy Z Fold7 has an 8.0" 1968 × 2184 inner display and a 6.5" 21:9 cover ([GSMArena](https://m.gsmarena.com/samsung_galaxy_z_fold7-13826.php)). The Pixel 11 Pro Fold pairs an 8.0" 2076 × 2152 inner display with a 6.5" 1080 × 2342 outer one ([Google Store UK](https://store.google.com/gb/product/pixel_11_pro_fold_specs?hl=en-GB)). The new "wide" or passport shape arrived in 2026 from both leading vendors. Samsung's standard **Galaxy Z Fold8 has a 7.6" main display that opens to 4:3 and a 5.5" cover screen at 10:16** ([Samsung Newsroom](https://news.samsung.com/global/galaxy-unpacked-july-2026-a-first-look-at-galaxy-z-fold8-ultra-galaxy-z-fold8-and-galaxy-z-flip8)). Apple's **iPhone Duo has a 7.6" 1878 × 2670 inner display and a 5.4" 1398 × 2034 outer display**, both with ProMotion and Dynamic Island ([Apple iPhone Duo specs](https://www.apple.com/iphone-duo/specs/)). The Duo starts at $1,999, at €2,399 in Portugal and €2,339 in Spain ([Variety](https://variety.com/2026/digital/news/apple-iphone-duo-18-foldable-price-availability-1236855996/); [4gnews](https://4gnews.pt/iphone-duo-oficial/); [Applesfera](https://www.applesfera.com/iphone/donde-cuando-se-podra-comprar-iphone-duo-espana)). Its panels are about 1.42:1 and 1.45:1, far from the roughly 2.17:1 of a Pro iPhone. Unity's platform team summarised it this way: apps designed around 20:9 "must support a 4:3 aspect ratio … close to an iPad's aspect ratio" ([Unity Discussions](https://discussions.unity.com/t/info-ipados-27-sdk-27-adaptability-changes-iphone-duo-updates/1737980)).

Android's reference emulators match these two shapes: a **1:1 foldable at 802 × 802dp and a 4:3 foldable at 1024 × 768dp** ([Adaptive app quality](https://developer.android.com/docs/quality-guidelines/large-screen-app-quality)). Google says square inner displays "generally fall into the medium or expanded" class in both orientations, while wide 4:3 displays reach expanded in landscape and pair with a 10:16 cover ([Learn about foldables](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/compose/layouts/adaptive/foldables/learn-about-foldables)). The wide shape has a consequence for a phone-first diary that is easy to miss. The Duo's outer display and the Fold8's cover are not just wider than a normal phone; they are noticeably shorter. The Duo outer is an estimated ~678pt tall against 874pt on an iPhone 17 Pro, inferred from Apple's pixel counts at @3x. Vertical space for Today's stacked charts and for `fitToContents` form sheets therefore gets tighter on the cover screen, at the same time as horizontal space opens up on the inner screen.

The market is small but growing. Foldables were **1.5% of European smartphone sales in Q1 2025**, and the Motorola Razr was the top-selling model in Western Europe in H1 2025 ([Counterpoint](https://counterpointresearch.com/en/insights/europe-foldable-smartphone-market-is-flatlining-but-competition-continues-to-intensify); [Counterpoint](https://counterpointresearch.com/en/insights/motorola-google-change-conversation-in-western-europe-foldables-market)). Most Razr and Flip users run the app on an ordinary phone-sized main screen. A secondary aggregator attributes to Counterpoint a 2026 forecast that gives Apple 25% of global foldable shipments in its first year ([Axis Intelligence](https://axis-intelligence.com/foldable-phone-market-share/)). No EU-specific 2026 or Duo sales figure exists, so Eleva's foldable audience is a few percent at most. The Surface Duo is irrelevant: support ended in October 2024 ([heise](https://heise.de/-9974707)). The same reflow work also covers tablets, Android desktop windowing and split-screen, and those are what the platform rules below actually police.

## Android already ignores the portrait lock; iOS follows with the 27 SDK

### Android: today's build already stretches on every unfolded Fold

For apps targeting API 36, "orientation, resizability, and aspect ratio restrictions no longer apply on displays with smallest width >= 600dp. Apps fill the entire display window … and pillarboxing isn't used." `screenOrientation="portrait"`, `resizeableActivity` and the aspect-ratio attributes are all ignored there ([Android 16 behavior changes](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/16/behavior-changes-16)). Eleva's `AndroidManifest.xml` sets `screenOrientation="portrait"` and does not declare the temporary `PROPERTY_COMPAT_ALLOW_RESTRICTED_RESIZABILITY` opt-out. So **on a Fold or Pixel Fold inner screen the app already rotates freely and resizes in split-screen today**. On cover screens and normal phones (below sw600dp) the portrait lock still holds. In a single session, then, the app moves between a portrait-locked compact layout and a free-rotating, resizable one. The opt-out buys almost nothing. It "won't apply when targeting API level 37", and **Google Play requires API 37 for new apps and updates from August 2027** ([Android Developers Blog, Feb 2026](https://developer.android.com/blog/posts/prepare-your-app-for-the-resizability-and-orientation-changes-in-android-17)). Letterboxing also fails Play's baseline quality tier (next section). The only category exemption is games, which a bladder diary must not claim.

The good news is about continuity. Eleva's MainActivity already handles `orientation|screenSize|screenLayout|smallestScreenSize` in `configChanges`. Expo added `smallestScreenSize` to its template (PR #42150, merged January 2026) because fold and unfold on a Galaxy Fold 6 was recreating the activity, wiping state and triggering an expo-router "linking configured in multiple places" error ([expo/expo PR #42150](https://github.com/expo/expo/pull/42150)). Folding Eleva therefore delivers a dimension change to the live React tree instead of a restart, so typed notes, the chosen time and navigation state survive by default. What remains is width-dependent layout state. That includes the onboarding pager, which uses `pageWidth = windowWidth`; the `calendar-header` width math; sheet detents measured from content; and the hard-coded 80dp tab-bar height. It also includes an unresolved React Native report. **`useWindowDimensions` failed to update on fold/unfold on Android 15** (RN 0.79), and the issue went stale without a fix ([react-native#52952](https://github.com/react/react-native/issues/52952)). A matching Expo report was closed in September 2026 without resolution ([expo/expo#42294](https://github.com/expo/expo/issues/42294)).

### iOS: three behaviours, chosen by the SDK Eleva builds with

On the iPhone Duo the app's behaviour depends on the SDK it is built with, not on the device. Eleva is built today with the iOS 26 SDK, so it runs in "full legacy mode": "The window mimics existing iPhone sizes, with black surrounding it." Early Duo owners have already complained about unoptimised apps "suffer[ing] from letterboxing" ([The Mac Observer](https://www.macobserver.com/news/new-iphone-duo-owners-slam-the-app-situation-as-unoptimised-apps-suffer-from-letterboxing/)). Built with the iOS 27.0 SDK, the window extends further but stops 240px short of the edge, with orientation locked to project settings. Built with the **iOS 27.1 SDK, the app becomes a full-screen resizable scene**. On the inner display it behaves "like iPad apps. They can rotate freely and run side by side", and there is "no way to opt out of side by side functionality" ([Unity Discussions](https://discussions.unity.com/t/info-ipados-27-sdk-27-adaptability-changes-iphone-duo-updates/1737980); [AppleMagazine](https://applemagazine.com/iphone-duo-app-compatibility/)). Eleva's old escape hatch is gone too. "Starting in iOS 27 and iPadOS 27, `UIRequiresFullScreen` no longer opts your app out of resizing", and a launch screen becomes mandatory for submission ([Apple TN3192](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3192-migrating-your-app-from-the-deprecated-uirequiresfullscreen-key)). Builds against the latest SDK must adopt the UIScene life cycle or "your app won't launch" ([Apple Developer Forums 812487](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/812487)). Eleva's iOS project currently has only an AppDelegate.

Expo has already absorbed most of this. **Expo SDK 58 shipped on 15 October 2026 with React Native 0.88.** It "makes iPhone apps resizable", notes that `requireFullScreen` no longer opts out and that `ScreenOrientation.lockAsync` "may have no effect while the app is resizable", and stops reading geometry from `UIScreen.main` ([Expo SDK 58 changelog](https://expo.dev/changelog/sdk-58)). Expo's Duo guide says apps "can lock to portrait or landscape if they fill the screen as the pose changes" ([Expo blog: Supporting iPhone Duo](https://expo.dev/blog/supporting-iphone-duo)). That echoes Apple's own wording: "be sure to fill the screen as the device pose changes … Prefer changing the aspect ratio over letterboxing" ([HIG: Designing for iPhone Duo](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-iphone-duo)). The notes did not confirm whether SDK 58's template adopts UIScene for Eleva automatically. Check that during the upgrade.

The deadline picture is less firm than on Android. Apple's posted requirement remains Xcode 26 / iOS 26 SDK since 28 April 2026 ([Apple Upcoming Requirements](https://developer.apple.com/news/upcoming-requirements/?id=04282026a)). Unity expects an SDK 27 requirement around late April 2027, which follows Apple's annual pattern but is not announced. The one dated rule is App Store Connect's: "Starting in April 2027, screenshots will be required for any app using the iOS 27.1 SDK or later" ([Screenshot specifications](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/screenshot-specifications)). Eleva can therefore stay letterboxed on the Duo until spring 2027. The first Xcode 27 build will make it a resizable, rotating, side-by-side app on the inner display all at once, and the layouts have to be ready before that build.

| When | Android | iOS / iPhone Duo |
|---|---|---|
| Now (targetSdk 36, Xcode 26.5) | Portrait lock ignored on sw ≥ 600dp: the phone layout stretches on Fold inner screens and tablets | Letterboxed iPhone-sized window on the Duo inner display; iPhone app in a resizable window on iPadOS 26 |
| Upgrade to Expo SDK 58 / Xcode 27.1 | — | Resizable, rotating, side-by-side on the Duo inner display; UIScene life cycle required; Duo screenshots required (from April 2027) |
| Late April 2027 (expected, not announced) | — | iOS 27 SDK likely mandatory for uploads |
| August 2027 | API 37 mandatory; the opt-out stops working | — |

## Stores now grade layout quality, not just whether the app runs

Google Play replaced its large-screen guidelines with the **Adaptive app quality guidelines**. The old version was archived on 21 January 2026 and Tier 2 was revised on 7 April 2026. The guidelines define three tiers ([Adaptive app quality](https://developer.android.com/docs/quality-guidelines/large-screen-app-quality)). **Tier 3, "Adaptive ready", is the baseline.** The app is "not letterboxed; it does not run in compatibility mode". It must keep scroll position, typed text and keyboard state across rotation, fold/unfold and resizing, survive chained changes such as rotate-then-fold, and work in multi-window ([Tier 3](https://developer.android.com/docs/quality-guidelines/adaptive-app-quality/tier-3)). **Tier 2, "Adaptive optimized", is the bar for the "Optimized for large screens" Play badge** that Google launched in May 2026. Its checklist reads like a defect list for Eleva's current build ([Tier 2](https://developer.android.com/docs/quality-guidelines/adaptive-app-quality/tier-2)):
- "Bottom sheets are not full width on large screens."
- Buttons and text fields do not stretch.
- Small modals do not cover the whole screen.
- "Navigation rails replace navigation bars."
- Touch targets are at least 48dp.
- Esc closes modals, Tab moves through the main flows, and custom drawables show focus states.

Google says large-screen users spend about 5× more than phone-only users and that badged apps are promoted ([Android Developers Blog, May 2026](https://developer.android.com/blog/posts/adaptive-development-for-the-expanding-android-ecosystem)). Since 2022 Play has also ranked large-screen quality in search, split ratings by device type, and warned users about apps that fail basic compatibility ([Android Developers Blog, 2022](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2022/03/helping-users-discover-quality-apps-on.html)). Eleva's badge application would go through a Google form. The store listing should then add unfolded 1:1 and 4:3 screenshots and folded 10:16 and 9:16 ones. Tier 1 ("differentiated": postures, drag and drop, stylus) does not fit a diary.

Apple's equivalent is the screenshot rule plus a badge. iPhone Duo screenshots are **1398 × 2034 for the outer display and 2007 × 2853 for the inner**, landscape variants accepted. Submitting them puts a "made for iPhone Duo" badge on the product page ([Screenshot specifications](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/screenshot-specifications)). The 2007 × 2853 inner size does not match the 1878 × 2670 panel, so verify the rendering scale in Device Hub before producing assets. With three locales (en-GB, pt-PT, es-ES), the Duo adds two screenshot sets × 3. Turning on `supportsTablet` would add a 13" iPad set × 3 on top. Review Guideline 2.4.1 still says only that iPhone apps "should run on iPad whenever possible" ([App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Eleva already reaches iPads as an iPhone app. Under iPadOS 26 such apps sit in a resizable window but keep the compact size class ([Fatbobman](https://fatbobman.com/en/posts/from-size-class-to-available-space/)), so the iPad build should be smoke-tested but needs no redesign of its own. Once the width-driven layout exists for the Duo, enabling `supportsTablet` costs little more than the screenshots.

## Both platforms draw the lines at 600 and 840

Apple and Google now give the same core instruction: lay out by available window space, never by device or orientation. Apple's Layout page says to "Determine layout based on size classes, not device type or orientation", and adds that even an orientation-locked app must "resize well" ([HIG: Layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout)). Android says window size classes "are not intended for isTablet‑type logic" and can change during a session ([Use window size classes](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/compose/layouts/adaptive/use-window-size-classes)). Apple publishes no point thresholds. Its size classes are coarse, and an iPhone-idiom app made wider can stay `compact` ([Fatbobman](https://fatbobman.com/en/posts/from-size-class-to-available-space/)). The practical answer for a React Native app is to use Android's dp thresholds on both platforms, because RN reports width in dp on Android and pt on iOS. Samsung uses the same lines: "Use a large screen layout for screens with a width of 600dp or more" ([Samsung One UI](https://developer.samsung.com/one-ui/largescreen-and-foldable/large_screen_layout.html)).

| Width class | Range | On these devices | Eleva layout |
|---|---|---|---|
| Compact | < 600 | phones, all cover screens, Flip FlexWindow, Duo side-by-side (~469pt) | today's design, unchanged |
| Medium | 600–839 | Fold/Pixel Fold inner (portrait and often landscape), Duo inner portrait (est.) | rail; 2-column Today; capped forms |
| Expanded | 840–1199 | 4:3 inner in landscape, Duo inner landscape (est.), tablets landscape | rail; Today + side pane; History list-detail |
| Large / XL | ≥ 1200 / ≥ 1600 | desktop windowing, external displays | cap content width; no new design needed |

Source for the ranges: [Use window size classes](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/compose/layouts/adaptive/use-window-size-classes). Height classes are compact < 480, medium 480–899 and expanded ≥ 900. Google warns that two panes are impractical when height is compact. Material's May 2026 update renamed size classes "breakpoints", and m3.material.io itself could not be fetched, so the exact Material margin figures used below come from secondary summaries. Those figures are 16dp margins at compact and 24dp from medium up, a 360dp fixed pane at expanded and a 400dp side-sheet maximum.

Google's three canonical layouts map directly onto Eleva's screens ([Canonical layouts](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/compose/layouts/adaptive/canonical-layouts)):
- **List-detail** shows both panes at expanded width and one at a time below it. When the window narrows, the detail stays visible; when it widens from a detail, the item is highlighted in the list. That fits History.
- **Supporting pane** gives the main content about two thirds. It splits 50/50 at medium and 70/30 at expanded, and the side content drops below at compact. That fits Today at expanded width.
- **Feed** derives the column count from a minimum card width. That fits Today's cards.

Navigation switches from a bottom bar to a leading-edge **navigation rail**. Compose's `NavigationSuiteScaffold` uses the rail whenever neither width nor height is compact, and Tier 2 requires it on large screens ([Build adaptive navigation](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/compose/layouts/adaptive/build-adaptive-navigation)). One community summary of m3.material.io places the rail only from expanded, so 600 versus 840 is a judgement call. Sheets get a maximum width: Compose and MDC default to **640dp** ([Compose SheetMaxWidth](https://kotlinlang.org/api/compose-multiplatform/material3/androidx.compose.material3/-bottom-sheet-defaults/-sheet-max-width.html)). Sheets that only hold actions can become menus. Samsung adds fold-specific ratios (reported in a search snippet, not the fetched page): 50:50 on the Z Fold, 42:58 in medium windows and 38:62 in wider ones. It also asks for small pop-ups instead of full-screen views for brief tasks ([Samsung: designing for foldable](https://developer.samsung.com/one-ui/largescreen-and-foldable/designing_for_foldable.html)).

Apple's Duo page adds rules that Android's do not have. Use "a compact width layout for the outer display and a regular width layout for the inner display". "Don't reinvent your app when it resizes". Show "an additional level of hierarchy on the larger inner display", with Mail's list-plus-message as the example. Prefer "an even number of columns so content divides cleanly" at the fold. "Avoid extreme layout changes as people fold the device" ([HIG: Designing for iPhone Duo](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-iphone-duo)). On the Duo, system toolbars and tab bars move into a vertical side rail in most poses. The exception is the inner display in portrait. System alerts, menus and sheets move clear of the fold automatically. Neither applies to Eleva's hand-built JS tab bar, and Expo confirms that custom JS tab lists in the vertical rail are "not supported" ([Expo blog: Supporting iPhone Duo](https://expo.dev/blog/supporting-iphone-duo)). Eleva has to draw its own rail.

## Health apps set a low bar, so a modest redesign leads

Few health apps have been documented with a real large-screen design. The one well-reported case is Fitbit, now Google Health. Before November 2024 it was "just a stretched-out phone app", and in 2023 it was letterboxed on the Pixel Fold. Its fix was modest: four stat rings side by side and a dual-column card layout so no card "spans the full screen width" in landscape, with no rail ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2024/11/19/fitbit-foldable-tablet-redesign/)). Apple Health on iPad uses a sidebar beside a richer card-and-chart area ([BGR](https://bgr.com/tech/hands-on-with-the-all-new-ipados-17-health-app/)). No large-screen layout was verified for Samsung Health, Flo, Clue, Withings, MyFitnessPal or Oura. In November 2026 Android Authority's "worst apps on foldables" list was all "gigantic borders" and "a ton of white space", and it named no health app ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/worst-android-apps-foldables-3729614/)). A diary with a rail, a reflowing Today and a list-detail History would therefore be ahead of the category's best-documented example.

The redesign below treats today's phone design as the compact layout and keeps it. The Mobbin references were gathered from iPhone and web screens, because Mobbin has no foldable or iPad screens. The wide ones are web apps, used here for structure only and not for visual style.

**Navigation.** Keep the compact bar with its centre +, the pattern of the [Bevel home floating tab bar](https://mobbin.com/screens/2d3e8d5f-f3f1-4370-8b34-f914c8eb31ed). From 600 wide, render the same custom `tabBar` as a vertical rail on the leading edge. Put the + as a FAB at the top of the rail, the position M3-based guidance gives it ([SAP Fiori for Android: Navigation rail](https://www.sap.com/design-system/fiori-design-android/v25-8/components/m3-standard-components/navigation-rail/usage)), and the Today and History items below it. On a wide screen a bottom-centre + is hard to reach one-handed. The rail puts it under the thumb of a hand holding the side of the device, which follows Samsung's grip guidance.

**Today.** At compact, keep the stacked cards: a card per measure with a sparkline, as in [Withings Health Mate home](https://mobbin.com/screens/57e69e10-f0a7-4651-a187-c06b6f52f949), and stacked chart cards, as in [Noom](https://mobbin.com/screens/446f528a-16b4-4961-9a83-617bed75679f). At medium, switch to a two-column grid, an even count as Apple asks. Put the three stat cards (fluids, voids, leaks) in one row, as Fitbit did with its rings, and pair the weekly or monthly chart with the fluid-balance chart. The [Bevel two-column stat grid](https://mobbin.com/screens/5addacbb-f7a4-4548-aac5-25863dae8bd9) shows the density this produces. At expanded, use a 70/30 supporting pane. Cards and charts take the main area, and the day's timeline, grouped by part of day, sits in a side pane. The structure follows the [Perplexity Health dashboard](https://mobbin.com/screens/5e42f20d-0ce8-4423-bda5-a4235d18c5e6) (metric row plus side pane) and the [Copilot Money two-column card dashboard](https://mobbin.com/screens/1bfa784d-99eb-4850-88e2-4064d4d06452). Charts currently size from their parent, so put them in grid cells with a maximum width rather than letting them span 900dp. Extra width is a good reason to show the month view by default, with the D/W/M segmented control and target line of [Google Health (Fitbit)](https://mobbin.com/screens/87da4565-88e3-4345-9001-744c9dafab89).

**History.** At compact, keep the calendar, filter chips, entry list and the entry detail modal. At expanded, show list-detail. The left pane holds the month calendar and the selected day's entries. The right pane shows the entry detail and edit form inline instead of the `(modals)/entry/[id]` modal. Selecting a calendar day updates the list without leaving the screen. Four references support this structure: [Rise](https://mobbin.com/screens/6da90a11-a3da-44bc-858f-cb5a36e9bf72) (small month calendar beside the day and a detail pane), the [Upwork work diary](https://mobbin.com/screens/b2768de7-eab0-49c5-a802-070901010baa) (month calendar with a day-detail panel), [Calendly](https://mobbin.com/screens/3b379998-72b4-4313-b418-ee0cb610baac) (day-grouped list with a detail pane) and the [Perplexity Health history](https://mobbin.com/screens/20122940-8ac0-4b93-980a-b4ac799c9b5b) (list with a detail pane). At medium (600–839) the platforms disagree. Android shows one pane, while Samsung recommends 50:50 on the Fold. Choose by pane width at the current font scale rather than by raw window width. Split only when each pane keeps at least about 320dp of usable width; otherwise fall back to one pane. At 200% text, ~300dp panes break (this rule is my judgement, not platform text). Follow Android's state rules on fold and unfold: keep the open entry visible when narrowing, and highlight it in the list when widening.

**Entry forms and sheets.** On wide windows the add-urination, add-fluid and add-leak modals should become a centred card about 560–640 wide that scrolls and keeps Save reachable. Google's Android 17 guidance specifically warns about bottom-anchored Save buttons pushed off-screen in landscape. [Xero's centred entry modal](https://mobbin.com/screens/2eec6581-658a-4b97-836a-35def27c63ea) shows the shape and adds two ideas worth taking on any width: quick increments (a natural fit for fluid volume) and "Save & add another". At medium and up the date and time fields can sit side by side, as in [Workable's add-entry modal](https://mobbin.com/screens/2d6b1dd4-336d-4deb-845a-d42f2caf059b). The form sheets are add-menu, export, goals, language and reminder-interval. iOS centres `formSheet` natively on large windows ([React Native Modal docs](https://reactnative.dev/docs/modal)). The notes found no source on how react-native-screens sizes `formSheet` on large Android windows, so cap the content at 640 and centre it rather than relying on the default. On the rail, the add-menu sheet can become a menu anchored to the + FAB.

**Settings.** Settings is a stack screen with a short list, so cap it at a readable column of about 600 (roughly 60–70 characters per line). Do that first and stop there unless it grows. A sidebar-plus-section layout, as in [Buffer](https://mobbin.com/screens/0398e56b-5120-4055-9348-2b046ded2e97), or a section list in a modal, as in [Height](https://mobbin.com/screens/75db6320-fc80-4601-9e4e-f10cf45298f6), is the expanded-width option if Settings later gains more sections.

**Cover screens.** The outer screens are compact, so they get today's layout. The work there is height and continuity: start an entry on the cover screen, unfold, and the same form with the same values should appear. No health app was found with a one-tap logging widget on a Flip's FlexWindow, so a "+ void / + 250 ml" widget would be a differentiator, not a convention.

## Expo SDK 58 decides how much of this is native

The cheapest portable foundation is a single `useWindowClass()` hook that reads `useWindowDimensions()` at render time and returns compact, medium or expanded at 600 and 840. Nothing should cache dimensions in module scope or Zustand. To guard against the Android 15 stale-dimensions bug, cross-check against `useSafeAreaFrame()` or a root `onLayout`. That fallback is what the RN issue's reporters used, and a Galaxy Fold 5 report also shows the window height including the status bar only while folded ([react-native#41918](https://github.com/react/react-native/issues/41918)). NativeWind v5's `sm:`–`2xl:` variants do work on native through window-width media queries and update without re-rendering. But v5 is still labelled "not intended for production use", and its defaults are web breakpoints (640/768/1024) ([NativeWind v5: Responsive design](https://www.nativewind.dev/v5/core-concepts/responsive-design)). Eleva is mostly StyleSheet code anyway. So the hook should carry the logic, and `medium:`/`expanded:` variants should be defined through `@theme --breakpoint-*` only if they are verified to work on 5.0.0-rc.1.

Several native options turn out to be poor fits:
- **`expo-router/unstable-split-view`** is "alpha … iOS only" and falls back to a plain `Slot` on Android, so Android foldables get nothing ([Expo docs v57: Split view](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v57.0.0/sdk/router/split-view.md)). It is still experimental in SDK 58, and its API is being renamed upstream. History's two panes should be built in JavaScript inside the screen.
- **Native tabs**, stable in SDK 58 with `preferredPlacement="sidebar"` on iOS 27, would give Eleva the system Duo rail and iPad sidebar for free. But they cannot host the custom centre + the same way, and no Expo documentation says they become a Material rail on Android ([Expo docs: Native tabs](https://docs.expo.dev/router/advanced/native-tabs/)). A JS rail keeps one design on both platforms and is the better trade for now.
- **`expo-hinge`** (SDK 58+) reports hinge angle and status but no fold bounds ([expo-hinge docs](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/latest/sdk/hinge/)). Expo passes on Apple's advice that hinge state is for "interactions and effects … not for layout". Community libraries that expose `FoldingFeature` bounds exist but are young and unvetted.

A diary needs none of the posture APIs. Book foldables report a flat, non-separating fold, so a centred two-pane gutter is enough. Tabletop mode adds little to a logging app.

SDK 58 is still worth adopting before Xcode 27 becomes mandatory, for four reasons. It carries the iOS 27 resizable-app handling. It provides `SceneGeometry.anchorPopover` for regular-width popovers. It provides `react-native-reserved-regions` and `ArrangementView` if a fold-aware split is ever wanted. And its changelog warns that apps "may need to add `<SafeAreaView>` in more places, such as inside scrollviews", because the Duo's vertical rail adds an 84pt horizontal inset ([Expo SDK 58 changelog](https://expo.dev/changelog/sdk-58); [Expo blog: Supporting iPhone Duo](https://expo.dev/blog/supporting-iphone-duo)). Eleva's absolutely positioned centre + and its horizontal chart rows are exactly the elements that guide tells you to audit.

## Testing needs fold events, not just big emulators

On Android, test with these emulator profiles:
- **Foldable (4:3 inner display)**, **Foldable (1:1 inner display)**, **Pixel Fold** and **Medium Tablet**, the profiles Play's checklist names.
- The Resizable emulator, for sweeping through 600 and 840.

Drive them from the shell. `adb emu fold`, `adb emu unfold`, `adb emu rotate`, `adb emu posture 2` (tabletop) and `adb emu resize-display` are documented in Google's August 2026 emulator post ([Android Developers Blog, Aug 2026](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/08/emulator-adaptive.html)). `adb shell am compat enable UNIVERSAL_RESIZABLE_BY_DEFAULT care.eleva.peediary` forces the large-screen behaviour on any build ([Android 16 behavior changes](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/16/behavior-changes-16)). Run the matrix on API 35 and API 36, because the open dimensions bug is specific to Android 15.

The core script is Google's Tier 3 state test: on each screen, scroll and type, then rotate, fold, unfold and resize, and confirm nothing is lost ([Tier 3](https://developer.android.com/docs/quality-guidelines/adaptive-app-quality/tier-3)). For Eleva specifically:
1. Start folded and open add-fluid.
2. Type a note with the keyboard up, then unfold and rotate.
3. Check that the note, the time and the open `fitToContents` sheet survive and re-measure.

Real Galaxy hardware is available free through Samsung Remote Test Lab ([Samsung](https://developer.samsung.com/support)). On iOS, the Duo simulator lives in Xcode 27.1's Device Hub, with fold, pose and side-by-side controls, and on EAS through the `sdk-58` image ([Expo blog: Supporting iPhone Duo](https://expo.dev/blog/supporting-iphone-duo)). Today's iPhone build should also get a quick pass in iPadOS 26 Windowed Apps mode. Maestro flows in `e2e/` can run against a foldable AVD, with fold and unfold triggered out-of-band through `adb emu` (no Maestro fold command was found). Jest snapshots of key screens can mock `useWindowClass` at 411, 673 and 900 to catch layout-branch regressions cheaply.

Impact combines user benefit with store and deadline exposure. Effort is a rough engineering estimate for this codebase (S ≈ days, M ≈ 1–2 weeks, L ≈ several weeks), not a sourced figure.

Do not add the Android restricted-resizability opt-out, except as a stopgap for a blocker found in testing. It reintroduces letterboxing, which fails Tier 3, and it stops working at API 37. Leave posture and hinge APIs, native SplitView and a NativeTabs migration until there is a concrete reason for them. Keep `orientation: "portrait"` for phones and cover screens for now, since Android honours it below sw600dp and iOS honours it on the Duo's outer display. But design every medium and expanded layout for both orientations, because neither platform honours the lock on the inner screen.

## Conclusion

The question has shifted from "should Eleva support foldables?" to "which version of its layout will the platform show?". Android already chose for Eleva: the phone design stretches on every unfolded Fold. iOS will choose at the first Xcode 27 build. The orientation lock, the setting that made a portrait-only diary cheap to build, has become a preference the operating systems override on exactly the screens where layout matters most. That makes adaptive layout a maintenance requirement on the same footing as the target-SDK bump, not a feature.

The opportunity is that the category's bar is low and Eleva's structure fits the platforms' patterns well. It has a dashboard of equivalent cards, a calendar-driven history that is a natural list-detail, short entry tasks that suit small centred modals, and only two tabs plus one primary action that fit a rail. One width hook and four screen changes move it from "stretched phone app" to a layout that would rank above Google's own Fitbit redesign. The same work also makes the iPad build, the Play badge and the Duo badge cheap. Do the S-sized items (1–3) before the next Android release, because they fix what Fold users see today. The SDK 58 upgrade (item 8) has to land before Apple's expected spring 2027 requirement.

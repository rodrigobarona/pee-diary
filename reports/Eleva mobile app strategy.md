# Diary discovery pulls against one-app precedent

No published measurement exists for the number that decides this question: the share of users of a free health tracker who go on to install, or pay inside, the same publisher's separate care app. Everything else is adjacent evidence, and it splits by funnel stage. Precedent, cost and conversion mechanics favour one consumer app: of 19 documented satellite-app cases, 16 were shut or folded back, digital-health companies have mostly consolidated, and every extra install loses roughly a quarter to a half of the users who tap. Acquisition, privacy and regulatory isolation favour a standalone diary: its store title matches the query exactly, clinicians outside Eleva can recommend it without endorsing a marketplace, only a strictly local build can carry Apple's "Data Not Collected" label, and its review and medical-device boundary stay separate from payments and video. Two findings weaken the binary framing. Since July 2025 one app can show a diary-specific store page for diary keywords, and Apple's own rules require a no-login path where accounts are not essential, so a guest diary inside one app is allowed. And if the diary is built as a shared module on each platform, moving between structures later is mostly packaging, store and sign-on work, not a rewrite. The bladder-diary niche itself is small (the category leader has 50K+ Android installs after about a decade) and clinician-triggered, so distribution through clinicians and the web matters more than store search under any structure. The one split that precedent clearly supports is member versus expert, not diary versus care.

## Sixteen satellite apps folded back, three survived

The 2014-2016 "app constellation" wave is the best-documented test of splitting one consumer job into its own app, and its base rate is poor. Dropbox closed Mailbox and Carousel in 2016 and folded Carousel's features into the main app, with press paraphrasing the company as saying customers preferred one app ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2015/12/07/dropbox-is-killing-its-carousel-and-mailbox-apps/)). Facebook shut Creative Labs and pulled Slingshot, Riff and Rooms ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2015/12/07/facebook-experimental-app-division-shut-down/)). Instagram removed Boomerang, Hyperlapse and IGTV "to better focus our efforts on the main app" ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2022/03/07/instagram-discontinues-its-standalone-boomerang-and-hyperlapse-apps/)). LinkedIn reintegrated Groups because a standalone app could not use the wider ecosystem, then retired Job Search ([The Letter Two](https://thelettertwo.com/2018/01/15/linkedin-to-place-larger-focus-on-groups-shutters-standalone-app/); [HCM Technology Report](https://www.hcmtechnologyreport.com/linkedin-will-fold-job-search-into-core-app/)). Google merged Duo into Meet and is collapsing Fitbit and Google Fit into one Google Health app in 2026 ([Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/news/google-just-merged-google-meet-and-duo-what-it-means-for-you); [Google blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/google-health/google-health-app/)). Duolingo moved its standalone Math app into the flagship in 2023 ([Duolingo investor relations](https://investors.duolingo.com/news-releases/news-release-details/duolingo-launches-music-and-math-its-flagship-app)). The survivors (Messenger, Uber Eats, Swarm) were daily-use jobs backed by huge distribution: Messenger drew about **95% one-star iOS reviews** in the month after the forced split, yet reached 500 million monthly users within months ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2014/08/15/facebook-slammed-for-poor-messenger-app-research-shows/); [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/facebook-reveals-500-million-people-now-using-messenger-month)), and Foursquare eventually shut its original app to focus on the spun-out Swarm ([Foursquare](https://foursquare.com/city-guide-sunset/)). Uber's value came from linking its two apps: cross-platform consumers spend about 3x more and retain about 35% better ([Uber Q3 2025 transcript](https://s23.q4cdn.com/407969754/files/doc_financials/2025/q3/Uber-Q3-25-Earnings-Call-Transcript.pdf)).

Digital health follows the same direction. Headspace made its app the "one front door" for meditation plus Ginger's coaching and therapy from 2023, and its help centre still describes work "to merge all services into a single, streamlined app" years later, which shows how slowly a two-app start unwinds ([Behavioral Health Business](https://bhbusiness.com/2022/11/18/a-year-after-3b-mega-merger-headspace-and-ginger-roll-out-unified-behavioral-health-offering/); [Headspace help centre](https://help.headspace.com/hc/en-us/articles/19803162155547-What-is-Headspace-Care)). Teladoc unified primary care, mental health and chronic care in one app ([Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/health-tech/teladoc-unveils-new-app-integrating-services-primary-care-and-mental-health-and-expands)); WeightWatchers put its telehealth clinic inside its app ([Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/digital-health/weightwatchers-rolls-out-telehealth-clinic-prescribe-weight-loss-meds)); Hinge Health delivers pelvic care through the member's existing app ([Hinge Health](https://hingehealth.com/resources/press-releases/womens-pelvic-health-program-launch)); Flo and Clue ship life stages as in-app modes ([Flo newsroom](https://flo.health/newsroom/flo-for-perimenopause-is-launching-to-empower-the-1-billion-women-who-experience-perimenopause-without-the-support-they-deserve); [Clue support](https://support.helloclue.com/hc/en-us/articles/16701057975581-What-are-modes)). Where two health apps persist, the line follows payer, access or audience brand, not a feature: Calm Health is invitation-only through payers but shares the Calm login ([Calm Health FAQ](https://support.calm.com/hc/en-us/articles/27054399791643-Calm-Health-FAQ)), and Hims and Hers split by audience ([Hims & Hers newsroom](https://news.hims.com/newsroom/hims-hers-doctor-led-ai-native-care-experience)). Western "super app" failure does not count against a single Eleva app, since Forrester's objections concern unrelated verticals and it recommends "a simple, light" app ([Forrester](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/the-super-app-window-has-closed/)).

There is counter-evidence. Benedict Evans notes single-purpose apps are "easy to understand but hard to find", while bundled features get buried unless a natural context surfaces them ([Benedict Evans](https://ben-evans.com/benedictevans/2014/8/1/app-unbundling-search-and-discovery)). In pelvic health, publishers do run a narrow utility beside a broader product: Squeezy has the clinician-linked Squeezy Connect ([App Store UK](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/id700740791)), and iUFlow's free diary fronts a hardware and clinician-portal business ([PRWire](https://prwire.com.au/pr/96667/kesem-health-announces-its-launch-of-iuflow-xl-a-bladder-monitoring-platform-with-extended-battery-life-xl)). Zuckerberg's own test for Messenger was frequency high enough that a tab was real friction ([Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.in/Mark-Zuckerberg-Reveals-Why-You-Were-Forced-To-Download-Facebooks-Separate-Messenger-App/articleshow/45073683.cms)); a bladder diary is intense for days, then dormant, which resembles the utilities that were folded back. Missing evidence: no documented case of a small health company running a free no-account tracker as a separate app to feed a care marketplace with reported conversion; Meta's newer standalone launches were not researched; no EU study compares satisfaction with bundled versus single-purpose health apps.

### The well-evidenced split is member versus expert

Wherever the provider is a professional running a practice, platforms ship a separate provider app: Doctolib Pro ([App Store FR](https://apps.apple.com/fr/app/doctolib-pro-agenda-praticien/id1107542306)), Doctoralia for specialists ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/doctoralia-for-specialists/id1237598188)), Portugal's knok ([App Store PT](https://apps.apple.com/pt/app/knok-exclusivo-para-m%C3%A9dicos/id1060653527?l=en-GB)), Teladoc ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/teladoc-health-provider/id1416079653)), Booksy, Fresha and Treatwell ([Booksy Biz Pro](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1509069011); [Fresha for business](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fresha-for-business/id1455346253); [Treatwell Pro](https://apps.apple.com/es/app/treatwell-pro-for-business/id1582612088?l=en-GB)). Single apps with a mode switch survive where both sides are peers, as at Airbnb ([Airbnb newsroom](https://news.airbnb.com/product-releases/airbnb-2025-summer-release/)). Fred Wilson's 2015 view was that combined apps do "not work that well", while conceding separate apps make the business "harder, more complicated, and more expensive" ([AVC](https://avc.com/2015/11/mobile-apps-for-both-sides-of-the-market/)). The closest analogue, Maven Clinic, runs a member app and a practitioner app, MPractice, which has **6 ratings, weighs 171.5 MB and ships roughly monthly**: the standing cost of a provider app for a small audience ([MPractice](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/mpractice/id942538862)). A thinner route exists: BetterHelp therapists message and schedule in the member app but, as of a 2021 help page, run video from a computer ([BetterHelp counselor help](https://counselor-onboarding.helpscoutdocs.com/article/403-can-i-use-my-phone-or-tablet-for-betterhelp)), and Kry/Livi's clinician tooling is web-based ([MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/emea/livi-connect-launched-across-europe-help-healthcare-professionals-amid-covid-19)). Sharetribe's 2026 guidance is web first, native for one side after validation, never both sides' apps at launch, because "every new feature must work on web and mobile and pass both app store reviews" ([Sharetribe Academy](https://www.sharetribe.com/academy/marketplace-mobile-app-why-when-how/)). On the member side, care apps embed trackers and programmes instead of splitting them: Hinge and Peppy do ([Hinge Health](https://www.hingehealth.com/how-it-works/experience); [Peppy](https://peppy.health/services/overview)), and Sword's listing places Bloom inside the Sword app ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/sword-health-ai-care/id1468523447)), although a separate low-install Bloom Android listing also exists and its status is unresolved ([AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/com.hibloom.android)). No source reports what share of users in an all-in-one health app use the secondary tracker. Member video belongs on the phone but need not be native at first: 74% of 210,383 Kaiser Permanente video visits ran on smartphones (2015-2017 data, [Ro](https://ro.co/research/smartphones-bridge-broadband-gap/)), and Portugal's SNS 24 lets patients join from app, portal or SMS link ([The Portugal News](https://www.theportugalnews.com/news/2024-12-19/sns-24-health-line-launches-new-video-consultations/94367)). One local constraint applies to every structure: Android held **70.76% of Portuguese mobile usage** in October 2026, so both platforms ship together ([Statcounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/os-market-share/mobile/portugal)).

## The diary niche counts installs in tens of thousands

The category is thin. iUFlow, the long-standing leader, shows 50K+ lifetime Android installs and 1.2K iOS ratings, is English-only, and was last updated on iOS in November 2024 ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id935581221); [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/search?q=bladder%20diary&c=apps&hl=en&gl=US)). The only actively developed multilingual rival, Bladderly, is localised into Portuguese and Spanish but sells a $4.99 "3-Day Pass" and $1.99 single PDF exports, with a 3.5 rating on Google Play ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1523268654); [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/search?q=Bladderly&c=apps&hl=en&gl=US)). Pelvic floor exercise apps are one to two orders of magnitude larger: Kegel Trainer has 1M+ Android installs ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/search?q=Kegel%20Trainer%20Olson&c=apps&hl=en&gl=US)). An ICS 2024 review of ten English-language diary apps scored them 7.6 out of 19 on average, found none with a second language, and concluded "most bladder tracking Apps are unsuited to clinical use" ([ICS 2024 abstract 14](https://www.ics.org/2024/abstract/14)). On Google Play Portugal, "diário miccional" returns Urinote, Bladderly and a hobby app, then generic journaling apps; only 3 of the first 12 results concern the bladder ([Google Play PT](https://play.google.com/store/search?q=di%C3%A1rio%20miccional&c=apps&hl=pt_PT&gl=PT)). Brazilian Portuguese is served by a pharma app last updated in March 2024 ([Minha Bexiga](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1537991229)). A free, account-free, European Portuguese diary with free clinician PDF export fills a real gap. The same padded results signal low query volume; no keyword-volume figure for any diary term in any storefront was obtained, and iOS search ordering could not be inspected.

Need is large but demand is prescribed. A representative Portuguese sample (n = 10,465) put self-reported urinary incontinence at **9.9% of adult women** ([World J Urol 2023](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s00345-023-04677-5)), and the EAU counts 55-60 million Europeans with continence problems ([EAU](https://uroweb.org/an-urge-to-act)). The 2026 EAU guideline gives a strong recommendation for a diary of at least 3 days at initial assessment and follow-up ([EAU guideline](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/non-neurogenic-female-luts/chapter/diagnosis)). Clinicians under-use it: only about a quarter of Turkish urologists and gynaecologists use questionnaires and diaries for overactive bladder ([UroToday](https://urotoday.com/recent-abstracts/pelvic-health-reconstruction/overactive-bladder/140009-clinical-preferences-and-treatment-attitudes-among-urologists-gynecologists-and-geriatricians-an-independent-online-questionnaire-survey-for-comparison-of-treatment-choices-in-the-management-of-overactive-bladder.html)), and 97.5% of US urogynaecology providers cited difficulty using them ([PubMed 36116064](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36116064/)). App diaries perform well once prescribed: in the Spanish eDM3d validation, 90.4% of 136 patients completed the first app diary and 88.2% would choose the app again ([ICS 2018 abstract 132](https://www.ics.org/2018/abstract/132)). The routes that demonstrably work in this space are clinician recommendation (Squeezy, built by NHS physiotherapists, is the top paid UK medical app: [App Store UK](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/id700740791)), hardware bundling and employer contracts; even the best-evidenced free app, Tät, got 3-month responses from only 11% of eligible downloaders ([Int Urogynecol J 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00192-021-04755-5)). Paper remains the default: Coloplast Spain's printable "diario miccional" carries links to its app ([Coloplast](https://prod-professional-es.coloplast.com/globalassets/hcp/pdf-file/v2/spain/cc/diariomiccional_460n0d2992.pdf)). No source quantifies how many patients are handed a diary each year in Portugal, Spain or the EU, and no repeat-episode or PDF-export rates are published.

## Every extra install loses a quarter to half of tappers

Store mechanics give a focused app a structural edge on its own keyword. Apple ranks on text relevance (title, subtitle, keywords, category) plus behaviour ([Apple Developer](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/search/)), and relevance between query and listing moves conversion: US brand-keyword Apple Ads campaigns convert at a median 73.5% tap-to-install against 59.8% for generic terms ([AppTweak](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/apple-ads-benchmarks)). That edge has narrowed. One app can now publish up to 70 custom product pages and assign keywords so a diary-specific page appears in search for those terms, with a deep link into the diary ([Apple Developer](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/custom-product-pages/)); vendors date the keyword feature to 30 July 2025 ([MobileAction](https://www.mobileaction.co/blog/apple-doubles-the-custom-product-page-limit/)). Google Play allows up to 50 custom listings, including by search keyword ([Google Play Console](https://play.google.com/console/about/storelistings/?hl=id)). On iOS the name, subtitle and icon stay fixed per app, so the result row still reads "Eleva Care". No controlled study compares a focused and a broad app on the same niche keyword.

The cross-install step is the measurable cost of two apps, and it has no public benchmark. Vendor web-to-app data puts completion at 40-60% of people who tap ([Linkrunner](https://linkrunner.io/blog/deep-linking-for-web-to-app-how-to-convert-mobile-web-visitors-into-app-users)); Foursquare said about one-third of users used both apps after its forced split ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2015-06-24-foursquare-swarm-split.html)); more than 10% of new Uber Eats users came through the rides channel ([Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/ubers-new-membership-bundles-rides-delivery-services/610240)). Even the brand-intent ad benchmark above leaves about a quarter of tappers uninstalled. The loss is avoidable under two apps if booking opens on the web with the diary attached, so the second app never gates the first purchase; that inference has no measured support either way.

Conversion evidence is adjacent only. Median download-to-paid for freemium apps is **2.18%** ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025/)); Flo reported about 5 million subscribers on nearly 70 million monthly users ([Flo newsroom](https://flo.health/newsroom/flo-health-raises-over-200m)). Tools that end in a clinical output hand off far more: 3.1 million of K Health's 10 million symptom-checker users chose a clinician chat ([Forbes Australia](https://www.forbes.com.au/news/innovation/this-ai-chatbot-has-helped-doctors-treat-3-million-people/)), and over 25% of Solv's symptom-checker users booked ([Infermedica](https://infermedica.com/case-studies/solv)). Both are US acute-need flows. Timing favours conversion inside the episode: median push opt-in is about half of users ([Airship](https://www.airship.com/mobile-app-push-notification-benchmarks-for-2026/)), median 30-day retention across 93 mental health apps was 3.3% ([JMIR 2019](https://www.jmir.org/2019/9/e14567)), and task reminders lifted diary adherence from 70% to 95% ([RTI](https://rti.org/publication/performance-acceptability-validation-phone-application-bowel-diary)). A no-account diary deleted after day 7 is unreachable; a single app keeps the user where booking lives.

The clinician loop is the strongest non-store argument for a neutral standalone tool. Among 158 Irish physiotherapists, 54.5% had prescribed apps, and the criteria rated "very important" were ease of use (96%), clear purpose (77%) and privacy (76%) ([World Physiotherapy](https://world.physio/congress-proceeding/exploring-use-mobile-apps-clinical-practice-survey-irish-physiotherapists)). Squeezy became a standard NHS recommendation as a clinician-authored, single-purpose app ([Bladder & Bowel Community](https://www.bladderandbowel.org/news/squeezy-the-nhs-physiotherapy-app-for-pelvic-floor-muscle-exercises/)). No study compares clinician recommendation of a neutral tool against a branded marketplace app, and there is no Portuguese or Spanish survey. Cost context cuts both ways: a median Apple Ads install costs about **$1.01 in Portugal** ([AppTweak](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/apple-ads-benchmarks)), so organic rank for a low-volume term is worth little in cash, and a single app can buy diary-intent traffic to a diary product page to measure real demand. A Portuguese web search for printable diaries returned academic PDFs and no consumer page in European Portuguese, an opening for an eleva.care page under any structure (observed once, from a US-based tool).

## A shared diary module makes the structure reversible

Better Auth ships no official Swift or Kotlin SDK; its only documented native integration is Expo, and the Swift option is a community package that supports only the latest Better Auth version ([Expo integration](https://www.better-auth.com/docs/integrations/expo); [BetterAuthSwift](https://github.com/ouwargui/BetterAuthSwift)). Two server-supported routes exist: REST plus the bearer plugin, which the docs say to use with caution ([Bearer plugin](https://www.better-auth.com/docs/plugins/bearer)), or the OAuth 2.1 provider plugin with Authorization Code and PKCE, rotating refresh tokens and revocation ([OAuth Provider](https://www.better-auth.com/docs/plugins/oauth-provider)) through ASWebAuthenticationSession and Chrome's Auth Tab ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/authenticationservices/aswebauthenticationsession); [Chrome](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/android/custom-tabs/guide-auth-tab)). Native Apple and Google sign-in work by ID token, with each bundle ID added as an accepted audience ([Better Auth Apple docs](https://www.better-auth.com/docs/authentication/apple)); guideline 4.8 makes an equivalent privacy-preserving login mandatory wherever Google login is offered on iOS ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Six stable Better Auth patches shipped between 1 and 10 November, several fixing session revocation ([releases](https://github.com/better-auth/better-auth/releases)). Unverified: whether the OAuth provider accepts custom-scheme redirects, how to skip consent for first-party clients, and whether the passkey plugin accepts Android app origins.

Single sign-on across two apps is cheap on iOS and awkward on Android. Apple documents that with a shared Keychain group "logging into one of your apps automatically grants the user access to all of your apps" ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/security/sharing-access-to-keychain-items-among-a-collection-of-apps)). Android's `sharedUserId` is deprecated; a signature-gated ContentProvider needs both apps on the same Play app-signing key ([Clerk](https://clerk.com/docs/android/reference/native-mobile/shared-session-sync)). One `eleva.care` passkey relying party can serve several apps through the association files ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/supporting-associated-domains); [Android](https://developer.android.com/identity/credential-manager/prerequisites)), and Better Auth's one-time-token plugin supports session hand-off ([docs](https://www.better-auth.com/docs/plugins/one-time-token)). Carrying context through an install needs a third-party vendor on iOS, which has no equivalent of Android's install referrer now that Firebase Dynamic Links has shut down ([Branch](https://www.branch.io/resources/blog/life-after-firebase-dynamic-links-three-paths-to-a-better-deep-linking-stack/)). One app has none of these problems.

The diary need not be written twice per platform. Apple documents local Swift packages shared across app targets ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/organizing-your-code-with-local-packages)), so a standalone shell and the member app can consume one diary module; the Android equivalent is standard practice but was not sourced. The no-account mode cannot use CloudKit, because apps "may not store personal health information in iCloud" ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)), and Better Auth's anonymous plugin leaves data migration to the application ([docs](https://www.better-auth.com/docs/plugins/anonymous)). Lightweight substitutes for a second listing do not exist: App Clips cannot use HealthKit, App Intents or background activity ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appclip/choosing-the-right-functionality-for-your-app-clip)), and Google ended Instant Apps in December 2025 ([Android](https://developer.android.com/topic/google-play-instant)); interactive widgets give one-tap logging only to people who already installed the app ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/widgetkit/adding-interactivity-to-widgets-and-live-activities)). Two apps mean four store binaries, listings and privacy declarations. A diary-only binary skips WebRTC, since Daily's native SDKs ship no prebuilt UI and the call screen is built per platform ([Daily iOS](https://docs.daily.co/reference/ios); [Daily Android](https://docs.daily.co/reference/android)); Google's data ties each 6 MB of APK to about 1% lower install conversion, though one developer measured no gain from cutting 42 MB ([Google Play](https://medium.com/googleplaydev/shrinking-apks-growing-installs-5d3fcba23ce2); [Medium](https://medium.com/@martinmazanec/shrinked-app-size-by-42mb-was-it-worth-it-ac34f76b14c7)). Messaging also stays out of the diary: a vendor such as Stream offers a Dublin region but lists subprocessors across regions ([Stream FAQ](https://getstream.io/security/faq/); [Stream subprocessors](https://getstream.io/legal/subprocessors/)), and push through FCM has no built-in end-to-end encryption, so notifications should carry no health content ([Firebase](https://firebase.google.com/docs/cloud-messaging/encryption)). No source quantifies the cost multiplier of two native codebases, the maintenance cost of two apps for a small team beyond vendor rules of thumb of 15-20% of build cost per year ([Koombea](https://www.koombea.com/blog/app-maintenance-cost/)), or how much coding agents shrink either.

## App scope moves the regulatory footprint, not the law

Under MDCG 2019-11 Rev.1 (June 2025), software that only stores, displays and communicates data is not medical device software, and "wellness or fitness apps" are excluded; software that interprets data for an individual is, and Rule 11 makes Class IIa the default, which requires a notified body ([MDCG 2019-11 Rev.1](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/b45335c5-1679-4c71-a91c-fc7a4d37f12b_en?filename=mdcg_2019_11_en.pdf)). Qualification is per module, so one app does not turn booking or video into a device. Rev.1 also says host functions an MDSW module depends on "should not be excluded from the MDSW description" and that "thresholds alerts may qualify as a medical device". INFARMED's circular of 29 July 2026 lists "aplicações que acompanham parâmetros de saúde" among device examples and booking and wellness apps among non-devices ([INFARMED](https://www.infarmed.pt/documents/15786/12277909/Solu%C3%A7%C3%B5es+digitais+em+sa%C3%BAde+enquanto+dispositivos+m%C3%A9dicos/287ef796-f7d5-ff11-1f48-403825ec9378)). If no interpretive feature is ever planned, MDR does not discriminate between structures; if flags or AI summaries arrive, a standalone diary confines the device boundary and the store's device label to one listing. No authority has ruled on bladder diaries, and the status of the Commission's December 2025 MDR reform proposal was not verified.

Portuguese telehealth duties attach to the care function identically in every structure. ERS requires telemedicine units to register before operating, and the registrant is whoever presents as responsible for care, "nomeadamente emitindo faturas ou recibos próprios" ([ERS manual 2025](https://www.ers.pt/media/2kskt534/manual-do-procedimento-de-registo-2025.pdf)); its 2024 alert requires informed consent, privacy and clinical-record entries for teleconsultations ([ERS Alerta 7/2024](https://ers.pt/media/kgahrh2u/alerta-supervisão07-2024-teleconsultas.pdf)). No ERS position on marketplaces and no Ordem dos Fisioterapeutas telehealth norm was found.

On privacy, the step-change is the moment data leaves the device. Recital 18 keeps providers of the means in scope where they are controllers ([GDPR Recital 18](https://gdpr-info.eu/recitals/no-18/)), CNPD's list requires a DPIA for devices transmitting health data over networks ([CNPD Regulamento 798/2018](https://omcentro.com/ficheiros/docs/r_798_2018.pdf)), and Norway's appeal court upheld a NOK 65 million fine on Grindr on the basis that app-use data is itself special-category ([Forbrukerrådet](https://www.forbrukerradet.no/news-in-english/grindr-loses-appeal/)). Apple's label rule mirrors this: "Data that is processed only on device is not 'collected'" ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/)). The consequence is narrower than it first looks: only a diary build with no sync and no off-device telemetry earns "Data Not Collected"; a diary with optional account sync must declare health data linked to the user, and an all-in-one app never qualifies. Independent reviewers reward the local-first shape: Privacy International's 2025 retest praised trackers that skip account creation ([Privacy International](https://privacyinternational.org/long-read/5593/all-eyes-my-period-period-tracking-apps-and-future-privacy-post-roe-world)), and secondary reports of Mozilla's 2026 testing rank a local, no-account tracker first ([CyberInsider](https://cyberinsider.com/mozilla-study-ranks-euki-as-the-most-private-period-tracker/)). No research covers bladder-diary users' privacy expectations, and the "not a controller" position for local-only use is unconfirmed by any regulator.

Store rules permit every structure. Guideline 5.1.1(v) says apps without significant account-based features must work without login, which supports a guest diary in one app; 4.3(a) warns against "multiple Bundle IDs of the same app", a low exposure if a standalone diary is duplicated inside the care app; and since 26 March 2026 health apps must declare regulated-medical-device status per listing ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/); [Apple Developer News](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=nyqbfz1y)). Paid 1:1 sessions may use Eleva's own payments under 3.1.3(d), while "one-to-few and one-to-many real-time services must use in-app purchase"; Google likewise exempts 1:1 services and says regulated clinical services should not use Play Billing ([Google Play](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10281818?hl=en)). Academy lectures are digital content: under Apple's EU terms effective 1 October 2026, in-app purchase costs 26% (15% reduced), alternative in-app payment 20% or 10%, and link-outs 15% or 10% on sales within 7 days ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/support/dma-and-apps-in-the-eu/)); these terms supersede the 2025 fee structure and remain contested by civil-society groups ([ARTICLE 19](https://www.article19.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Open-letter-Apples-new-EU-business-terms-FINAL.pdf)). Commission follows the transaction type, not the app, so adding lectures does not tax bookings; it adds a second payment regime to one review. Whether Apple's licence agreement confirms 1:1 sessions as non-commissionable in the EU was not read. EHDS wellness-app labelling applies only if interoperability with an EHR system is claimed, from 26 March 2029 ([EHDS Art. 47](https://www.ringholm.com/ehds/article-47.htm)).

## Conclusion

The evidence reframes the choice. App count matters less than three design decisions available under every structure: building the diary as a shared, local-first module with no telemetry; routing the first booking to the web with the diary attached, so no install sits between result and purchase; and distributing through clinicians and a printable web page, because store search for this term is thin. With those fixed, what remains between the structures is a trade of acquisition reach, privacy labelling and regulatory isolation against cost and funnel continuity, and the asymmetry of reversal: merging two apps later is slow, while splitting one later forces a second download.

The unknowns that decide it are cheap to measure and expensive to guess. Diary keyword volume in Portuguese and Spanish storefronts, install-to-booking conversion, and clinician willingness to recommend a marketplace-branded listing are all unpublished; a small Apple Ads test against a diary product page and a web diary page would produce the first two. Five questions need a qualified opinion before any structure is final: device status of planned indices and AI summaries, the ERS registrant in a marketplace model, controller roles with experts, Apple's EU commission treatment of 1:1 sessions, and how Apple treats iCloud device backups of local health data.

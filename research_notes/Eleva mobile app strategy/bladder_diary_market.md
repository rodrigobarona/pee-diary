## 1. Which bladder diary / voiding diary apps exist on iOS and Android, and how strong are they?

### Takeaway
The category is small and thin: the long-standing leader (iUFlow) has roughly 50K+ lifetime Android installs and 1.2K iOS ratings after about a decade, is English-only, and was last updated on iOS in November 2024. The only actively developed, multi-language competitor (Bladderly, localised into Portuguese and Spanish) has moved to subscriptions and paywalled PDF export, and has a 3.5 rating on Google Play. A free, no-account, PT/ES/EN diary with free PDF export is differentiated on paper, but the ceiling of the category is low.

**Independent quality reviews of the category**
- ICS 2024 framework analysis: 13 English-language bladder diary apps identified, 10 included (downloaded October 2023), scored with a modified APPLICATIONS system (0-19) by four reviewers. Mean score 7.6 (range 5.25-9.75). No app offered any language other than English; 90% were free; only 50% required a password; 70% could export data; 40% had custom reminders; 20% had health-professional oversight; 10% cited literature; none tracked bowel and bladder together. Conclusion: "most bladder tracking Apps are unsuited to clinical use"; recommended fixes are data security, multi-language support, medical content and data transfer — [ICS 2024 abstract 14](https://www.ics.org/2024/abstract/14)
- Brazil, 2020 (International Urogynecology Journal): search of iTunes and Google Play for voiding diary apps found 55 apps, 28 eligible; mean app scores ranged from 1.7 to 4.5 [snippet; full text not read] — [PubMed 32592019](https://www.pubmed.gov/32592019/), [Springer](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s00192-020-04382-6)
- Exeter systematic review of pelvic floor apps (ICS 2019): the one bladder-diary-plus-Kegel app included ("Healthy Bladder: Diary & Kegel") scored about 3.46 on MARS [snippet] — [University of Exeter repository](https://ore.exeter.ac.uk/repository/bitstream/10871/39443/3/ICS%20Abstract_APP_Review_2019.pdf)

### Inferences
- The incumbent pattern is "free diary as front end for something else" (iUFlow: hardware and clinician portal; Bladderly: subscription and sound-based measurement; Minha Bexiga: pharma patient support). No listed competitor pairs a diary with bookable human experts.
- Eleva Diary's planned attributes (free, no account, local-only storage, free PDF export, PT/ES/EN) map directly onto the weaknesses the ICS 2024 review named (no non-English languages, weak data security, poor data transfer) and onto Bladderly's paywall ($1.99 per PDF export, $4.99 3-day pass).
- Using Bladderly's own claim (63,000 diary days / 7,800 users) gives about 8 diary days per user: an estimate, from unverified marketing copy, but consistent with one or two 3-day episodes per user.
- Category size signal: the best-known app has 50K+ Android installs in about ten years of availability (estimate of timespan based on the App Store ID and 2020-2024 version history). This is a niche measured in tens of thousands of lifetime installs per app, not millions.
- Low ratings counts (42 to 1.2K on iOS) mean a new entrant can reach rating parity with modest volume; they also confirm low absolute traffic.

### Gaps
- Not verified (no listing found or not fetched): Vesica, My Bladder Diary, UroBladderDiary, Day2Day, BladderTrakHer, My IC Diary, Carin/LifeSense, Diary Pod, iP Voiding Diary. Several may have been delisted; this was not confirmed either way.
- Whether iUFlow and Bladderly strictly require an account could not be confirmed from store pages (both collect email/name per privacy labels).
- No Sensor Tower, Appfigures or AppTweak download estimates were accessible; AppBrain and AppGoblin blocked direct fetching (HTTP 403/401).
- iOS ratings on the Portuguese, Spanish and Brazilian storefronts were not retrieved (App Store returned HTTP 429).

## 2. Which pelvic floor / pelvic health apps exist, how do they acquire users, which include a bladder diary, and which combine a tracker with human experts?

### Takeaway
Pelvic floor exercise apps are a category one to two orders of magnitude larger than bladder diaries (Kegel Trainer 1M+ Android installs versus 50K+ for the top diary), and the successful ones acquire users through clinician recommendation (Squeezy, Tät), hardware sales (Perifit, Elvie, Emy), or employer/health-plan contracts (Sword Bloom, Hinge). Squeezy and URinControl include a bladder diary as a secondary feature; none of the consumer apps found combines a free diary with a marketplace of bookable licensed experts.

### Inferences
- Acquisition routes that demonstrably work in this space are (a) clinician prescription (Squeezy is the top paid UK medical app because NHS physiotherapists tell patients to buy it), (b) hardware bundling, and (c) B2B2C contracts. Pure organic store search is the weakest of the observed routes.
- The combination "bladder diary + pelvic floor programme + access to a human specialist" exists only inside closed, employer-funded US programmes (Hinge, Sword Bloom) and in clinician-linked tools (Squeezy Connect). An open, consumer-facing EU marketplace attached to a diary appears to be unoccupied.
- Squeezy and URinControl treat the diary as a feature inside a broader pelvic health app, which is precedent for the "diary inside the all-in-one Eleva app" option; iUFlow and Bladderly are precedent for the standalone option.

### Gaps
- No data found for Emy/Fizimed, Leva (Axena Health), Flyte (Pelvital), Easy Kegel install counts, or current Jude customer numbers and whether Jude now ships an app.
- No verified 2026 user or download totals for Tät, Elvie, or Bloom; Sword and Hinge numbers are company claims.
- Whether Sword Bloom or Hinge pelvic health are sold in Portugal or Spain was not established.

## 3. How large is the need (prevalence in Portugal, Spain, Brazil, EU), and what do guidelines say about diary duration?

### Takeaway
Prevalence is very large (roughly one in ten adult Portuguese women self-report urinary incontinence; one in five women over 40; 55-60 million Europeans have continence problems), and EAU and NICE both give the diary a strong, standing role: at least 3 days at initial assessment and again at follow-up. The number of people actually handed a diary is unknown and practice surveys suggest clinicians use diaries far less than guidelines recommend.

### Cited Findings

**Guidelines**
- EAU Non-neurogenic Female LUTS guideline (2026 edition): "Ask patients with LUTS to complete a bladder diary as part of the standardised initial assessment and follow-up" (Strong); "Use a bladder diary with a duration of ≥ 3 days" (Strong); "Bladder diaries of three to seven days' duration are reliable tools" (LE 2b); "sensitive to change and are a reliable outcome measure" (LE 2b); "The optimum number of days required for bladder diaries appears to be based on a balance between accuracy and compliance" [fetched 11 Nov 2026] — [EAU guideline, Diagnosis chapter](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/non-neurogenic-female-luts/chapter/diagnosis)
- EAU Male LUTS pocket guideline (2026 limited update): "Have the patient complete a bladder diary for at least three days" (Strong) [snippet] — [EAU pocket guideline](https://d56bochluxqnz.cloudfront.net/documents/pocket-guidelines/EAU-Pocket-on-Non-Neurogenic-Male-LUTS-2026.pdf)
- NICE Quality Standard QS77, statement 2: women first presenting with urinary incontinence are asked to complete a bladder diary for a minimum of 3 days, covering variations in usual activities such as working and leisure days [snippet; direct fetch blocked] — [NICE QS77](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/qs77/chapter/Quality-statement-2-Bladder-diaries-and-); NICE NG123 (2019, reviewed March 2025) covers management in women 18+ — [NICE NG123](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng123/)
- Portuguese-language clinical references repeat the 3-day minimum (GPnotebook PT: at least 3 days covering work and leisure days; a Hospital Fernando Fonseca training resource lists the 3-day diary as a complementary diagnostic exam); no dedicated Portuguese national norm (DGS) for pelvic physiotherapy was found [snippet] — [GPnotebook PT](https://gpnotebook.com/pt/pages/ginecologia/avaliacao-da-incontinencia-urinaria-na-mulher/investigacao), [HFF repository](https://repositorio.hff.min-saude.pt/bitstreams/ea37cf80-4c74-4a47-a4cc-2c52d9e96e8f/download)
- The Portuguese Urology Association hosts a Portuguese-language OAB patient decision aid dated June 2025 — [APU](https://apurologia.pt/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Portuguese%20OAB%20PDA%20iPDF%20FINAL_10.06.2025.pdf)

**Practice gap: how often clinicians actually use diaries**
- Dutch survey on nocturia evaluation: most doctors reported using a diary for 24 hours or up to 3 days despite the EAU recommendation — [EMJ Urology](https://www.emjreviews.com/urology/abstract/the-discrepancy-between-european-association-of-urology-eau-guidelines-and-daily-practice-in-the-evaluation-of-nocturia-results-of-a-dutch-survey)
- Turkish online survey: use of questionnaires and voiding diaries for OAB diagnosis was 23.9% among urologists and 25.1% among gynaecologists [snippet] — [UroToday](https://urotoday.com/recent-abstracts/pelvic-health-reconstruction/overactive-bladder/140009-clinical-preferences-and-treatment-attitudes-among-urologists-gynecologists-and-geriatricians-an-independent-online-questionnaire-survey-for-comparison-of-treatment-choices-in-the-management-of-overactive-bladder.html)
- US urogynaecology provider survey (fielded 2019; 371 of 851 responded): 20.8% used diaries less than 20% of the time in the past year; 97.5% cited difficulty in using bladder diaries [snippet] — [PubMed 36116064](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36116064/)
- ICI-RS 2023 review: completion depends on clear instructions, patient education, follow-up and reinforcement [snippet] — [UWE repository](https://uwe-repository.worktribe.com/output/11546911/can-we-increase-the-value-of-data-from-bladder-diaries-international-consultation-on-incontinence-research-society-2023)

**Portugal**
- Representative sample of Portuguese women aged 18+ (n = 10,465; World Journal of Urology 2023): self-reported urinary incontinence 9.9%, rising from 6.3% at ages 18-39 to 40.8% at ages 75-85; lower with higher education and income [snippet] — [World J Urol 2023](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s00345-023-04677-5)
- Correia et al. 2009 (Int Urogynecol J; telephone interviews, 1,934 people aged 40+): urinary incontinence in the previous month 21.4% of women (95% CI 19.0-23.9) and 7.6% of men (4.8-10.4); overactive bladder 29.4% of women and 35.1% of men; awareness of the condition was low [snippet] — [Springer Medizin](https://www.springermedizin.de/prevalence-treatment-and-known-risk-factors-of-urinary-incontine/8145662), [University of Porto repository](https://repositorio-aberto.up.pt/handle/10216/21944)
- Widely quoted "more than 600 thousand Portuguese" with urinary incontinence is attributed to the Portuguese Urology Association and rests on 2008 data [snippet] — [Correio da Manhã](https://www.cmjornal.pt/sociedade/detalhe/incontinentes_com_cura_ate_90_por_cento); CUF states three in ten women and one in ten men are affected — [CUF](https://www.cuf.pt/saude-a-z/incontinencia-urinaria)
- A pharma-campaign estimate (Astellas, 2021) puts overactive bladder at about 1.7 million Portuguese aged over 40 (treat with caution; source attribution from search summary) [snippet] — [SEP webinar notice](https://www.sep.org.pt/artigo/eventos/15-de-marco-webinar-sobre-bexiga-hiperativa-tem-tratamento/?print=pdf)

**Spain**
- Observatorio Nacional de Incontinencia (Salinas et al., 2010): overall urinary incontinence prevalence 15.8% (95% CI 12.3-21.5), about 6.51 million people (adjusted to the 2009 population); 24% of women and 7% of men; a 2025 Spanish nursing care plan still cites "more than 6.5 million" [snippet] — [Quirónsalud presentation](https://www.quironsalud.es/es/comunicacion/eventos/ii-jornada-innovacion-intercambio-docente/presentaciones-jornada-conjunta-ap-ah-urologia-hgv.ficheros/474811-INCONTINENCIA%20URINARIA.%20UN%20PROBLEMA%20FRECUENTE..pdf), [SECPAL 2025](https://www.secpal.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/PAC-cuidados-continencia-2025.pdf)
- EPICC study (Asociación Española de Urología, 2009): overactive bladder and/or incontinence close to 10% in women aged 25-64, about 5% in men aged 50-65, above 50% in the institutionalised elderly group [snippet] — [Actas Urológicas Españolas](https://scielo.isciii.es/pdf/aue/v33n2/v33n2a11.pdf)

**Brazil**
- Brazil LUTS study (Soler et al., Neurourol Urodyn 2018; telephone survey, 5,184 people aged 40+ in five cities): LUTS prevalence 75% (69% men, 82% women); overactive bladder 25% of men and 24% of women; moderate-to-severe IPSS in 21% of men and 24% of women; a 2019 follow-up found low treatment seeking [snippet] — [PubMed 29106747](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29106747/)

**EU / international**
- EAU "An Urge to Act" campaign: some 55-60 million Europeans have continence health problems; economic burden of urinary incontinence in the EU estimated at €69.1 billion in 2023, rising to €86.7 billion by 2030 without action; burden four times higher for women than men [snippet] — [EAU An Urge to Act](https://uroweb.org/an-urge-to-act), [EAU press release](https://uroweb.org/press-releases/the-annual-economic-burden-of-urinary-incontinence-could-reach-87-billion-in-2030-if-no-action-is-taken)
- EPIC study (Irwin et al., Eur Urol 2006; adults 18+ in Canada, Germany, Italy, Sweden, UK): overactive bladder 11.8% overall (10.8% men, 12.8% women); all urinary incontinence 9.4%; nocturia of one or more voids per night 48.6% of men and 54.5% of women, two or more voids 20.9% and 24% [snippet] — [PubMed 17049716](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17049716/), [PMC4902138](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4902138)
- An APU integrated care plan (2017) cites population surveys estimating that 12% of the Western population has overactive bladder [snippet] — [APU](https://apurologia.pt/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/Plano-de-Cuidados-Integrados-Bexiga-Hiperativa.pdf)

### Inferences
- The addressable population is not the prevalent population. It is the subset who reach a clinician who uses diaries, which practice surveys suggest is a minority (about a quarter of specialists in the Turkish survey routinely use them). Prevalence figures therefore overstate diary demand by a wide margin.
- Because the diary is prescribed (EAU: at initial assessment and follow-up), demand is clinician-triggered: people search for a diary app the week they are told to keep one. This favours distribution through clinicians (urologists, urogynaecologists, GPs, pelvic physiotherapists) over store search alone, and Eleva's own expert network is a natural distribution channel.
- Rough order of magnitude for Portugal (estimate, no direct source): applying 9.9% to roughly 4.5 million adult women gives on the order of 400-450 thousand women with self-reported urinary incontinence; only a fraction seek care in any year.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies how many patients per year are given a bladder diary by urologists, urogynaecologists, GPs or pelvic physiotherapists in Portugal, Spain, Brazil or the EU.
- No survey of pelvic floor physiotherapists' diary use was found.
- ICS standardisation documents were not read directly; the ICS position is represented here only through ICS conference abstracts and the ICI-RS paper.
- No recent (post-2010) population prevalence study for Spain, and no Brazil urinary incontinence figure, was retrieved.

## 5. What does research say about bladder diary apps (validation, adherence, completion, quality)?

### Takeaway
App diaries agree well with validated paper diaries and are completed at least as reliably (about 90%+ over 3 days), and most patients prefer them; a Spanish-language validation exists (eDM3d, Barcelona/Madrid). Independent reviews consistently rate publicly available diary apps as poor for clinical use, and the literature treats diaries as a short assessment instrument rather than a continuously used product.

### Cited Findings
- Spain, Mateu Arrom et al., Neurourology and Urodynamics 2019: app version (eDM3d) of the validated Spanish 3-day paper diary tested in 136 patients with overactive bladder or nocturia; paper-app agreement ICC 0.76-0.95 and kappa 0.56-0.84; 123 (90.4%) completed all variables of the first app diary; 120 (88.2%) would choose the app if they had to repeat a diary; interface limited to four buttons (void, intake, wake, sleep); authors from Fundació Puigvert, Hospital Clínic and Hospital Ramón y Cajal [snippet] — [ICS 2018 abstract 132](https://www.ics.org/2018/abstract/132), [Neurourol Urodyn 2019](https://zendy.io/title/10.1002/nau.23914), [doctoral thesis, University of Barcelona](https://diposit.ub.edu/dspace/bitstream/2445/145398/1/LMA_TESI.pdf)
- Turkey, Koç University, 2022 crossover study (60 participants): app diary completion 96.6% and 92.6% in the two phases versus 90% and 79.4% for paper; app "comparable with and highly correlated with the paper form" [snippet] — [Koç University repository](https://research.hub.ku.edu.tr/entities/publication/42ab388d-9718-4b91-abc7-3cca58286572)
- Brown et al., Obstetrics & Gynecology 2007 (paper diaries): 90.7% of 3-day diaries were complete versus 50% of 7-day diaries (difference 40.7 points, 95% CI 28.8-52.6); even the first 3 days of the 7-day diary were less complete (65%); small unbalanced arms (248 versus 40) [snippet] — [PubMed 17267824](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17267824/)
- Abrams et al., Neurourology and Urodynamics 2016, electronic diaries of differing duration versus paper in overactive bladder: compliance generally high, at least 90% of diaries had one or more entries per day; lowest reliability was the 3-day paper diary [snippet] — [Neurourol Urodyn 2016](https://zendy.io/title/10.1002/nau.22800)
- Ku et al., Neurourology and Urodynamics 2004 (men with LUTS): accuracy did not differ between 2-, 3- and 7-day diaries but burden rose with length [snippet] — [Neurourol Urodyn 2004](https://zendy.io/title/10.1002/nau.20027)
- Kim et al. 2022: smartphone acoustic uroflowmetry diary with automatic voided-volume recording compared with paper for compliance and correlation (numbers not retrieved) — [PMC9041157](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9041157)
- Quality reviews: ICS 2024 (10 apps, mean 7.6/19, "most bladder tracking Apps are unsuited to clinical use", none multilingual) — [ICS 2024 abstract 14](https://www.ics.org/2024/abstract/14); Brazil 2020 (55 apps found, 28 eligible, scores 1.7-4.5) [snippet] — [PubMed 32592019](https://www.pubmed.gov/32592019/); ICS 2025 rapid review (only 4 evidence-based self-management apps downloadable in Spain, UK and Lithuania) — [ICS 2025 abstract 264](https://www.ics.org/2025/abstract/264)
- EAU: diaries are "sensitive to change and are a reliable outcome measure", supporting repeat use at follow-up — [EAU guideline](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/non-neurogenic-female-luts/chapter/diagnosis)

### Inferences
- "Validated against paper" is an achievable and rare differentiator. Structuring Eleva Diary's fields and PDF around a validated instrument (for Spanish, the DM3d structure: frequency-volume chart, urgency, leakage, fluid intake) would give clinicians a reason to recommend it.
- The research supports a 3-day default: completion falls sharply at 7 days on paper, and app completion is about 90%+ at 3 days.
- The 88.2% preference for an app over paper among Spanish patients suggests patient-side demand exists once a clinician has asked for a diary.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed real-world analytics (cohort retention curves, days of use per installer) for any public bladder diary app were found.
- The eDM3d app's current public availability in stores could not be confirmed.
- The Brazilian review's detailed findings (share of apps in Portuguese, features) were not retrieved because the full text was paywalled or blocked.
- No Portuguese (Portugal) validation study of an app diary was found.

## 6. What do 3-7 day episodes mean for retention, lifetime value, and using the app as a funnel into ongoing care?

### Takeaway
A bladder diary is an episodic instrument by clinical design (3 days at assessment, repeated at follow-up), so classic retention metrics will look poor and are the wrong yardstick; the value sits in the moment of completion, when the user holds a clinician-ready report and has a defined problem. No published conversion data exists for a diary-to-consultation funnel, but every incumbent uses the diary as a front end to something else.

### Cited Findings
- Clinical cadence: at least 3 days at initial assessment and again at follow-up; 3-7 days is the reliable range (EAU 2026); 3 days minimum at first presentation (NICE QS77) — [EAU guideline](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/non-neurogenic-female-luts/chapter/diagnosis), [NICE QS77](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/qs77/chapter/Quality-statement-2-Bladder-diaries-and-)
- Diary products are built around this cadence: Minha Bexiga offers fixed 3- or 7-day diaries — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1537991229); Bladderly sells a "3-Day Pass" for $4.99 and single PDF exports for $1.99 — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1523268654)
- Usage intensity signal (unverified developer claim): 7,800 Bladderly users recorded 63,000 diary days — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1523268654); AppGoblin estimates Bladderly monthly active users at 916 (iOS) and 546 (Android) against about 9.4K and 7.4K installs [snippet, estimates] — [AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/1523268654)
- General health-app retention benchmarks: median 30-day retention of 3.3% (15-day 3.9%) across unguided mental health apps (Baumel et al., JMIR 2019) — [JMIR 2019](https://jmir.org/2019/9/e14567/PDF); a 2024 JMIR analysis found a median of 70% of users discontinue within the first 100 days — [JMIR 2024](https://jmir.org/2024/1/e56897/PDF); in the MyHeart Counts research app average engagement was 4.1 days — [PMC9092233](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9092233/); a vendor benchmark reports 44% 30-day retention for healthcare apps versus 67% across categories (Apptentive 2022; not comparable methodology) — [Apptentive](https://apptentive.com/blog/healthcare-apps-mobile-customer-engagement-benchmarks/)
- Even the best-evidenced free pelvic floor app loses most users quickly in the wild: only 11% (2,672) of eligible Tät downloaders answered the 3-month follow-up [snippet] — [Int Urogynecol J 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00192-021-04755-5)
- Examples of an episodic or free tool feeding a longer service:
  - iUFlow: free diary app in front of a uroflowmeter and clinician portal that sends a clinical report to the patient's clinician — [PRWire](https://prwire.com.au/pr/96667/kesem-health-announces-its-launch-of-iuflow-xl-a-bladder-monitoring-platform-with-extended-battery-life-xl)
  - Squeezy: paid consumer app plus "Squeezy Connect", which links the patient to a clinician who monitors adherence — [App Store UK](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/id700740791)
  - Bladderly: free diary leading to subscriptions and to the related proudP urine-flow test — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1523268654), [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/search?q=pee%20tracker&c=apps&hl=en&gl=US)
  - Minha Bexiga: free diary published by the pharmaceutical company EMS S/A as overactive-bladder patient support — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1537991229)
  - Jude: asks for a 3-day diary before a GP appointment within a supplements-and-support business — [Jude GP Toolkit](https://attachments.gorgias.help/uploads.gorgias.io/bgJ1Q6QpoyxvXKOM/Jude_GP_Toolkit-babac99e-0c83-4f00-9520-496defb45a8d.pdf)
  - Hinge Health: pelvic programme with exercise tracking, coaches and pelvic floor physical therapists, sold to employers — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20230328005377/en/Hinge-Health-Announces-Major-Advancements-to-its-Womens-Pelvic-Health-Program)
- Telehealth funnel benchmarks (vendor blogs, directional only): visit-to-transaction rates in telehealth are often below 10% — [Bask Health](https://bask.health/blog/online-sales-strategy); health and fitness users take longest of any category to commit to paying — [Progress](https://progress.com/blogs/how-to-increase-health-app-subscriptions-user-retention)

### Inferences
- Day-30 retention is not a meaningful success metric for a diary. Better metrics: share of installers who complete a 3-day diary, share who export the PDF, share who tap through to an expert, and repeat episodes (follow-up diaries after 6-12 weeks of bladder training or physiotherapy).
- The diary has near-zero standalone lifetime value when free; its value is as customer acquisition for 1:1 sessions. Illustrative arithmetic (assumptions, not data): 1,000 installs × 50% diary completion × 5% booking a session = 25 bookings. Whether that is viable depends on session margin and repeat-booking rate, which are Eleva-internal numbers.
- The completion moment is high intent: the user has a PDF made for a clinician and may not have one. An offer to review the diary with a pelvic floor physiotherapist is contextually natural at that point, and the follow-up diary recommended by EAU creates a legitimate reason for a second episode and a re-engagement notification.
- Local-only storage with no account is a trust and ASO advantage but removes re-marketing ability (no email, no server-side cohort data). A funnel would rely on in-app prompts, opt-in notifications and deep links.
- Standalone and all-in-one are not mutually exclusive: precedent (Squeezy/Squeezy Connect, Bladderly/proudP) shows publishers running a narrow, findable utility alongside a broader service app.

### Gaps
- No published conversion rate from a free health tracker into paid human consultations was found, in this category or adjacent ones; all funnel numbers available are vendor estimates.
- No public data on repeat diary episodes per user or on PDF-export rates.
- No evidence was found on whether clinician-recommended installs convert to marketplace bookings (the user already has a clinician) versus self-directed installs.

- No app found is written for European Portuguese by a clinician-led publisher, free, account-free and with free clinician PDF export. Brazilian Portuguese is served only by a pharma app with a slow update cadence and a generic tracker.

Winning likely means replacing the paper handout (clinician-distributed QR code) rather than winning store search.
- Spanish offers the largest reachable population (Spain plus Latin America) and has a validated diary structure (DM3d) to align with; Brazil is the largest Portuguese-speaking market but would need pt-BR localisation distinct from pt-PT.

### Gaps
- iOS App Store results for "diário miccional" / "diario miccional" in the PT, ES and BR storefronts could not be inspected, so iOS-only Portuguese or Spanish apps may have been missed.
- Translation quality of Bladderly's and Urinote's Portuguese and Spanish versions was not assessed.
- No user reviews in Portuguese or Spanish were retrieved for any app.
- Whether Minha Bexiga has an Android version and how many installs it has was not confirmed.

# App portfolio strategy: one all-in-one app vs multiple focused apps (state of evidence, November 2026)

*Reading guide. Each finding is tagged **[FACT]** (documented event or number), **[CO]** (company-reported claim, not independently verified) or **[OPINION]** (analyst or practitioner view). Verification level: **(V)** = I fetched and read the page; **(S)** = taken from the search-result extract of the page, not read in full, so wording and context should be re-checked before quoting. Dates of older cases are given explicitly. Nothing here relies on US HIPAA.*

## 1. Best documented unbundling cases outside health, and what happened to them

### Takeaway
Of the 2014-2016 "app constellation" wave, almost every satellite app was shut down or folded back into the parent (Dropbox Carousel and Mailbox, Facebook Creative Labs apps, Instagram Boomerang/Hyperlapse/IGTV, LinkedIn Job Search and Groups, Google Duo, Foursquare City Guide). The survivors (Messenger, Uber Eats, Swarm) share one trait: the split-off job is high-frequency and valuable enough to earn its own home-screen slot, and the parent had enormous distribution to force or fund adoption.

### Cited Findings

**Facebook / Messenger (split forced in 2014; kept)**
- [FACT] (V) After Facebook removed chat from the main app, about 95% of iOS reviewers gave Messenger one star in the App Annie review data for 14 July to 13 August 2014. Complaints: no choice, feedback ignored, a duplicate app using more battery and memory. Facebook did not dispute the data and said over 200 million people used Messenger monthly. — [Fortune, 15 Aug 2014](https://fortune.com/2014/08/15/facebook-slammed-for-poor-messenger-app-research-shows/)
- [FACT] (S) Messenger reached 500 million monthly users by November 2014, more than double the pre-split figure. — [InformationWeek](https://informationweek.com/it-leadership/facebook-messenger-nets-500-million-users); [The Next Web, 10 Nov 2014](https://thenextweb.com/news/facebook-reveals-500-million-people-now-using-messenger-month)
- [CO] (S) Zuckerberg (first public Q&A, Nov 2014) called asking everyone to install another app "a big ask", justified it by messaging volume (over 10 billion messages a day) and the friction of reaching chat through a tab in a slow-loading app, and conceded the transition was not handled smoothly. — [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.in/Mark-Zuckerberg-Reveals-Why-You-Were-Forced-To-Download-Facebooks-Separate-Messenger-App/articleshow/45073683.cms); [PCWorld](https://www.pcworld.com/article/2844994/zuckerberg-speaks-why-facebook-isn-t-cool-and-the-reason-behind-the-forced-messenger-migration.html); [The Drum, 8 Nov 2014](https://www.thedrum.com/news/2014/11/08/mark-zuckerberg-admits-he-didn-t-handle-facebook-messenger-transition-smoothly)
- [FACT] (S) By mid-2016 AP reported more than 900 million Messenger users, with some users still resenting the extra app. — [AP via NST, July 2016](https://www.nst.com.my/amp/news/2016/07/161177/facebook-pushing-more-people-app-they-didnt-choose)
- [FACT] (S) Partial re-bundling followed: an April 2019 test put a basic "Chats" inbox back in the main Facebook app, a later test restored voice and video calls, and later reports under the Meta name describe a Messenger inbox returning to the Facebook app. Messenger remained a standalone app throughout. — [Social Media Today, 2019](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/facebooks-looking-to-bring-messaging-back-into-the-main-app/552674/); [SlashGear](https://www.slashgear.com/facebook-test-brings-video-and-voice-calls-back-to-the-main-app-23688140); [Thurrott](https://www.thurrott.com/cloud/280244/meta-testing-adding-messenger-back-to-its-main-facebook-app); [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/97874-meta-bringing-messenger-back-facebook-mobile-app.html)

**Facebook Creative Labs (2014 to Dec 2015; shut down)**
- [FACT] (S) Facebook closed Creative Labs in December 2015 and pulled Slingshot, Riff and Rooms from the stores. A spokesperson said elements of the three had been incorporated into the main Facebook apps. Quartz described the output as "unpopular apps". The lab followed Zuckerberg's early-2014 "lots of apps" strategy. — [Fortune, 7 Dec 2015](https://fortune.com/2015/12/07/facebook-experimental-app-division-shut-down/); [Quartz](https://qz.com/568986/facebook-closes-creative-labs-an-experimental-division-that-produced-unpopular-apps); [MacRumors](https://macrumors.com/2015/12/07/facebook-ends-creative-labs-initiative)

**Foursquare / Swarm (split May 2014; original app eventually killed)**
- [FACT] (S) Foursquare moved check-ins into Swarm in 2014 and made the main app a discovery guide. Loyal users reported quitting once Swarm was required for check-ins; loss of badges and mayorships, crashes and battery drain were cited. — [NPR, 30 July 2014](https://www.npr.org/blogs/alltechconsidered/2014/07/30/336531811/some-loyal-foursquare-users-are-checking-out-after-swarm-spinoff); [Engadget, 24 June 2015](https://www.engadget.com/2015-06-24-foursquare-swarm-split.html)
- [CO] (S) Foursquare said about one-third of users used only Swarm, one-third only the new Foursquare, and one-third both. The exact page carrying this quote was not verified; it surfaced in 2015 coverage. — [Engadget, 2015](https://www.engadget.com/2015-06-24-foursquare-swarm-split.html); [Fortune, 13 May 2015](https://fortune.com/2015/05/13/foursquare-swarm-update/)
- [OPINION] (S) VentureBeat ran both "a relaunch saved Foursquare from certain death" and, later, a piece on the "frightening decline" once launch hype subsided. — [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/how-a-relaunch-saved-foursquare-from-certain-death); [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/as-relaunch-hype-subsides-will-foursquare-survive)
- [FACT] (S) Foursquare shut the City Guide app on 15 December 2024 (web version 28 April 2025) to "focus our efforts on building an even better experience in Swarm". The spun-out app outlived the parent. — [Foursquare sunset page](https://foursquare.com/city-guide-sunset/); [Engadget, Oct 2024](https://www.engadget.com/social-media/foursquare-is-killing-its-city-guide-app-to-focus-on-the-check-in-app-swarm-191054153.html); [The Register, 27 Nov 2024](https://www.theregister.com/2024/11/27/foursquare_to_close/)

**Dropbox Carousel and Mailbox (2013/2014 to 2016; shut down)**
- [FACT] (S) Dropbox announced in December 2015 that Mailbox would close on 26 February 2016 and Carousel on 31 March 2016. Carousel's key features were folded into the main Dropbox app; the Mailbox team moved to Dropbox Paper. Press coverage paraphrased Dropbox as saying customers had shown they prefer one app over several, and noted the pivot to business users. — [Engadget, 7 Dec 2015](https://www.engadget.com/2015/12/07/dropbox-is-killing-its-carousel-and-mailbox-apps/); [PCWorld](https://www.pcworld.com/article/418666/dropbox-to-shutter-mailbox-email-and-carousel-photo-apps.html); [The Register](https://www.theregister.co.uk/2015/12/07/dropbox_to_kill_mailbox_and_carousel/)

**Instagram satellite apps (2014-2022; shut down)**
- [FACT] (S) Instagram removed standalone Boomerang (2015) and Hyperlapse (2014) from the stores after 1 March 2022, shortly after announcing the end of the standalone IGTV app, "to better focus our efforts on the main app". Both features already lived in the main camera. The 2019 close-friends "Threads" app was shut in 2021. Apptopia figures reported by press: Hyperlapse 23 million lifetime downloads; Boomerang's figure is garbled in the extract (probably 301 million). — [9to5Mac, 7 Mar 2022](https://9to5mac.com/2022/03/07/instagram-discontinues-its-standalone-boomerang-and-hyperlapse-apps/); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-has-removed-its-separate-boomerang-and-hyperlapse-apps-from-app-s/619945); [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2022/03/07/instagrams-boomerang-and-hyperlapse-apps-are-no-more/)

**LinkedIn (unbundled 2014-2015; consolidated 2018-2019)**
- [FACT] (S) LinkedIn shut its standalone Groups iOS app on 15 February 2018 and reintegrated Groups into the flagship app, saying a standalone app could not take advantage of the wider LinkedIn ecosystem. It retired the standalone Job Search app in May 2019, saying job seekers would get more value from the core app. — [The Letter Two, Jan 2018](https://thelettertwo.com/2018/01/15/linkedin-to-place-larger-focus-on-groups-shutters-standalone-app/); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/linkedin-to-integrate-groups-into-the-mobile-app/265845/); [HCM Technology Report, 2019](https://www.hcmtechnologyreport.com/linkedin-will-fold-job-search-into-core-app/)

**Google (repeated consolidation)**
- [FACT] (S) In June 2022 Google announced Duo and Meet would become one app (Duo renamed Meet and given Meet's features). Allo was shut and features moved to Messages; Inbox was sunset in favour of Gmail. No single official rationale; commentary cites duplication and a 2020 reorganisation of messaging under one team. — [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/news/google-just-merged-google-meet-and-duo-what-it-means-for-you); [PYMNTS, 2022](https://www.pymnts.com/news/2022/google-to-merge-google-meet-and-duo-features/); [How-To Geek](https://www.howtogeek.com/808900/google-is-mixing-up-its-video-chat-apps-again/)
- [FACT] (S) Current example: on 7 May 2026 Google announced the Fitbit app becomes the "Google Health" app (switch on 19 May 2026, mandatory, automatic data transition, some features such as badges dropped), and Google Fit users will be invited to migrate later in 2026. Three health apps are being collapsed into one. — [Google blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/google-health/google-health-app/); [9to5Google, 10 May 2026](https://9to5google.com/2026/05/10/google-health-kills-the-fitbit-we-knew-but-maybe-thats-not-a-bad-thing/); [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/google-fit-to-health-data-migration-3664447/)

**Uber / Uber Eats (two apps kept, but increasingly bundled)**
- [FACT] (S) In June 2019 Uber began embedding Eats ordering inside the main Uber app while keeping the Eats app. — [TechCrunch, 4 June 2019](https://techcrunch.com/2019/06/04/uber-eats-uber-eats/)
- [CO] (S) More than 10% of new Uber Eats users arrived through the ride-hailing channel (figure reported in 2019-2021 coverage; exact date unverified). — [Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/ubers-new-membership-bundles-rides-delivery-services/610240)
- [CO] (S) Q3 2025 earnings call: cross-platform consumers spend about 3x more and retain about 35% better than single-product consumers. — [Uber Q3 2025 transcript](https://s23.q4cdn.com/407969754/files/doc_financials/2025/q3/Uber-Q3-25-Earnings-Call-Transcript.pdf)
- [CO] (V) Q2 2026 prepared remarks: Uber One is "our strongest cross-platform consumer value proposition"; members account for more than 70% of Delivery gross bookings, up about 20 points in two years; the agreed Delivery Hero acquisition will "nearly double the number of markets where we offer both Mobility and Delivery". — [Uber Q2 2026 prepared remarks](https://s23.q4cdn.com/407969754/files/doc_earnings/2026/q2/transcript/Uber-Q2-26-Prepared-Remarks.pdf)

### Inferences
- Scorecard from the cases above: kept as separate apps = Messenger, Uber Eats, Swarm. Shut or folded back = Carousel, Mailbox, Slingshot, Riff, Rooms, Boomerang, Hyperlapse, IGTV, Threads (2019 version), LinkedIn Groups, LinkedIn Job Search, Duo, Allo, Inbox, Foursquare City Guide, Fitbit app (2026). The base rate for satellite apps surviving is low even for companies with hundreds of millions of users.
- Stated reasons for folding back are consistent: focus engineering on the main app, the feature gains more inside the parent ecosystem, and users preferring one app.
- The survivors were daily-use jobs (messaging, food ordering, check-ins). A bladder diary is intensive but episodic (typically a few days at a time), which resembles the utilities that got folded back more than it resembles Messenger.
- Uber's trajectory matters for Eleva: even where two apps survive, the value came from linking them (shared account, membership, in-app cross-entry), not from separation itself.

### Gaps
- No verified long-run download or retention series for Swarm vs Foursquare after the split.
- Zuckerberg's often-repeated claim that people reply about 20% faster in Messenger could not be sourced in this session.
- Meta's newer standalone launches (Threads 2023, Edits 2025) are counterexamples I did not research or source here.
- No primary Dropbox blog text retrieved; the "customers prefer one app" line is press paraphrase.

## 2. Digital health and wellness cases: tracker or utility app next to a care app, or merged

### Takeaway
The dominant pattern in digital health through 2026 is consolidation into one member-facing app with modes or programmes inside it (Flo, Clue, Hinge Health, Teladoc, WeightWatchers, Google Health). Where two apps exist, the split usually follows a different payer, access model or brand (Calm vs Calm Health, Headspace vs Headspace Care, Teladoc vs BetterHelp, Hims vs Hers, Doctolib vs Doctolib Pro), not a different feature, and several of those companies say they are working to merge.

### Cited Findings

**Merged, or merging, into one app**
- [FACT] (S) Headspace + Ginger (US$3bn merger, 2021): from 1 January 2023 the Headspace app became the single entry point ("one front door") for employer members, combining meditation with Ginger coaching, therapy and psychiatry. In October 2023 the Ginger app was rebranded Headspace Care. — [Behavioral Health Business, 18 Nov 2022](https://bhbusiness.com/2022/11/18/a-year-after-3b-mega-merger-headspace-and-ginger-roll-out-unified-behavioral-health-offering/); [MedCity News, Nov 2022](https://medcitynews.com/2022/11/one-front-door-headspace-health-launches-new-platform-following-merger-with-ginger); [BusinessWire, 31 Oct 2023](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20231031458164/en)
- [CO] (S) Headspace's help centre says it is "actively working to merge all services into a single, streamlined app experience"; meanwhile care is delivered through either app depending on plan, and a Headspace Care membership does not grant access to the Headspace app. The Headspace Care app was still receiving updates in November 2025. Five years after the merger the two-app state had not been fully unwound. — [Headspace help centre](https://help.headspace.com/hc/en-us/articles/19803162155547-What-is-Headspace-Care); [Headspace help centre](https://help.headspace.com/hc/en-us/articles/19804167223835-Does-my-Headspace-Care-membership-grant-me-access-to-the-Headspace-app); [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/be/app/headspace-care-ginger/id515118602)
- [FACT] (S) Teladoc launched one app on 5 January 2023 putting primary care, mental health and chronic condition management under one account and one brand. BetterHelp, its direct-to-consumer therapy app, stayed separate. Teladoc reported one in three Primary360 members used at least one additional Teladoc service. A successor platform, Teladoc One, was announced in 2026. No app-specific adoption figures were found. — [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/health-tech/teladoc-unveils-new-app-integrating-services-primary-care-and-mental-health-and-expands); [Teladoc press release](https://www.teladochealth.com/newsroom/press/teladoc-health-launches-fully-integrated-whole-person-care-experience-with-new-app); [Healthcare Dive](https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/teladoc-integrated-health-app-telehealth-services/639766/); [Healthcare IT News, 2026](https://www.healthcareitnews.com/news/teladoc-launches-ai-driven-platform-new-era-connected-care)
- [FACT] (S) WeightWatchers acquired telehealth firm Sequence (2023) and made WeightWatchers Clinic "the complete product integration of Sequence ... available within the WeightWatchers App experience"; its GLP-1 programme is also in the same app. — [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/digital-health/weightwatchers-rolls-out-telehealth-clinic-prescribe-weight-loss-meds); [MedCity News, Dec 2023](https://medcitynews.com/2023/12/weightwatchers-glp1-weight-loss/); [WW press release via Fitt](https://insider.fitt.co/press-release/weightwatchers-launches-new-behavior-change-program-designed-to-support-the-unique-needs-of-individuals-on-glp-1-medications/)
- [FACT] (S) Hinge Health's Women's Pelvic Health programme (launched April 2022) is "accessible via a member's existing Hinge Health app", pitched as a single platform for all musculoskeletal care. — [Hinge Health press release](https://hingehealth.com/resources/press-releases/womens-pelvic-health-program-launch); [BusinessWire, 26 Apr 2022](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220426006354/en)
- [FACT] (S) Flo is one app with modes for cycle tracking, trying to conceive, pregnancy and perimenopause. "Flo for Partners" (Oct 2023) and "Flo for Perimenopause" (available 21 July 2025) shipped as in-app features, not new apps. Flo cites over 440 million cumulative downloads (Dec 2025). — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/flo-cycle-period-tracker/id1038369065); [Flo newsroom, July 2025](https://flo.health/newsroom/flo-for-perimenopause-is-launching-to-empower-the-1-billion-women-who-experience-perimenopause-without-the-support-they-deserve); [Flo for healthcare professionals](https://med.flo.health/)
- [FACT] (S) Clue (Berlin) is one app with five modes, including Conceive, Pregnancy and Perimenopause; life stages are monetised as paid modes inside the same app. — [Clue support](https://support.helloclue.com/hc/en-us/articles/16701057975581-What-are-modes); [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/clue-period-cycle-tracker/id657189652)

**Retention context for standalone health trackers**
- [FACT] (S) Baumel et al., JMIR 2019: across 93 Android mental health apps with 10,000+ installs, median 15-day retention was 3.9% and 30-day retention 3.3%. Tracker apps (median 6.1% at day 30) and peer-support apps (8.9%) retained better than breathing-exercise apps (0.0%). — [JMIR 2019;21(9):e14567](https://www.jmir.org/2019/9/e14567)

### Inferences
- In health, the line between apps tends to be drawn around who pays and who is allowed in (consumer vs employer or insurer, patient vs professional, men vs women brand), not around features. Eleva's diary-versus-care line is a feature line, which is the type that companies have mostly merged.
- A second app for professionals (Doctolib Pro pattern) is a better-evidenced split than a second app for the same consumer. If Eleva ever runs two apps, member vs expert is the precedent-backed boundary.
- Calm Health is the closest analogue to option A done deliberately: separate apps, shared login, shared content. It exists because access is invitation-only through payers, a constraint Eleva does not have.
- Headspace shows the cost of starting with two apps: re-merging was announced in 2022 and was still incomplete in late 2025.
- Flo and Clue show that a free tracker can be the top of the funnel inside the same app that sells paid services; neither chose a separate free app for that role.
- No documented case was found of a small health startup running a free no-account tracker as a separate app purely to feed a care marketplace, with reported conversion numbers. Option A would be an experiment without a published benchmark.

### Gaps
- No usable evidence found in this session on Natural Cycles, Maven Clinic, Ro, Ada Health, MyFitnessPal, Oura or Whoop app-portfolio decisions. From general knowledge these run a single consumer app, but I did not source that here.
- No Portugal-specific precedents found.
- No company reported conversion from a free tracker into paid care sessions.
- Headspace help-centre pages returned HTTP 403 on direct fetch; quotes come from search extracts and may be outdated.
- Elvie's 2025 change of ownership to Willow is implied by the App Store publisher name but was not separately sourced.

## 3. Super apps in Western and EU markets versus Asia

### Takeaway
Analysts largely agree the WeChat-style super app has not transferred to the West, citing no unmet need (the phone OS already acts as the bundle), lower trust, entrenched specialist competitors and tighter regulation. What has worked in the West is narrower: bundling adjacent services under one account and one membership (Uber), which is not the same as a super app.

### Cited Findings
- [OPINION] (V) Forrester, "The Super App Window Has Closed" (1 Aug 2023, Xiaofeng Wang and Julie Ask): four obstacles in the West are no compelling consumer need because iOS and Android already provide super-app-like functions, lower trust, more competition, and stricter regulation. Recommendation: do not copy the model; build "a simple, light, easy-to-use" app that earns trust through convenience. — [Forrester](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/the-super-app-window-has-closed/)
- [FACT] (V) Same source, survey data: 58% of online adults in metro China said they trust brands' social media content versus 20% in the US (Forrester 2022 benchmark survey). — [Forrester](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/the-super-app-window-has-closed/)
- [OPINION] (V) Dealroom (27 Oct 2021): barriers are antitrust concerns about walled gardens, mature economies with entrenched oligopolies, and heterogeneous consumer behaviour. PayPal was the leading Western aspirant; its rumoured US$45bn Pinterest bid was dropped after shareholder pushback and a 12% share fall in three days. — [Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/blog/where-are-the-western-superapps)
- [FACT] (S) PayPal announced a "super app" in 2021, then pulled back from stock-trading ambitions in 2022 as it cut costs; by 2026 its new CEO was pinning recovery on Venmo as a money-management app. — [TechCrunch, 29 July 2021](https://techcrunch.com/2021/07/29/paypals-new-super-app-is-ready-to-launch-will-also-include-messaging/); [Axios, 3 Aug 2022](https://www.axios.com/pro/fintech-deals/2022/08/03/a-dent-in-paypals-superapp-ambitions); [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/save-paypal--ceo-lores-pins-hopes-on-venmo)
- [OPINION] (S) European commentary: users favour best-in-class single-purpose apps with entrenched loyalty; Asia's super apps grew from mobile-first adoption, fragmented markets and low card penetration, conditions absent in Europe. — [Netguru](https://www.netguru.com/blog/europe-lagging-superapp-race); [LogRocket](https://blog.logrocket.com/product-management/super-apps-growth-playbook-tech-mirage)
- [OPINION] (S) UX view: super apps trade immediate intuitiveness for eventual learnability; Western design norms prize minimalism. Nielsen Norman Group's WeChat mini-program research found usability problems such as lost progress, and that users discovered mini programs mostly through chats and friends, not by browsing. — [Foolproof](https://foolproof.co.uk/journal/design-its-not-all-the-same); [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/wechat-mini-programs/); [arXiv 2412.06238](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.06238)
- [OPINION] (V) Benedict Evans (1 Aug 2014): "single-purpose applications have an advantage on smartphone interfaces" because there is "very little room for links to more features" and extras get pushed into hamburger menus; yet bundling works when the app has a natural context (Baidu Maps, WeChat). The trade-off is "app discovery challenges" versus "feature discovery challenges". — [Benedict Evans](https://ben-evans.com/benedictevans/2014/8/1/app-unbundling-search-and-discovery)

### Inferences
- Option B (booking, experts, academy, video, messaging, diary) is not a super app in the analysts' sense. All features serve one domain and one relationship (a woman and her pelvic-health care), with no third-party mini-apps or unrelated verticals. The Western super-app failure literature therefore does not count against option B; it is closer to the Teladoc, Hinge and Flo single-domain bundle.
- Evans's "natural context" test favours bundling here: the diary's output is an input to the consultation, so the care relationship is a context that makes the diary discoverable.
- The trust finding cuts the other way for a sensitive tracker: a low-commitment, no-account tool may be easier to try than a marketplace app that looks like it wants registration. That is an argument about onboarding design, which can be met inside one app as well as with a second app.

### Gaps
- No rigorous EU consumer study comparing satisfaction with bundled versus single-purpose health apps was found; most sources are commentary.
- Status of X's "everything app" and of European candidates (Revolut, Bolt) in 2026 was not established.

## 4. Decision criteria recommended by product leaders and analysts

### Takeaway
There is no canonical framework from the named authorities; published criteria are practitioner heuristics that converge on the same tests: are the audiences different, are the jobs distinct and frequent enough to deserve a home-screen slot, does the feature gain or lose from the parent's context, and can the team afford a second product. I found nothing specific from Lenny Rachitsky, Reforge or Intercom on this question.

### Cited Findings
- [OPINION] (V) Evans: unbundle when a feature is buried and would be understood better alone; bundle when a shared context (map, chat) makes features discoverable. Unbundled apps are "easy to understand but hard to find". — [Benedict Evans, 2014](https://ben-evans.com/benedictevans/2014/8/1/app-unbundling-search-and-discovery)
- [OPINION] (S) Evans and Sinofsky (a16z) frame bundling and unbundling as a recurring cycle, not a one-way trend; by 2015 commentators were already asking whether mobile was re-bundling. — [a16z, 16 Mobile Theses](https://a16z.com/16-mobile-theses/); [Benedict Evans podcast note](https://www.ben-evans.com/benedictevans/2014/8/15/podcast-bundling-and-unbundling)
- [OPINION] (S) Agency guidance: no universal rule; split when a distinct user group's experience no longer works inside the larger app (Uber rider vs driver is the standard example, after the shared app became "too big, too complex, too heavy"). Costs: ongoing spend rises, users can be confused, notifications must be coordinated; Foursquare is cited as the cautionary tale. — [Glance](https://thisisglance.com/learning-centre/when-should-i-split-my-app-into-multiple-products)
- [OPINION] (S) Localytics/Upland: go multi-app only if products differ enough and the team can handle several apps; a single app is "typically better if you lack capacity or all your services are similar". — [Upland Localytics](https://uplandsoftware.com/localytics/?p=1558)
- [OPINION] (S) LinkedIn's own reason for re-merging Groups: a standalone app "was not able to take advantage of the overall LinkedIn ecosystem". — [The Letter Two](https://thelettertwo.com/2018/01/15/linkedin-to-place-larger-focus-on-groups-shutters-standalone-app/)
- [OPINION] (S) Zuckerberg's test for Messenger: the activity must be frequent enough that reaching it through a tab is real friction. — [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.in/Mark-Zuckerberg-Reveals-Why-You-Were-Forced-To-Download-Facebooks-Separate-Messenger-App/articleshow/45073683.cms)
- [OPINION] (V) Forrester: favour a simple, light app and deliver "only the services they need" dynamically, instead of adding apps or building a super app. — [Forrester](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/the-super-app-window-has-closed/)
- [FACT] (V) Platform rule relevant to scope: Apple guideline 5.1.1(v) says "If your app doesn't include significant account-based features, let people use it without a login", and guideline 4.3(a) warns against multiple bundle IDs of the same app. Guideline 5.1.3(ii) says apps "may not store personal health information in iCloud". — [Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

### Inferences
Criteria checklist a judge panel can score (my synthesis of the sources above):

| Criterion | Points to two apps when | Points to one app when | Eleva reading (inference) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Audience overlap | Audiences barely overlap | Same people use both | Diary users with symptoms are the care marketplace's target; a share will be non-Eleva patients sent by any clinician |
| Job distinctness | Jobs unrelated | One job feeds the other | Diary output feeds the consultation |
| Frequency | Satellite job is daily and habitual | Satellite job is episodic | Diary is intense for days, then dormant |
| Context | Feature is buried in menus | Parent gives context and discovery | Depends on whether diary gets a first-class tab |
| Access or payer | Different gatekeeper or payer | Same account works | Same consumer; no payer wall |
| Brand and trust | Parent brand deters trial | Parent brand adds trust | Open question; untested |
| Store discovery | Distinct search intent ("bladder diary") | Same keywords | Distinct intent exists; a focused listing may rank better |
| Team capacity | Team can fund two products | Capacity is tight | Founder-led small team |
| Reversibility | — | — | Merging later is slow (Headspace); splitting later forces a second download (Messenger, Swarm) |

- Most rows lean to one app for a small team; the rows that lean to two apps are store discovery and low-commitment trial, both of which concern acquisition, not ongoing use.
- A no-login diary inside the single app is permitted and even encouraged by Apple's 5.1.1(v), so "usable with no account" does not require a separate app.

### Gaps
- No Lenny's Newsletter, Reforge, Intercom or Nielsen Norman Group piece that directly addresses one app versus several was found; do not attribute a framework to them.
- No quantitative evidence on whether a focused store listing outperforms a broad one for health keywords (app store optimisation data from Sensor Tower, data.ai or AppsFlyer was not found).

## 5. Cost of maintaining multiple apps for small teams, and cross-install rates

### Takeaway
Rule-of-thumb maintenance is 15-25% of build cost per app per year (vendor estimates, not research), and it rises with each additional native app. No public benchmark exists for what share of app-one users install app two; the few disclosed numbers suggest a minority do, even for giants, which is the main quantitative risk for a funnel that depends on diary users later downloading a second Eleva app.

### Cited Findings
- [OPINION] (S) Agency estimates: annual maintenance is commonly 15-20% of initial development cost, with ranges up to 25-35% and over 40% possible in year one; "if you develop multiple native apps, your annual app maintenance costs could be higher". These come from vendors with a commercial interest. — [Koombea](https://www.koombea.com/blog/app-maintenance-cost/); [Netguru](https://www.netguru.com/blog/mobile-app-maintenance-cost); [Glance](https://thisisglance.com/learning-centre/how-much-does-it-cost-to-maintain-a-mobile-app-each-year)
- [CO] (S) Foursquare (2015): roughly one-third of users used both apps after the split, one-third each used only one. — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2015-06-24-foursquare-swarm-split.html)
- [CO] (S) Uber: more than 10% of new Eats users came through the rides channel; in 2019 the CEO said 50% of Eats customers did not use Uber rides. — [Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/ubers-new-membership-bundles-rides-delivery-services/610240); [The Verge mirror, 30 May 2019](https://techyes.pages.dev/2019/5/30/18646470/uber-eats-ride-hailing-promotions-dara-khosrowshahi)
- [CO] (S) Teladoc: one in three Primary360 members used at least one additional service, within one unified platform. — [Fierce Healthcare, Jan 2023](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/health-tech/teladoc-unveils-new-app-integrating-services-primary-care-and-mental-health-and-expands)
- [FACT] (S) AppsFlyer documents how to attribute cross-promotion installs between a publisher's own apps; its illustrative example shows source apps converting at 7.92% and 18.22% of clicks to installs. These are documentation examples of click-to-install conversion, not a benchmark and not a share of the user base. — [AppsFlyer help](https://support.appsflyer.com/hc/en-us/articles/115004481946)
- [OPINION] (S) Ad-tech vendors say cross-promotion is cheaper than acquiring a new user, with the caveat that promoting a second app to a still-loyal user can cannibalise the first. — [Singular](https://singular.net/blog/the-coming-explosion-in-app-cross-marketing); [Adikteev](https://adikteev.com/blog/why-smart-cross-promotion-is-essential-to-your-app-marketing-strategy)
- [FACT] (S) comScore: 51% of US smartphone users downloaded zero apps in a typical month (2017 report; 65.5% in the 2014 report), and 42% of app time went to the user's single most used app. — [TechCrunch, 25 Aug 2017](https://techcrunch.com/2017/08/25/majority-of-u-s-consumers-still-download-zero-apps-per-month-says-comscore/); [Quartz, 2014](https://qz.com/253618/most-smartphone-users-download-zero-apps-per-month)

### Inferences
- Two apps roughly double the fixed overhead that does not scale with features: two store listings and review cycles, two release trains, two sets of privacy labels and consent flows, two analytics and crash pipelines, and, for option A, a sync protocol plus account-linking between a local-only store and the Eleva backend. Shared code (one repository, shared modules) reduces but does not remove this.
- If only a minority of diary users ever install the second app (a third at Foursquare with a forced migration; lower should be assumed for a voluntary cross-install), the diary-to-booking funnel in option A loses most users at the download step. In option B the same step is a tab switch.
- The US download-reluctance data is old (2014, 2017) but directionally supports treating every extra install as a significant drop-off point.

### Gaps
- No Sensor Tower, data.ai, AppsFlyer, Adjust or Appfigures benchmark on same-publisher cross-install share was found. This number appears to be unpublished and would have to be measured.
- No independent (non-vendor) study of maintenance cost for small teams running two apps versus one.
- No current (2025-2026) or EU-specific data on monthly app download behaviour.

## 6. How users react to being pushed into a bigger app or into a second app

### Takeaway
Forced moves in either direction generate loud, measurable backlash (Messenger's ~95% one-star month; Foursquare loyalists quitting; Fitbit users losing features in the 2026 Google Health switch), but backlash did not reverse the decision in any case found, and outcomes depended on the underlying value of the job. The lesson for a new product is to avoid needing a forced migration later, in either direction.

### Cited Findings
- [FACT] (V) Messenger, Aug 2014: about 95% one-star iOS reviews over one month; a typical review read "Facebook keep thinking that we should want another app outside of the main app. They just can't get that we don't". — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2014/08/15/facebook-slammed-for-poor-messenger-app-research-shows/)
- [FACT] (S) Facebook answered with an in-app explainer on permissions; usage still more than doubled to 500 million within months. — [The Next Web, 27 Aug 2014](https://thenextweb.com/news/facebook-responds-messenger-backlash-news-feed-alert-explanation-mobile-apps); [InformationWeek](https://informationweek.com/it-leadership/facebook-messenger-nets-500-million-users)
- [FACT] (S) Foursquare, 2014: long-time users told NPR they stopped once Swarm was required, saying the value was no longer there. — [NPR](https://www.npr.org/blogs/alltechconsidered/2014/07/30/336531811/some-loyal-foursquare-users-are-checking-out-after-swarm-spinoff)
- [FACT] (S) Forced bundling, 2026: the Fitbit-to-Google Health change is mandatory with no option to stay on the old app, and drops features such as badges; coverage framed it as "kills the Fitbit we knew". — [9to5Google, 10 May 2026](https://9to5google.com/2026/05/10/google-health-kills-the-fitbit-we-knew-but-maybe-thats-not-a-bad-thing/)
- [OPINION] (S) Dropbox's stated lesson on closing Carousel and Mailbox, as paraphrased by press: customers preferred one app to several. — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2015/12/07/dropbox-is-killing-its-carousel-and-mailbox-apps/)
- [FACT] (S) Clue review example of in-app friction: a user could not switch life stage to pregnancy without subscribing, showing that bundling modes in one app moves the complaint from "another app" to "paywalled mode". — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/clue-period-cycle-tracker/id657189652)

### Inferences
- Backlash is about being forced and about losing something, more than about the number of apps. A product that starts in its final shape avoids it.
- Both options carry a migration risk if reversed: A to B means telling diary-only users to move to a bigger app (the Fitbit pattern, plus a local-data import problem because the diary has no account); B to A means telling users to download a second app for something they already had (the Messenger and Swarm pattern).
- Rating damage from a forced split fell on the new app's listing. For a small publisher with few reviews, a burst of one-star reviews would weigh far more than it did for Facebook.

### Gaps
- No longitudinal data on whether Messenger's or Swarm's ratings recovered.
- No survey of EU or Portuguese users on preference for bundled versus separate health apps.
- No evidence specific to women's-health users on whether a discreet single-purpose tracker is preferred over a tracker inside a branded care app; this is a plausible but unsupported claim in either direction.

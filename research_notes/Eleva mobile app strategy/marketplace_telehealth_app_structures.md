# Marketplace and telehealth mobile app structures (one app vs customer/"Pro" apps, bundling, sequencing)

Observation date for everything below: 11 November 2026. Evidence labels used in the findings:
- **[fetched]** = I opened the page itself in this session.
- **[listing found]** = the App Store / Google Play listing was returned by a store-restricted search; listing existence and the quoted rating are from the search summary, not from opening every listing. Ratings differ by storefront and date.

## 1. Customer versus provider apps: one app with a mode switch, or separate apps?

### Takeaway
Among the companies checked, separate customer and provider ("Pro"/"Biz"/"Driver") apps are the dominant pattern wherever the provider is a working professional running a business on the platform (booking, health, delivery, home services); a single app with a mode switch survives mainly where the two sides are the same kind of person (Airbnb host/guest, BlaBlaCar driver/passenger). I found no case of a company merging a provider app back into the customer app; Etsy rebuilt its seller app as a new separate app, Thumbtack kept two apps, and Airbnb stayed unified and expanded the single app in 2025.

**Reasons given**
- Fred Wilson (USV), 3 Nov 2015: in the mobile era "it seems that it is better to have different native mobile apps for both sides of the market"; he had seen combined apps and "it doesn't work that well"; he acknowledges it makes the business "harder, more complicated, and more expensive". He names Hailo, Sidecar, Etsy (buyer app plus Sell on Etsy) and SoundCloud (Pulse) as two-app examples [fetched] — [AVC](https://avc.com/2015/11/mobile-apps-for-both-sides-of-the-market/)
- Commenters on that post: Peter Secor argued Lyft's single app worked because many drivers are also passengers; Laurie Kalmanson argued for "separated workflows" with easy identity switching rather than separate apps; Anshumani Ruddra and Joe Cardillo warned that a separate supply-side app tends to get less staffing and attention, which hurts over time [fetched] — [AVC](https://avc.com/2015/11/mobile-apps-for-both-sides-of-the-market/)
- An interview-prep write-up summarises the Uber rationale as workflow disparity: drivers work 8+ hours in the app, riders use it for 5 to 10 minutes, with different mental models. This is third-party commentary, not an Uber statement [search summary] — [PracHub](https://prachub.com/interview-questions/uber-app-strategy-bundle-ride-hailing-and-food-delivery-or-unbundle-them)
- Sharetribe (CEO Juho Makkonen, published 4 Feb 2026, updated 18 Sep 2026): buyers and sellers have different needs; supply-side tasks may be complex and infrequent while buyers browse and message on mobile; default to web first, then "add a mobile app for one or both user groups" once behaviour is understood; do not build separate interfaces for both sides at launch [fetched] — [Sharetribe Academy](https://www.sharetribe.com/academy/marketplace-mobile-app-why-when-how/)

**Changes of approach and reported friction**
- Thumbtack's April 2024 relaunch ("One App for Your Home", described as the biggest product update in its history) was a customer-side repositioning; the Pro app remained a separate listing [search summary] — [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240402253139/en/One-App-for-Your-Home-Introducing-a-New-Thumbtack-for-a-New-Generation-of-Homeowners)
- Airbnb hosts on the community forum complained after a redesign that moving between the guest inbox and host inbox went from one click to "at least 4 clicks" and that the travelling inbox sometimes shows no option to switch back to hosting [search summary] — [Airbnb Community](https://community.withairbnb.com/t5/Community-Cafe/New-interface-is-awkward-to-terrible/td-p/1327358)
- Etsy sellers in June 2024 still had the retired "Sell on Etsy" app installed and could not update, because it was filed under "S" rather than "E" on their phones [search summary] — [EcommerceBytes](https://www.ecommercebytes.com/2024/06/08/what-to-do-if-you-cant-update-the-etsy-seller-app/)
- Founder anecdotes in the AVC thread: Michael Brill's team built one app for both sides, found only sellers needed it and moved buyers to a responsive website; Ish Jindal's team removed the supply side from mobile and kept it on desktop after the combined version "got really messed up" [fetched] — [AVC](https://avc.com/2015/11/mobile-apps-for-both-sides-of-the-market/)

### Inferences
- The split correlates with role asymmetry, not company size: when the provider is a professional with scheduling, records and money to manage, every booking and health platform checked ships a distinct provider app. Eleva's experts (licensed professionals with CRM, finances, session records) sit on the "separate provider surface" side of that line; its members sit on the consumer side.
- "Separate surface" does not have to mean "separate native app". BetterHelp and Preply both serve providers on mobile with a thin subset (messages, schedule) and keep the heavy work on a computer or browser. That is a third option between "one app with a mode switch" and "two native apps".
- No evidence was found of anyone merging a pro app into a consumer app. The direction of travel in the evidence is either "stay unified because both sides are peers" (Airbnb) or "keep separate and rebuild the supply app" (Etsy).

### Gaps
- Vinted, ClassPass, Zocdoc, Superprof and Cambly were not verified. The only Vinted listing surfaced was "Vinted Go Driver", a logistics app, which says nothing about the main marketplace app. No "Cambly Tutors" or Zocdoc provider listing surfaced in store-restricted searches, which is absence of a search hit, not proof of absence.
- No first-party statement from Airbnb, Uber, Doctolib, Booksy or Fresha explaining why they chose their structure was found; the reasons above are from investors, vendors and commentators.
- The Lyft example in the AVC thread is from 2015 and was not re-checked against the 2026 store listings.
- Preply's help centre was summarised in search results as saying tutors can enter the Preply Classroom from a phone or tablet browser (video, file sharing, messaging, notes) while students can join via the app; I did not capture the article URL, so this is unverified. A third-party guide says tutors should use a computer for the lesson and the phone for schedule and messages — [StoryLearning](https://storylearning.com/teach/english-teaching-apps)

## 2. Telehealth and therapy platforms: app structure, provider tooling, and where video runs

### Takeaway
Patient/member video inside a native app is standard among the telehealth players checked; providers are served by a separate native provider app (Teladoc, Talkspace, Maven's MPractice, Doctolib Pro, Doctoralia for specialists, knok's doctor app) or by web/desktop tools (Kry/Livi's Livi Connect, BetterHelp therapists for video). Where a provider mobile app exists it is usually a companion (agenda, messaging, notifications), not the full back office.

- The closest structural analogue to Eleva (women's-health marketplace of independent practitioners delivering 1:1 video) is Maven, and Maven runs two native apps. Its provider app is small in audience (6 ratings) yet 171.5 MB and updated about monthly, which illustrates the standing cost of a provider-only native app for a small user base.

- Provider video on mobile is the least-supported capability: BetterHelp forbade it (as of 2021) and Kry/Livi's clinician tooling is web. Provider mobile apps concentrate on agenda, notifications and messaging.

## 3. Bundling education (courses, lectures) with care, and how store commission rules shape it

### Takeaway
Care platforms that offer classes (Maven, Peppy) put them in the same member app as booking, but those classes are included in a sponsored membership rather than sold individually. For a company that sells courses, Apple's rules draw a hard line: 1:1 real-time services may use the company's own payments, while one-to-many live sessions and unlockable digital content must use in-app purchase if sold inside the app.

### Cited Findings
- Maven's member app includes drop-in groups and on-demand classes (for example Infant CPR, Breastfeeding 101) alongside provider video appointments; membership is typically employer- or health-plan-sponsored [search summary] — [Maven enrolment page](https://www.mavenclinic.com/maven-enrollment/enroll-today)
- Peppy's single app includes live events and "structured courses and pathways" in video, audio and article formats alongside consultations; it is offered through employers and insurers at no extra cost to the user [search summary] — [Peppy services overview](https://peppy.health/services/overview); [Vitality: how to get the Peppy app](https://vitality.co.uk/support/health/menopause/how-to-get-the-peppy-app)
- Apple guideline 3.1.3(d), text as fetched on 11 Nov 2026: "If your app enables the purchase of real-time person-to-person services between two individuals (for example tutoring students, medical consultations, real estate tours, or fitness training), you may use purchase methods other than in-app purchase to collect those payments. One-to-few and one-to-many real-time services must use in-app purchase." [fetched] — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Apple guideline 3.1.1: "If you want to unlock features or functionality within your app, (by way of example: subscriptions, in-game currencies, game levels, access to premium content, or unlocking a full version), you must use in-app purchase." [fetched] — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Apple guideline 3.1.3(a) "Reader" apps: "Apps may allow a user to access previously purchased content or content subscriptions (specifically: magazines, newspapers, books, audio, music, and video)", and may offer free-tier account creation and account management for existing customers [fetched] — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Apple guideline 3.1.3(e): physical goods or services "consumed outside of the app" must use non-IAP payment methods [fetched] — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Apple staff reply on the developer forum (2023): asynchronous, message-based person-to-person services are not "real-time", so in-app purchase is required for them [search summary] — [Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/723322)
- Outside the United States storefront, apps "may not include buttons, external links, or other calls to action that direct customers to purchasing mechanisms other than in-app purchase" unless they hold a StoreKit External Purchase Link Entitlement available in specific regions [fetched] — [App Review Guidelines 3.1.1(a)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- EU link-out terms reported in mid-2025: developers in the EU may promote and link to external offers for digital goods, paying a 2% initial acquisition fee plus a 5% (Tier 1) or 13% (Tier 2) store services fee, and a 5% Core Technology Commission that replaces the earlier per-install Core Technology Fee [search summary] — [MacRumors, 22 Jul 2025](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/07/22/app-store-eu-changes-accepted/); [TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-apple-eu-fees-digital-markets-act/)
- Those EU terms remain contested: a September 2026 open letter from civil-society groups says Commission acceptance has been signalled only informally and argues Apple still charges up to 15% for steering, contrary to the DMA's free-of-charge requirement [search summary] — [ARTICLE 19 open letter, Sep 2026](https://www.article19.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Open-letter-Apples-new-EU-business-terms-FINAL.pdf)
- Course-platform branded apps route in-app course sales through store billing: Thinkific states "All in-app purchases will be charged a 30% fee by Apple and Google" (15% under Apple's small-business programme) and that courses bought on the web still appear in the app; Kajabi's Branded App creates an in-app purchase per "App Offer" that Apple must approve, with refunds handled by Apple/Google [search summary] — [Thinkific: Sell Products in the Mobile App](https://support.thinkific.com/hc/en-us/articles/16261125243159-Sell-Products-in-the-Mobile-App); [Thinkific: Apple IAP setup](https://support.thinkific.com/hc/en-us/articles/19158621261079-Branded-Mobile-Set-Up-In-App-Purchases-in-the-Apple-App-Store); [Kajabi Branded App FAQs](https://help.kajabi.com/articles/mobile-apps/branded-app/kajabi-branded-app-faqs)

### Inferences
- Eleva's core transaction (a paid 1:1 live video session with an expert) fits the 3.1.3(d) wording ("medical consultations", "fitness training") and can be charged with Eleva's existing payment processor inside a native iOS app with no Apple commission.
- Academy products fall on the other side of the line: live group lectures are "one-to-many real-time services" and recorded courses are unlockable content. Sold inside the iOS app, both need in-app purchase and Apple's commission. The pattern Thinkific documents (buy on the web, consume in the app) avoids that, at the cost of not being able to show a buy button or link in the EU without an entitlement and its fees.
- Putting 1:1 booking and paid courses in one iOS binary means one app review has to accept two different payment regimes side by side. This is an inference from the guideline text, not a documented rejection case; it is a concrete argument for keeping Academy purchase on the web even if course playback is in the app.
- Asynchronous paid messaging with an expert (if Eleva ever sells it) would need in-app purchase on iOS under Apple's forum guidance.

### Gaps
- Google Play's equivalent rules and current EU terms were not researched.
- Domestika, MasterClass, Headspace and Mindbody were not checked for how they handle course purchase in app versus web.
- No source was found giving results (revenue, completion, retention) from bundling courses into a care app versus a separate learning app.
- The Apple guidelines page showed no "last updated" date in the portion fetched, and the final 14K characters of the page were not read. The EU fee figures are from 2025 press coverage and a 2026 advocacy letter, not from an Apple or Commission primary document.

## 4. Bundling trackers and diaries with care

### Takeaway
Care apps commonly embed tracking (Hinge and Sword progress tracking, PhysiApp exercise and pain logs, Peppy's tracker, MyMulticare's symptom checker) rather than shipping it as a separate app, but I found no published feature-level usage data for secondary trackers inside all-in-one health apps. General research shows self-tracking engagement decays quickly and is hard to lift with notifications.

### Cited Findings
- Hinge Health keeps exercise guidance, care-team support and progress tracking in one member app [search summary] — [Hinge Health](https://www.hingehealth.com/how-it-works/experience)
- Sword's Bloom pelvic-health programme lives inside the main Sword app [listing found] — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/sword-health-ai-care/id1468523447)
- PhysiApp patients log exercise completion, sets, reps, pain and difficulty, and clinicians review session-level adherence in their dashboard, so the tracker's value is tied to the clinician reading it [search summary] — [Physitrack](https://www.physitrack.com/insights/hinge-health-vs-sword-health)
- Peppy lists a health tracker in its app alongside chat, consultations, events and courses, with limited availability in some markets [search summary] — [Peppy US information sheet (NVIDIA benefits)](https://images.nvidia.com/aem-dam/Solutions/benefits/documents/Peppy-US-Menopause-Information-Sheet.pdf)
- MyMulticare added a symptom checker to the insurer app in a 2021 update [listing found] — [MyMulticare](https://apps.apple.com/pt/app/mymulticare/id1507984984)
- A review of mHealth engagement reports that app-based symptom reporting completion ranges from 42% to 82% of expected data in studies [search summary; the mapping of this figure to this specific paper was not confirmed by opening it] — [PMC9171603](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9171603)
- A 2024 randomised trial of a remote symptom-tracking app for depression found that adding notifications and visual progress tracking did not increase objective or subjective engagement [search summary; paper mapping not confirmed] — [JMIR mHealth 2024, e44214](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2024/1/e44214/PDF)
- In a chronic-pain symptom-management app study, engagement peaked at 4.35 days in the first week and fell to 2.73 days by the final week [search summary] — [PMC8577401](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8577401/figure/fig0001)
- A digital mental health paper cites a 30-day retention rate of about 15% for top-performing health apps [search summary; exact source paper not confirmed] — [JMIR mHealth 2026, e78827](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2026/1/e78827/XML)
- Cautionary anecdote on unbundling: a commenter on the AVC thread said the Foursquare/Swarm split made her stop using both apps [fetched] — [AVC](https://avc.com/2015/11/mobile-apps-for-both-sides-of-the-market/)

### Inferences
- The observed pattern is "tracker inside the care app, read by the clinician". Trackers in these products are an input to a care relationship, not a standalone destination, which favours keeping a bladder diary reachable from the care app if the expert is meant to see the data.
- A bladder diary has a usage shape unlike the rest of Eleva: many short entries per day for a few days, often before or between sessions. That is the high-frequency, on-the-go pattern that Sharetribe's guidance treats as the strongest case for native (section 5), and it is stronger native justification than booking a session every week or two.
- Standalone and bundled are not mutually exclusive: a diary can be a module of the care app and also usable without an expert relationship. No source in this session measured which performs better.

### Gaps
- No data found on what share of users in an all-in-one health app use the secondary tracker, nor any telehealth company reporting tracker adoption. This is the key missing number for the diary decision.
- Flo, Elvie, Clue and other tracker-first women's-health apps that later added care or content were not researched, so the "tracker first, care later" route is undocumented here.
- The engagement statistics above come from search summaries; the papers were not opened and the figures should be verified before being quoted.

## 5. Sequencing for small teams: what to ship natively first, what to leave on the web

### Takeaway
Current marketplace guidance (Sharetribe, 2026) is web-first, adding native for one side only after validation, and never building both sides' apps at once; frequency of return and on-the-go use are the stated triggers for native. Comparable platforms that do serve providers on mobile give them a thin companion (agenda, notifications, messaging) and leave records, finances and administration on web or desktop.

### Cited Findings
- Sharetribe (Juho Makkonen; published 4 Feb 2026, updated 18 Sep 2026): "start web-first unless your users are on the go"; add a native app "after you've validated your concept and reached initial liquidity"; test demand with a no-code wrapper before a custom build; do not launch website and native app simultaneously [fetched] — [Sharetribe Academy](https://www.sharetribe.com/academy/marketplace-mobile-app-why-when-how/)
- Same article on maintenance: "Every new feature must work on web and mobile and pass both app store reviews", and ongoing maintenance often matters more than launch price [fetched] — [Sharetribe Academy](https://www.sharetribe.com/academy/marketplace-mobile-app-why-when-how/)
- Same article on when mobile-first is right: users on the move, fast photo listing, frequent returns ("weekly, daily, or multiple times per day"), or mostly phone users; web-first suits detailed, high-stakes, desktop-heavy tasks (Upwork, Zillow, most B2B) [fetched / search summary] — [Sharetribe Academy](https://www.sharetribe.com/academy/marketplace-mobile-app-why-when-how/)
- Same article on discovery: keep a website because native apps "add little to search engine optimization (SEO)" and are mostly invisible to AI assistants [fetched] — [Sharetribe Academy](https://www.sharetribe.com/academy/marketplace-mobile-app-why-when-how/)
- Same article, reported outcomes: one Sharetribe customer added an app after years web-only and "bookings rose significantly"; another pivoted to mobile and dropped its web version. Vestiaire Collective and Grailed "were originally launched web-first" [fetched] — [Sharetribe Academy](https://www.sharetribe.com/academy/marketplace-mobile-app-why-when-how/)
- Same article, cost benchmarks: wrapper about $50/month; template app from $199/month plus $249 setup; custom app on an existing API $10k to $20k; custom app from scratch at least $30k to $50k [fetched] — [Sharetribe Academy](https://www.sharetribe.com/academy/marketplace-mobile-app-why-when-how/)
- Sharetribe help centre: "for many early-stage marketplaces, a responsive website is enough" [search summary] — [Sharetribe help](https://www.sharetribe.com/help/en/articles/12166610-how-to-build-a-mobile-app)
- Provider-side precedent for a thin mobile surface: BetterHelp therapists message and manage schedules on phone, video on computer [fetched] — [BetterHelp counselor help](https://counselor-onboarding.helpscoutdocs.com/article/403-can-i-use-my-phone-or-tablet-for-betterhelp)
- Provider-side precedent: Doctolib Pro mobile centres on agenda, real-time booking notifications and messaging; a reviewer notes patient file and history are not available in the app [listing found] — [App Store FR](https://apps.apple.com/fr/app/doctolib-pro-agenda-praticien/id1107542306)
- Provider-side precedent: Booksy keeps shifts, inventory, reporting and full POS in a separate tablet/web tier rather than the phone app [search summary] — [Selfnamed](https://www.blog.selfnamed.com/business/top-salon-booking-apps)
- Push and retention (vendor data, old): Airship's 2017 study of 63 million new app users found those who received one or more push notifications in their first 90 days had 190% higher average retention than those who received none [search summary] — [Airship](https://www.airship.com/blog/7-mobile-engagement-statistics-that-show-how-push-notifications-boost-roi/)
- Reminders and no-shows (SMS, not push): an observational study found 11.2% non-attendance with an SMS reminder versus 18.1% without, a 38% lower likelihood of non-attendance [search summary] — [BMC Ophthalmology 2008](https://bmcophthalmol.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/1471-2415-8-9/tables/1); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18513438/)
- Behaviourally worded SMS reminders cut missed hospital appointments by more than a third in a government behavioural-insights trial [search summary] — [NSW Behavioural Insights Unit](https://www.nsw.gov.au/departments-and-agencies/behavioural-insights-unit/blog/using-reminders-to-cut-missed-hospital-appointments)
- App versus mobile-web conversion (vendor data, old, e-commerce not booking): Criteo's H1 2016 report is cited as showing apps convert about three times as many shoppers as mobile web and retain new users about twice as well; MobiLoud (an app-builder vendor) claims 1.7 to 3x higher conversion in apps [search summary] — [Retail Dive](https://www.retaildive.com/ex/mobilecommercedaily/sophisticated-retailer-apps-see-54pc-of-transactions-occur-in-app-report); [MobiLoud](https://www.mobiloud.com/blog/mobile-apps-vs-mobile-websites)
- Browser join is a deliberate product choice for low-frequency video: Zoom built a mobile browser client for patients who connect once or twice a year; a KLAS report notes providers value link-based joining without an app and that app-download confusion is a concern [search summary] — [Zoom blog](https://www.zoom.com/en/blog/mobile-browser-client-healthcare); [KLAS](https://klasresearch.com/report/telehealth-performance-2022-what-is-the-state-of-telehealth-post-pandemic/1917)

### Inferences
- Applying the Sharetribe triggers to Eleva: member booking every week or two is borderline on frequency; the bladder diary (multiple entries a day) and session-day actions (reminder, join, message) clearly meet the on-the-go test; expert CRM, finances, service setup and clinic ("Teams") administration are the desktop-heavy, high-stakes tasks that the guidance leaves on the web.
- The reminder evidence is for SMS, which Eleva can send without a native app. The no-show argument therefore does not by itself justify native; push adds a free, richer channel but the measured effect in the literature found here is SMS.
- The conversion evidence is weak for Eleva's purposes: it is retail, eight to ten years old, vendor-sourced, and compares self-selected app users with web visitors. It should not be used to forecast booking conversion.
- A defensible order implied by the comparables: (1) member app with reminders, join-session, messaging, diary; (2) expert companion functions (agenda, new-booking notifications, messaging, join-session) either as a role-gated area of the same app or a later thin app; (3) everything else stays responsive web.

### Gaps
- No PWA-specific evidence was found; the Sharetribe article does not mention PWAs.
- No push-notification-specific study on no-show rates or rebooking was found, and nothing current from Airship, OneSignal or Adjust was retrieved.
- No mobile-web versus native conversion data specific to appointment or session booking was found.
- No founder or mobile-lead interview from a comparable telehealth marketplace describing its native build order was found. The a16z, Lenny's Newsletter, NFX and Point Nine sources suggested in the brief were not reached.
- No evidence gathered on native Swift plus Kotlin versus cross-platform cost for a small team; that sits outside this note's scope but affects the sequencing.

## 6. Usage patterns: native value for weekly or fortnightly 1:1 sessions, and device mix for sessions

### Takeaway
Published device-mix data, mostly from US health systems and several years old, shows the majority of patient video visits happen on phones (58% to 74%), which supports a good phone join experience for members. I found no session-device data from therapy or tutoring marketplaces, and the comparables suggest providers lean to computers for delivering sessions.

### Cited Findings
- Kaiser Permanente, 2015 to 2017, across 210,383 video visits: 74% on a smartphone, 20% on desktop, 6% on tablet [search summary; the specific page carrying this figure was not opened, most likely one of the two linked] — [Ro research note](https://ro.co/research/smartphones-bridge-broadband-gap/); [Digital Commerce 360](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/?p=827956)
- A Penn Medicine study of 55,812 telemedicine patients: 41.6% used a desktop or laptop and 58.4% used a telephone or tablet (smartphones not separated) [search summary; paper mapping not confirmed] — [PMC9440396](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9440396)
- American Well reported that 60 percent of its video visits were on mobile, per a MobiHealthNews headline; the article returned HTTP 403 so date and detail are unverified — [MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/american-well-60-percent-video-visits-are-mobile)
- Patient preference surveys: one found 45% wanted to use a phone and 39% a laptop for telehealth; another found over 59% preferred mobile devices and 38% computers [search summary] — [mHealthIntelligence](https://mhealthintelligence.com/news/79-of-patients-want-call-text-to-signal-start-of-telehealth-visit); [AARP](https://www.aarp.org/health/conditions-treatments/connecting-virtual-doctor-visits/)
- Providers: BetterHelp required therapists to run video from a computer "in a private, quiet setting" (as of July 2021) [fetched] — [BetterHelp counselor help](https://counselor-onboarding.helpscoutdocs.com/article/403-can-i-use-my-phone-or-tablet-for-betterhelp)
- Member-side session flow in a comparable app is short: Maven members open Appointments and tap Launch up to 5 minutes before; the provider's summary note then appears under Appointments and in Messages [search summary] — [Maven support](https://support.mavenclinic.com/hc/en-us/articles/34984852264723-How-do-I-launch-my-appointment-How-do-I-prepare-for-my-appointment)
- Preply positions its app as the place to "book and attend lessons from anywhere, right from your phone", with messaging and lesson management [listing found / search summary] — [Preply app page](https://preply.com/en/app)
- Sharetribe's frequency heuristic: weekly or more frequent returns make mobile-first "a strong fit" [search summary] — [Sharetribe Academy](https://www.sharetribe.com/academy/marketplace-mobile-app-why-when-how/)
- Reviews of the BetterHelp app mention lag and glitches in mobile video sessions [search summary] — [WhistleOut review](https://www.whistleout.com/CellPhones/Apps/betterhelp-app-review)

### Inferences
- For a member with a session every one to two weeks, the app's recurring jobs in the comparables are: get reminded, join the call, read the follow-up note, message, rebook. That is a narrow surface. Browsing experts and first booking can stay on the web (where SEO and shareable links work) without weakening the app.
- Pelvic physiotherapy sessions may involve the member moving, lying down or demonstrating exercises, which plausibly favours a phone or tablet that can be propped up over a laptop. This is an inference with no source; it would be worth checking against Eleva's own session device logs.
- Experts delivering back-to-back sessions and writing session records are likely on a computer, consistent with BetterHelp's rule and Kry/Livi's web tooling, so native video for experts is a lower priority than native video for members.

### Gaps
- No share-of-sessions-by-device data from BetterHelp, Talkspace, Preply, italki, Maven, Doctolib, Kry or any European telehealth company was found.
- All device-mix data found is US, health-system based and dated 2015 to roughly 2022; none is Iberian.
- No vendor data (Daily, Twilio, Doxy.me, Zoom) on browser versus native join rates or call quality was found.
- Eleva's own web analytics (device split for bookings and for session joins) would answer this more reliably than any external source and were not available to this research.

## 7. Costs of a multi-role app

### Takeaway
Documented costs of combining roles are mostly qualitative: role-switch friction (Airbnb hosts), combined builds that "got really messed up" and were unwound (founder anecdotes), and single-session constraints (BetterHelp). Documented costs of splitting are also real: confusion over which app to install or review (Thumbtack, Etsy), under-investment in the supply-side app, double maintenance and double store review, and user loss when an app is split (Foursquare/Swarm anecdote).

### Cited Findings

**Costs of combining**
- Airbnb hosts reported the host/guest inbox switch going from one click to "at least 4 clicks" after a redesign, and sometimes no visible way back to hosting mode [search summary] — [Airbnb Community](https://community.withairbnb.com/t5/Community-Cafe/New-interface-is-awkward-to-terrible/td-p/1327358)
- Fred Wilson, on combined apps he had seen: "it doesn't work that well" [fetched] — [AVC](https://avc.com/2015/11/mobile-apps-for-both-sides-of-the-market/)
- Drew Meyers (hospitality marketplace, both roles in one app) said it is hard to avoid adding complexity; Ish Jindal's combined version "got really messed up" and supply was moved to desktop; Michael Brill's team found only one side needed the app [fetched] — [AVC](https://avc.com/2015/11/mobile-apps-for-both-sides-of-the-market/)
- BetterHelp therapists can be logged in on only one device at a time and must log out of the app before starting a session on the computer [fetched] — [BetterHelp counselor help](https://counselor-onboarding.helpscoutdocs.com/article/403-can-i-use-my-phone-or-tablet-for-betterhelp)
- App size reference point: Maven's provider-only app, with video, scheduling, notes and messaging, is 171.5 MB on iOS [fetched] — [MPractice](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/mpractice/id942538862)

**Costs of splitting**
- Thumbtack's developer reply on its customer app tells a reviewer to leave the review on the Pro app, showing users post to the wrong listing [search summary] — [Thumbtack customer app listing](https://apps.apple.com/app/852703300)
- Etsy sellers were still on the retired seller app a year after its removal and could not update [search summary] — [EcommerceBytes](https://www.ecommercebytes.com/2024/06/08/what-to-do-if-you-cant-update-the-etsy-seller-app/)
- Multicare maintains two listings (MyMulticare and Multicare Medicina Online); a 2023 MyMulticare review complained it was impossible to request an online medicine consultation from that app [listing found / search summary] — [MyMulticare](https://apps.apple.com/pt/app/mymulticare/id1507984984); [Multicare Medicina Online](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/multicare-medicina-online/id1291442289)
- Supply-side apps tend to get less staffing and product attention, and suppliers may leave if under-served (Anshumani Ruddra, Joe Cardillo, Vasudev Ram) [fetched] — [AVC](https://avc.com/2015/11/mobile-apps-for-both-sides-of-the-market/)
- Splitting an app can lose users outright: the Foursquare/Swarm split made one commenter stop using both [fetched] — [AVC](https://avc.com/2015/11/mobile-apps-for-both-sides-of-the-market/)
- Each additional native surface multiplies release work: "Every new feature must work on web and mobile and pass both app store reviews" [fetched] — [Sharetribe Academy](https://www.sharetribe.com/academy/marketplace-mobile-app-why-when-how/)
- Small-audience provider apps earn few ratings: MPractice has 6 ratings; Treatwell Pro has 185 on the Spanish store, versus thousands for consumer apps [fetched / listing found] — [MPractice](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/mpractice/id942538862); [Treatwell Pro](https://apps.apple.com/es/app/treatwell-pro-for-business/id1582612088?l=en-GB)

**Review risk**
- Apple's 1:1 payment exemption is narrow ("between two individuals"; "One-to-few and one-to-many real-time services must use in-app purchase"), and a rejection notice quoted in a third-party write-up applied exactly that distinction to a group-service app [fetched / search summary] — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/); [Rejection notice text](https://rentry.co/zhasq)

### Inferences
- With two platforms written natively (Swift and Kotlin), every extra app is two extra codebases. Four codebases (member and expert on each platform) for a founder-led team is the heaviest option on the table; the comparables that run that structure (Maven, Doctolib, Booksy) are far larger companies.
- A role-gated single app carries a specific Eleva risk the sources hint at: the more that lives in one binary (1:1 sessions, group lectures, paid courses, expert finances), the more one review rejection or one payment-rule dispute blocks releases for every audience.
- Splitting the diary into its own app has an asymmetric profile: it gives a clean, low-risk store listing and its own search presence, but the Foursquare/Swarm and Multicare examples show users do not reliably follow a product across two apps.

### Gaps
- No quantified data found on engineering cost, crash rates, app size or review rejection rates for multi-role versus single-role apps.
- No documented case found of a health marketplace regretting or reversing a combined customer-plus-provider app.
- No evidence gathered on permission-model complexity for three roles (member, expert, clinic admin) in one mobile app; all three-role examples found (for example Doctolib's secretariat planning) keep the admin role on desktop.
- Several founder anecdotes are from a 2015 comment thread and reflect that era's tooling.

- No 2026 data found on health-app usage habits in Portugal or Spain (share of adults using health apps, telehealth adoption, willingness to pay).
- Ratings for My CUF, Lusíadas, SNS 24 and the Doctoralia Portugal patient app were not retrieved; iFeel and Top Doctors (Spain) were not researched.
- Statcounter's monthly figures for Portugal swing by about 9 points across 2026 (62% to 71% Android), so any single month is noisy; no alternative source (for example Kantar or operator data) was checked.
- No device-share data segmented by gender, age or income for Portugal was found, which matters for a women's-health audience.

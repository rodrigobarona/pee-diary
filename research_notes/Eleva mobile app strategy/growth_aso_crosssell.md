# Growth mechanics: free standalone tracker (Eleva Diary + Eleva Care) vs one all-in-one Eleva Care app — ASO, cross-promotion, conversion, brand architecture

Research date: 11 November 2026. Option A = free standalone "Eleva Diary" plus a separate Eleva Care app. Option B = one Eleva Care app with the diary inside.

Evidence labels used on every finding:
- **[OFFICIAL]** platform documentation or regulatory filing
- **[MEASURED]** peer-reviewed study, company filing, or a benchmark with a stated sample
- **[VENDOR]** number published by a company selling a related product (ASO tool, MMP, ad-tech); directional only
- **[OPINION]** practitioner commentary without data
- **[SECONDARY-UNVERIFIED]** figure seen only in a compilation or search snippet; I could not open the primary

Read this first: most hard numbers are US or global. EU, Portugal and Spain data is thin and is called out wherever it exists. I found **no** published measurement of the exact thing the founder is asking about (cross-install or paid-conversion rate from a free health tracker app into the same publisher's separate paid-care app). Both halves of the hypothesis therefore rest on adjacent evidence plus inference, and the notes say so in each Gaps section.

---

## 1. ASO: how the stores rank, whether a focused app beats a broad one for a niche keyword, what custom pages can and cannot do, and the share of installs from search

### Takeaway
The stores rank on text relevance (title weighted most) plus behaviour (downloads, ratings, conversion, and on Google Play engagement/quality), so an app whose title literally says "bladder diary" has a structural relevance and tap-through advantage for that query; but I found no controlled measurement of "focused vs broad app" ranking, only mechanism and vendor reasoning. Since July 2025 Apple's custom product pages and Google's keyword-targeted custom store listings let one broad app show a diary-specific page for diary searches, which narrows (not closes) the gap: on iOS the name, subtitle and icon stay fixed and the keywords must already sit in the 100-character keyword field.

### Cited Findings

**How Apple ranks (global, current doc as of Nov 2026)**
- [OFFICIAL] Apple: "Search results are based on a number of factors, including text relevance (matches for your app's title, subtitle, keywords, and primary category), as well as user behavior (downloads, ratings and reviews, and more)." Keyword field is "limited to 100 characters total". Promotional text "doesn't affect your app's search ranking". Ratings and reviews "can influence how your app ranks in App Store search". — [Apple Developer, Search](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/search/)
- [OFFICIAL] Apple's long description is not listed among indexed fields on that page; only title, subtitle, keywords and category are named. — [Apple Developer, Search](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/search/)

**How Google Play ranks (global, current doc as of Nov 2026)**
- [OFFICIAL] Google Play help states search results prioritise relevance, engagement and quality, with the balance shifting by type of search; ranking guidance names "user relevance" and "editorial value", and says Google "may factor in other signals, such as app quality or how users respond to results for a given query". — [Play Console Help, Get discovered on Google Play search](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/4448378?hl=en); [Play Console Help, App discovery and ranking](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9958766); [Google Play Help, Understand how search works](https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/11906913?hl=en)
- [VENDOR] ASO vendors report that Google indexes title, short description (80 characters) and full description (up to 4,000 characters), that the title is the strongest text field, and that retention and uninstall rate feed ranking. Weights are not published by Google and the vendors disagree on them. — [AppTweak, Google Play ranking factors](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/google-play-ranking-factors?lang=en-US); [AppRadar](https://appradar.com/academy/google-play-ranking-factors); [ASOMobile](https://asomobile.net/en/blog/app-store-and-google-play-ranking-factors/)

**Custom product pages (Apple) — what one broad app can do**
- [OFFICIAL] "You can publish up to 70 additional versions of your product page." Each can "vary the screenshots, promotional text, and/or app previews". Keywords can be assigned so that "your custom product page [appears] in search results for those selected keywords, rather than your default product page"; "each keyword combination is unique to a single product page". Each page can carry a deep link into a specific in-app destination (iOS 18 / iPadOS 18 or later). Page metadata goes through review but independently of an app update. — [Apple Developer, Custom product pages](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/custom-product-pages/)
- [VENDOR] ASO vendors date the organic-search keyword assignment to 30 July 2025 and the limit increase from 35 to 70 pages to 29 October 2025, and state that assignable keywords must come from the app's keyword field (not from words in the title or subtitle). — [MobileAction](https://www.mobileaction.co/blog/apple-doubles-the-custom-product-page-limit/); [AppTweak, CPP framework](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/cpp-framework?lang=en-US); [Stora, 70-keyword-linking guide (May 2026)](https://stora.sh/blog/2026-05-13-custom-product-pages-70-keyword-linking-aso-guide)
- [OFFICIAL] Limit by omission: Apple's page lists only screenshots, promotional text and app previews as variable. It does not offer per-page app name, subtitle or icon. — [Apple Developer, Custom product pages](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/custom-product-pages/)

**Custom store listings (Google Play)**
- [OFFICIAL] Google's Play Console page says developers can create up to 50 custom store listings "tailored by country, user or buyer state, or search keyword". — [Google Play Console, Store listings](https://play.google.com/console/about/storelistings/?hl=id)
- [VENDOR] Keyword-targeted listings were added in 2025; the listing is shown only when the query matches the defined keywords; Play Console suggests keywords based on where the app already has traction; targeting a single term gives little traffic; listings are not auto-translated. — [MobileAction](https://www.mobileaction.co/blog/custom-store-listings-on-google-play/); [ASOMobile](https://asomobile.net/en/blog/custom-store-listing-in-google-play/); [AppTweak](https://apptweak.com/aso-blog/what-are-custom-store-listings-and-how-to-use-them)
- Conflict: one vendor says each custom listing can have its own app name, descriptions, icon and graphics; another says the icon, category and in-app purchases are global and cannot change per listing. I did not resolve this against Play Console. — [MobileAction](https://www.mobileaction.co/blog/custom-store-listings-on-google-play/); contradicted by [Gummicube](https://www.gummicube.com/blog/what-are-google-custom-store-listings)

**In-app events (Apple) and promotional content (Google)**
- [OFFICIAL] "In-App Events appear in search results on the App Store on iOS and iPadOS next to your app." People who have downloaded the app before see the event card; people who never downloaded it see screenshots or previews instead. — [Apple Developer, Search](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/search/)
- [VENDOR] Vendors disagree on the cap (5 published at once vs 10 published with 15 approved) and claim event name (30 chars) and short description (50 chars) are keyword-indexed; not confirmed against Apple documentation. — [MobileAction guide](https://www.mobileaction.co/guide/in-app-events-promotional-content-guide); [Phiture](https://phiture.com/mobilegrowthstack/in-app-events-how-to-get-approved/)
- [OFFICIAL] Google promotional content is available to all games and only to apps "that meet our eligibility criteria"; content must run in-app at the same time as the event. — [Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12929944)

**Share of installs that come from store search**
- [VENDOR/OFFICIAL-marketing] The widely quoted figure is Apple's own: about 65% of App Store downloads occur directly after a search (and about 70% of visitors use search). It originates in Apple Search Ads marketing material and is several years old; 2025-2026 pages restate it without new measurement. — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/apple-expands-app-store-search-ads-reach-high-intent-users); [DEV Community roundup (2026)](https://dev.to/rishbz/app-store-keyword-ranking-statistics-why-rankings-decide-your-downloads-2026-omc)
- [OFFICIAL, MEASURED] Apple's 2024 App Store Transparency Report (global, calendar 2024, published May 2025): 813,110,348 average weekly visitors; 440,896,463 average weekly customer accounts using search; 839,266,915 average weekly downloads; 1,898,240,007 average weekly redownloads; 169,235 unique apps appeared in the top 10 results for at least 1,000 searches per week. — [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/05/30/app-store-2024-transparency-report/); [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2025/05/30/apple-shares-full-app-store-transparency-report-for-2024/)
- [MEASURED, old] Health-specific discovery survey (US, NYU Langone, June 2015): among people with a health app, 35% found it by searching the app store, 30% via friends and family, 18% via web search. — [MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/survey-58-percent-smartphone-users-have-downloaded-fitness-or-health-app)
- [MEASURED, old] Google/Ipsos (2015, cross-category): app awareness sources were 52% friends/family/colleagues, 40% browsing the app store, 27% search engines. TUNE (2014): roughly half of users found their last app through app-store search. — [Think with Google PDF](https://thinkwithgoogle.com/_qs/documents/2922/mobile-app-marketing-insights_5.pdf); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/10/03/roughly-half-of-users-are-finding-apps-via-app-store-search-says-study)

**Store page conversion benchmarks**
- [VENDOR, stated sample] AppTweak, US, calendar 2025 (published 18 May 2026): average App Store page-view-to-install 8.56%; Google Play 16.15%; App Store impression-to-install 3.8%; a 7.8% impression-to-install figure is attributed to Medical in the body text but to Navigation in the summary (internal inconsistency in the source). No Health & Fitness figure is given. — [AppTweak](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/average-app-conversion-rate-per-category)
- [VENDOR, stated sample] Apple Ads search-results campaigns, US, 2025, ~2,800 advertisers: brand-keyword campaigns convert at a median 73.5% tap-to-install with 25.3% tap-through, versus 59.8% and 8.5% for generic keywords; median cost per install $2.84 brand vs $5.31 generic. — [AppTweak, Apple Ads benchmarks (Aug 2026)](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/apple-ads-benchmarks)

**The niche itself: bladder diary apps**
- [MEASURED, store listings] The category is tiny by rating volume. Bladderly, a prominent free bladder diary app, shows a 4.5 average from 191 ratings on the US App Store; UroBladderDiary shows 2.9 from 7 ratings. — [AppFollow, Bladderly (US)](https://apps.appfollow.io/ios/bladderly-smart-bladder-diary/1523268654?country=us); [App Store, UroBladderDiary](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/urobladderdiary/id562587825)
- [MEASURED] Academic reviews of bladder/voiding diary apps exist (ICS 2024 framework analysis of English-language apps; a Brazilian descriptive analysis of free voiding diary apps on both stores), indicating a fragmented field of small apps. — [ICS 2024 abstract 14](https://ics.org/2024/abstract/14); [UroToday](https://urotoday.com/recent-abstracts/pelvic-health-reconstruction/urinary-incontinence/122548-content-and-functionality-features-of-voiding-diary-applications-for-mobile-devices-in-brazil-a-descriptive-analysis.html)
- [MEASURED] Portuguese-language supply is mostly Brazilian: "Minha Bexiga" (App Store), a 2017 Brazilian physiotherapy journal list of diary apps ("Miccional Diary", "EduMicc", "Diario micc", etc.), and a university project URINAPP. A registered trial (NCT07602413) is developing a Portuguese mobile version of the ICIQ bladder diary with completion rate and format preference as outcomes; I could not retrieve its country or sponsor. — [App Store, Minha Bexiga](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1537991229); [Revista Pesquisa em Fisioterapia](https://www5.bahiana.edu.br/index.php/fisioterapia/article/view/1263/805); [UFC repository, URINAPP](https://repositorio.ufc.br/handle/riufc/56037?mode=full); [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07602413](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07602413)

### Inferences
- From Apple's own transparency numbers, about 54% of weekly App Store visitors use search (440.9M / 813.1M, my calculation). That is a share of visitors, not of downloads, and is lower than the marketing "70% of visitors" line. The safest phrasing for the judge panel: "search is the single largest discovery surface, somewhere between half and two-thirds, and Apple's 65% figure is old marketing data".
- Mechanism favours a focused app for the exact query: the title is the heaviest text field on both stores, iOS gives only a title and a subtitle (30 characters each is the long-standing limit; not re-verified this session), and a broad Eleva Care app has to spend those characters on booking/experts/pelvic health. A standalone app can put "Bladder Diary" / "Diário Miccional" in the title in every locale. It also wins on search-result tap-through because the icon, name and first screenshots all match the query. This is reasoning from documented ranking factors, not a measured A/B.
- Since July 2025 the gap is smaller than the founder's hypothesis assumes. Option B can assign "diário miccional", "bladder diary", "diario miccional" from its keyword field to a diary-specific custom product page with diary screenshots and a deep link straight into the diary; on Google Play it can go further with a keyword-targeted custom listing (possibly with its own name and description). What Option B cannot do on iOS: change the app name, subtitle or icon the searcher sees in the result row, or rank on title-weight for the diary term.
- The brand-vs-generic Apple Ads split (73.5% vs 59.8% conversion; 25.3% vs 8.5% tap-through) shows how much relevance between query and listing matters. A searcher typing "bladder diary" who sees "Eleva Care: pelvic health experts" is a generic-intent impression; the same searcher seeing "Eleva Diary: Bladder Diary" is close to an exact match.
- The niche is small. If the best-known English-language bladder diary app has under 200 US ratings, absolute keyword volume in Portugal (about 10 million people) will be very low. A standalone app can plausibly rank top-3 for "diário miccional" in pt-PT with little competition, but the prize is likely tens to low hundreds of installs per month, not thousands. Discovery benefit is real in rank terms and modest in volume terms.
- In-app events are a re-engagement surface (event cards show to past downloaders), useful to Option B for bringing lapsed diary users back, not for first discovery.

### Gaps
- No controlled study or vendor dataset comparing ranking or conversion of a single-purpose app versus a multi-feature app on the same niche keyword. All "focused apps rank better" statements I found are reasoning from ranking factors.
- No keyword volume or difficulty data for "diário miccional", "diario miccional", "bladder diary", "diário da bexiga" in the PT, ES or BR stores. This needs an ASO tool (AppTweak, Sensor Tower, Appfigures) or Apple Search Ads popularity scores and is the single most decision-relevant missing number.
- Apple's title/subtitle 30-character limits and Google's 30/80/4,000 limits were not re-verified against official pages in this session.
- I could not confirm from Google documentation whether a custom store listing can change the icon.
- No post-launch data yet on how much organic traffic keyword-assigned custom product pages actually capture; all commentary is from vendors.

## 2. Cross-promotion between a publisher's own apps: mechanisms and measured cross-install or cross-sell rates

### Takeaway
The mechanisms are mature and free (in-app store sheets, deep links, smart banners, "more by this developer"), but there is no public benchmark for what share of a free companion app's users install or pay for the publisher's main product. The best adjacent numbers are vendor-reported and suggest that every extra app-install hop loses roughly half or more of the users who tap, which is the measurable cost of Option A.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL, not re-fetched this session] Apple provides SKStoreProductViewController (a full in-app App Store product sheet) and SKOverlay (a dismissible banner that lets users install another app without leaving the current one). — [Apple, SKStoreProductViewController](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/skstoreproductviewcontroller); [Apple, SKOverlay](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/skoverlay)
- [VENDOR] Ad-tech vendors report SKOverlay lifts ad conversion "by up to 30%" depending on category, and that it helps only in some placements. These are ad-network contexts, not owned cross-promo. — [Mapendo](https://mapendo.co/blog/skoverlay); [Jampp](https://jampp.com/blog/how-skoverlay-can-boost-skan-conversions-with-just-one-click); [Smadex](https://smadex.com/elevate-your-ios-performance-with-skoverlay/)
- [VENDOR] AppsFlyer's cross-promotion attribution documentation says installs and conversion rate for cross-promotion campaigns are "much higher" than other media sources and shows one illustrative campaign converting at 7.92% (impression or click to install; a single example, not a benchmark). — [AppsFlyer Help, Cross-promotion attribution](https://support.appsflyer.com/hc/en-us/articles/115004481946)
- [VENDOR/OPINION] Guidance is to measure cross-promo installs as their own campaign and judge them on incremental installs, not raw taps. — [Sonar](https://trysonar.app/blog/app-cross-promotion)
- [MEASURED, games] An academic study of top iOS games found cross-promotion effectiveness depends on audience overlap between the two apps and on whether the source app is growing or declining. — [AIS eLibrary, SAIS 2026](https://aisel.aisnet.org/sais2026/19)
- [VENDOR, 2016, one e-commerce company, 10 days] Branch: users who installed from a smart banner had a 2.6x higher in-app purchase rate than general installers and 5.65x the purchase rate of mobile-web users; banner-referred sessions were 8% of sessions but 20% of purchase revenue. No click-through or install rate is reported. — [Branch](https://www.branch.io/resources/blog/10-stats-on-the-effectiveness-of-smart-banners/)
- [VENDOR] Web-to-app funnels: a native smart banner alone converts about 1-3% of mobile web visitors; of people who tap, about 40-60% complete install and open; "full programs" reach 8-15%. Sources sell deep-linking products and advise comparing only against your own baseline. — [Linkrunner](https://linkrunner.io/blog/deep-linking-for-web-to-app-how-to-convert-mobile-web-visitors-into-app-users); [U2L](https://u2l.ai/blog/web-to-app-conversion)
- [VENDOR] AppsFlyer case study (FOX): custom smart banners produced 15-35% more installs than a generic banner and 10x banner click-through in A/B tests. — [AppsFlyer customer story](https://www.appsflyer.com/zh-hans/customers/fox/)
- [VENDOR] Deferred deep linking: if the user lacks the app they are sent to the store with link parameters stored and replayed on first open; mismatch between the promise and the store listing causes high drop-off. — [Linkrunner](https://linkrunner.io/blog/deep-linking-for-web-to-app-how-to-convert-mobile-web-visitors-into-app-users)
- [OFFICIAL] Custom product pages can carry a deep link to a specific in-app destination (iOS 18+), which lets a cross-promo tap land on a tailored page and then in the right screen. — [Apple Developer, Custom product pages](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/custom-product-pages/)
- [OPINION, 2014-2015 era] App "constellation"/unbundling commentary: Facebook Messenger is cited as the only unbundled app that clearly succeeded, because the feature was already heavily used and Facebook pushed installs hard from the main app; Foursquare's Swarm split caused backlash and confusion; forced migrations produced spikes of negative reviews; most constellation apps were not promoted and failed. — [Andrew Chen guest post](https://andrewchen.com/why-arent-app-constellations-working-guest-post/?amp); [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/why-breaking-up-is-hard-to-do-for-social-apps-like-facebook-foursquare-and-linkedin); [CIO](https://www.cio.com/article/246432/social-messaging-apps-struggle-with-decision-to-bundle-or-unbundle.html)
- [OFFICIAL company release] Consolidation counter-example: Duolingo moved its standalone Math app and new Music course into the flagship app on 9 November 2023 so users "learn more subjects in one place", with one streak shared across subjects. — [Duolingo investor relations](https://investors.duolingo.com/news-releases/news-release-details/duolingo-launches-music-and-math-its-flagship-app); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/09/06/duolingo-confirms-its-app-will-soon-include-both-math-and-music-lessons/)

### Inferences
- Option A adds a second install (or at least a second surface) between "I logged my diary" and "I booked a session". Using the only available adjacent benchmarks, a warm, well-matched hop loses about 40-60% of tappers at the install-and-open step (vendor web-to-app data) and 25% even under near-ideal brand-intent conditions (Apple Ads brand campaigns convert about 73.5% tap-to-install). Option B has no such step.
- That loss is avoidable in Option A if the paid action does not require the second app: a booking can open eleva.care in an in-app browser or via a universal link, with the diary export attached. In that design the second app becomes a retention tool for people who already booked, not a gate in front of the first booking. This design choice changes the cross-sell comparison more than the one-app-vs-two-apps choice itself.
- The unbundling record says a second app works when (a) the split-off behaviour is distinct and already wanted on its own, and (b) the publisher actively drives traffic between apps. A bladder diary found by people who do not know Eleva satisfies (a). It is the reverse direction of the classic case (small app feeding a big one), for which I found no measured precedent.
- Duolingo's consolidation shows the direction large consumer publishers moved when the goal was engagement and cross-use among existing users. It says nothing about top-of-funnel discovery by strangers, which is the founder's argument for the standalone app.
- "More by this developer" and developer-name search are passive and low-volume; they should not be counted on for cross-sell.

### Gaps
- No published benchmark for the share of free companion-app users who install or pay for the same publisher's main product, in health or any other non-game category. AppsFlyer, Adjust and Branch publish mechanics and case studies, not portfolio cross-install rates.
- No measured SKOverlay or SKStoreProductViewController conversion rate for owned (non-ad) cross-promotion.
- I did not find the reasons or results for other health consolidations (Ovia merging its apps, Headspace folding Headspace Care into one app); a targeted search returned nothing usable.
- The Messenger "84% one-star ratings" figure seen in a blog was not verified and is excluded from findings.

## 3. Conversion benchmarks: free tool or tracker to paid service

### Takeaway
Reported free-to-paid conversion clusters in low single digits for freemium apps (about 2% of downloads; about 7-8% of monthly actives at Flo's scale), while intent-heavy health tools that lead directly to a clinician report far higher hand-off rates (25-31% in company-reported figures). No company I checked publishes a conversion rate from a free tracker to paid 1:1 care specifically.

### Cited Findings
- [MEASURED, vendor dataset] RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2025 (75,000 apps, over $10B tracked revenue, global): median download-to-paid conversion is 2.18% for freemium apps versus 12.11% for hard-paywall apps; median download-to-trial is 6.2% (top decile 20.3%); Health & Fitness median trial-to-paid is 39.9% (top decile 68.3%); Health & Fitness top-decile download-to-paid is 12.1%; 67% of Health & Fitness subscriptions are yearly; Western Europe has refund rates under 3% and third-renewal rates above 79%. — [RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025/)
- [VENDOR] Adapty reports Health & Fitness trial-to-paid at about 62%, a different sample and method from RevenueCat's 39.9%. — [Adapty, State of in-app subscriptions 2026](https://adapty.io/state-of-in-app-subscriptions-2026)
- [SECONDARY-UNVERIFIED] RevenueCat 2026: Health & Fitness median revenue per install $0.48 at day 14 and $0.66 at day 60 (seen in a secondary write-up only). — [RevenueCat report landing page](https://revenuecat.com/report)
- [OFFICIAL company release] Flo Health (global, June-July 2024): nearly 70 million monthly active users and close to 5 million paid subscribers at its $200M+ raise. A later Flo newsroom page cites 73 million MAU and 6 million paid subscribers (undated in the snippet I saw). — [Flo newsroom](https://flo.health/newsroom/flo-health-raises-over-200m); [Flo newsroom, board announcement](https://flo.health/newsroom/mandy-ginsberg-joins-flo-health-board); [TechCrunch](https://www.techcrunch.com/2024/07/30/fertility-tracking-app-flo-health-raises-200m-at-a-1b-valuation)
- [SECONDARY-UNVERIFIED] 2026 secondary sources put Flo at about 77 million MAU, 420 million lifetime users and a "7% conversion rate". — [Space Coast Daily (Jan 2026)](https://spacecoastdaily.com/2026/01/one-in-five-u-s-women-already-use-flo-now-the-420-million-user-app-is-building-infrastructure-for-the-future-of-digital-health)
- [MEASURED/partner] Flo's link to care is a discount partnership, not a clinical funnel: Hims & Hers subscribers get 40% off Flo and 28 days of Natural Cycles. I found no Flo-to-telehealth conversion figure. — [Femtech Insider](https://femtechinsider.com/hims-hers-adds-natural-cycles-flo-health-and-other-wellness-partners-to-subscriber-benefits/)
- [COMPANY-REPORTED, US] K Health: more than 10 million people have interacted with its free AI symptom checker and 3.1 million have chosen to chat with a doctor or nurse; the CEO says about 70% of people who start an AI conversation complete a chat-based medical visit (no method given; "complete a visit" may not equal "pay"). — [Forbes Australia](https://www.forbes.com.au/news/innovation/this-ai-chatbot-has-helped-doctors-treat-3-million-people/)
- [VENDOR case study, US] Solv Health with Infermedica's symptom checker: over 25% of symptom-checker users continue to book an appointment, about twice the rate of non-users. Solv is a scheduling marketplace (closest structural analogue to Eleva). — [Infermedica case study](https://infermedica.com/case-studies/solv)
- [MEASURED, SaaS] General freemium benchmarks: ChartMogul median free-to-paid 8% across products, with 3-5% "good" and 8-12% "great" for self-serve freemium; First Page Sage average 3.7% for traditional freemium (80+ SaaS clients, 2021-2025). — [ChartMogul](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-conversion-report/); [Userpilot summary](https://userpilot.com/blog/freemium-to-premium)
- [OFFICIAL filing, US] Hims & Hers FY2025 10-K: marketing expense $919.3M (+35% YoY) of which customer acquisition costs $798.5M (up from $594.5M); management states marketing was 39% of revenue for the year, down from 46%; subscribers exceeded 2.5 million. Quiz-style intake is its front door, but no quiz-to-paid rate is disclosed. — [SEC 10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1773751/000177375126000022/hims-20251231.htm); [Earnings call transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/02/23/hims-hers-hims-earnings-call-transcript)

### Inferences
- Flo's paid share of monthly actives is about 7.1% (5M / 70M) to 8.2% (6M / 73M) by my calculation, and roughly 1.4% of lifetime users if the 420M figure is right. That is subscription to software, not to human care, and Flo is a daily-use product; a 3-7 day diary will sit well below it on any "share of installs that ever pay" measure.
- K Health's figures imply about 31% of everyone who touched the free tool went on to a clinician chat (3.1M / 10M, my calculation). With Solv's 25%, this suggests that when the free tool ends in a concrete clinical output and the next step is a clinician, hand-off rates in the 25-30% range are achievable in company-reported data. Both are US, both are acute-need symptom flows with same-day visits; a pelvic health diary is lower urgency. Treat 25-30% as a ceiling for "tapped through to book", not a forecast of paid sessions.
- A planning range the panel can defend for Eleva: 2% of diary installs paying (freemium median) as a conservative base; high single digits if the diary result is tied to a specific expert action; 25%+ only among users who complete a full diary and see a result-based referral. None of these is a measured tracker-to-care rate.
- Hard-paywall apps convert about 5.5x better per download than freemium ones (12.11% vs 2.18%), but that comes with far fewer downloads. For a top-of-funnel tool the relevant lesson is the reverse: a free, no-account tool maximises volume and accepts low conversion, so its economics depend on how cheaply each install is acquired (see section 8).

### Gaps
- No reported free-tracker-to-paid-care conversion rate from GoodRx, Zocdoc, Clue, Ovia, BabyCenter, MyFitnessPal, Calm, Headspace, Noom, Ada, Babylon, Kry/Livi, Doctolib, Ro, Sword or Hinge was found. Those companies publish user counts or revenue, not this funnel step. Ada partner conversion: nothing found.
- No European (let alone Portuguese or Spanish) free-to-paid care conversion figure found.
- OpenView product-led-growth benchmark and "engineering as marketing" case numbers (for example HubSpot Website Grader) were searched for and not retrieved.
- Hims & Hers gross new subscribers for 2025 were not retrieved, so a per-subscriber CAC cannot be computed from the filing data above.

## 4. Health app retention and engagement benchmarks, and what a 3-7 day diary implies

### Takeaway
Typical Health & Fitness apps keep somewhere between 3% and 9% of installers at day 30 depending on the source, and about half of users grant push permission; a diary that is designed to end after 3-7 days will sit at the bottom of that range, so the cross-sell must happen inside the diary episode (and via the exported report), not through later re-engagement.

### Cited Findings
- [VENDOR, stated sample] Airship push benchmarks for 2026 (data January-December 2025, more than 3 billion app users, 15 verticals, global): median opt-in rate 53.3% on Android and 48.85% on iOS, with the gap closing after Android 13 made permission explicit. For Medical, Health & Fitness, the top-decile Android direct open rate is 21.28%, "nearly double the overall average". — [Airship](https://www.airship.com/mobile-app-push-notification-benchmarks-for-2026/)
- [SECONDARY, citing Airship 2025] Medical, Health & Fitness median opt-in: 54.98% iOS and 58.48% Android. — [MobiLoud](https://www.mobiloud.com/blog/push-notification-statistics)
- [VENDOR, page not retrievable (HTTP 429); figures from search snippet] Adjust: global benchmark about 26% day-1, 13% day-7, 7% day-30; Health & Fitness day-1 retention about 24% in Europe and APAC versus 21% in North America. — [Adjust, user retention guide](https://adjust.com/resources/guides/user-retention)
- [MEASURED] Reminders matter for short diaries: in a phone bowel-diary validation study, adherence was 93.3% paper vs 95.0% app; notifications prompted 29.6% of app entries and raised adherence from 70% to 95%; the app was preferred. — [RTI](https://rti.org/publication/performance-acceptability-validation-phone-application-bowel-diary)
- [MEASURED, small] Automated bladder diary feasibility study: 12 of 13 patients (92%) preferred the electronic diary. — [ICS 2021 abstract 134](https://www.ics.org/2021/abstract/134)
- [MEASURED, trial registration] A UK crossover study comparing a 3-day electronic and 3-day paper bladder diary cites background evidence that a paper diary fitted with a hidden sensor was not opened on 32% of study days although reported compliance for those days exceeded 90% (paper diaries get back-filled). — [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT03249987](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03249987)
- [VENDOR study] Minze reports 84% of patients preferred its app diary and that 42% of paper diaries lacked leakage or urgency entries versus 3% of app diaries. — [Minze Health](https://minzehealth.com/?p=2576)
- [MEASURED] In-app events show an event card in search results to people who previously downloaded the app, a store-level re-engagement surface that does not depend on push permission. — [Apple Developer, Search](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/search/)

### Inferences
- With roughly half of users opting into push, and day-30 retention in single digits even for habit apps, a no-account diary has three realistic remarketing channels: push (about 50% reach, only while installed), an optional email captured at export time, and the PDF/report itself, which travels to a clinician. Without an account or email, a user who deletes the app after day 7 is unreachable. This weakens Option A's "no account needed" stance unless the export step asks for an email or offers an Eleva account at the moment of value.
- Diary-specific evidence points the other way on in-episode engagement: reminders lift completion substantially and patients prefer apps to paper. So push opt-in should be requested for the diary's own job ("remind me to log") rather than for marketing; that is both the highest-acceptance framing and the one that keeps the app installed through the conversion moment.
- Episodic use is not automatically bad for Option B: a single app benefits when the diary episode ends, because the user is already inside the product that holds booking, education and follow-up. In Option A the standalone app is at highest risk of deletion exactly when the diary is finished.
- Diaries recur clinically (before a first assessment, then after weeks of treatment). A "repeat your diary to compare" prompt at 6-12 weeks is a natural, non-promotional re-engagement hook in either option.

### Gaps
- I could not open the primary AppsFlyer, Adjust, Sensor Tower, Mixpanel or Amplitude 2025-2026 retention reports. Secondary compilations surfaced by search disagree widely for Health & Fitness: roughly 20-28% day-1, 8-18% day-7 and 3-9% day-30; another cites AppsFlyer Q3 2024 cross-category averages of 23.9% / 8.3% / 3.7% on iOS and 20.3% / 5.6% / 2.1% on Android. I could not pin these to a verifiable primary and have not listed them as findings. Candidate compilations: [GetStream](https://getstream.io/blog/app-retention-guide), [Enable3](https://enable3.io/blog/app-retention-benchmarks-2025), [Quash](https://quashbugs.com/blog/app-retention-rate-statistics).
- No retention benchmark specific to the Medical category or to short-episode trackers was found.
- No Europe-specific push opt-in figure for health apps. A Batch 2025 benchmark (Android opt-in falling from 85% to 67%, iOS from 58% to 56%) was seen only in a secondary summary and not verified.
- A paediatric paper-vs-mobile voiding diary comparison (63% vs 52% full completion, not significant; more data gaps on mobile) appeared in search but I could not confirm the source record, so it is excluded from findings.

## 5. In-app cross-sell design patterns in health contexts, and the effect of aggressive upsell on ratings

### Takeaway
The evidence that exists supports result-based, clinician-oriented hand-offs ("your diary shows X; share it with a specialist") over generic upsell, and review analyses of tracker apps consistently list paywalls and aggressive prompts among top complaints; but this is mostly company-reported or low-quality review mining, not controlled evidence.

### Cited Findings
- [VENDOR case study, US] A triage tool that ends in a concrete result doubles onward booking: over 25% of Solv symptom-checker users went on to book, twice the rate of non-users. — [Infermedica case study](https://infermedica.com/case-studies/solv)
- [COMPANY-REPORTED, US] K Health's model is a free AI intake whose output is handed to a clinician; 3.1 million of 10 million tool users chose a clinician chat. — [Forbes Australia](https://www.forbes.com.au/news/innovation/this-ai-chatbot-has-helped-doctors-treat-3-million-people/)
- [MEASURED, qualitative, Netherlands] Women using an app-based treatment for urinary incontinence valued that it lowered the barrier to treatment and raised awareness and adherence, and named the lack of face-to-face contact with a professional as a drawback. — [Wessels et al., JMIR 2020 (PMC7320303)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7320303/)
- [MEASURED] Notifications framed as task reminders are accepted and effective in diary contexts (29.6% of entries prompted by notifications; adherence 70% to 95%). — [RTI](https://rti.org/publication/performance-acceptability-validation-phone-application-bowel-diary)
- [LOW-QUALITY review mining, 2026] Aggregated review dashboards for period trackers report: "aggressive prompts" as the top complaint theme in recent Flo reviews; paywall/pricing complaints in 8 of 20 tracker apps reviewed; Clue reviews citing essential features being locked; users saying Flo Premium absorbed features that used to be free. These are AI-generated or vendor review summaries, not studies. — [Marlvel, Flo reviews](https://marlvel.ai/apps/flo-cycle-period-tracker/reviews); [Unstar research page](https://unstar.app/vi/research); [Unstar comparison](https://unstar.app/blog/flo-clue-stardust-apple-health-period-tracking-apps-ranked-2026)
- [OFFICIAL] Ratings and reviews "can influence how your app ranks in App Store search", so upsell-driven low ratings have a direct discovery cost. — [Apple Developer, Search](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/search/)
- [MEASURED, vendor dataset] Hard paywalls convert 12.11% of downloads versus 2.18% for freemium (global, RevenueCat 2025), which is why many trackers push paywalls early. — [RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025/)
- [OPINION, historical] Forced moves between a publisher's apps generated waves of negative reviews (Messenger, Swarm). — [CIO](https://www.cio.com/article/246432/social-messaging-apps-struggle-with-decision-to-bundle-or-unbundle.html); [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/why-breaking-up-is-hard-to-do-for-social-apps-like-facebook-foursquare-and-linkedin)

### Inferences
- Patterns that fit the evidence, in likely order of yield: (1) result-based referral at diary completion ("your 3-day diary shows 11 voids a day and 2 leaks; a pelvic physiotherapist can interpret this"); (2) export-to-clinician, where the PDF carries Eleva branding and a booking link or QR code and reaches both the patient's own clinician and potential new experts; (3) "share this diary with an Eleva specialist" as a one-tap booking that attaches the data; (4) content-led nurture from the Academy keyed to what the user logged; (5) a repeat-diary prompt weeks later. The first three need no push permission and no account before the moment of value.
- The qualitative finding that incontinence-app users miss professional contact is the opening for a human-care offer: it is a stated unmet need, so a specialist prompt reads as service, not upsell, provided the diary itself is never gated.
- A standalone free tool has more to lose from heavy upsell than an all-in-one app: its ratings are its ranking asset in a low-volume niche (a handful of 1-star "it's just an ad for Eleva" reviews can move a 100-rating average), and its "neutral tool" positioning is what clinicians would recommend. In Option B users already expect commerce inside the app, so prompts are less surprising, but the diary must still work fully for non-customers or the keyword-targeted product page will convert poorly and draw negative reviews.
- Timing implication from sections 3-4: the conversion window is the end of the diary episode. Prompts before the user has logged at least a day of data have no result to anchor to.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or controlled evidence on how upsell intensity affects ratings or reviews of health tracker apps. The review-mining sources are weak.
- No published A/B data on "share with a specialist" or export-to-clinician conversion rates.
- No practitioner write-up with numbers on tone or timing of referral prompts in pelvic health or women's health specifically.

## 6. The business-to-clinician loop: experts recommending a free tool as an acquisition channel

### Takeaway
Clinician prescription of apps is real but uneven (about half of surveyed Irish physiotherapists have prescribed an app; most South African ones have not), and clinicians choose on patient ease of use, accuracy and privacy rather than on official endorsement; Squeezy shows a clinician-authored, single-purpose pelvic health app can become a standard recommendation. I found no study comparing spread of a neutral tool versus a branded marketplace app through clinicians.

### Cited Findings
- [MEASURED, Ireland, 2023, n=158 members of the Irish Society of Chartered Physiotherapists] 76.4% use apps in clinical practice (50.4% daily) and 54.5% have prescribed apps to patients. Rated "very important" when choosing an app: easy for patients to use 96%; accurate information 88%; clearly stated purpose 77%; high data privacy 76%; engaging 68%. Rated "not important": data sharing between physio and client 38%; approval by HSE/NHS or other health system 36%; colleague recommendation 23%. — [World Physiotherapy Congress 2023 proceeding](https://world.physio/congress-proceeding/exploring-use-mobile-apps-clinical-practice-survey-irish-physiotherapists)
- [MEASURED, South Africa, 2020, n=255] 60% of physiotherapists used apps in practice, mostly for administration and communication, but most did not prescribe apps to patients and voiced concerns about doing so. — [South African Journal of Physiotherapy (PMC7203535)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7203535/)
- [MEASURED, Norway, primary care; low response rate of about 6.8%] 272 of 335 responding physiotherapists (81.2%) were positive about recommending digital health technologies to patients, with smartphone/tablet apps rated the most suitable tool. Source record not individually confirmed; it is one of these two papers. — [JMIR Rehabilitation 2024, e54116](https://rehab.jmir.org/2024/1/e54116/XML); [PLOS Digital Health 2024 (PMC11458024)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11458024/)
- [MEASURED, Australia] Health professionals largely did not recommend self-management apps for low back pain; 93% cited lack of knowledge of app effectiveness. — [Primary Health Care Research & Development (Cambridge)](https://core-cms.cambridgecore.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/E4F7F24610840357902EEFE0581E4E04/S1463423625000209a.pdf/health-professionals-use-of-smartphone-apps-for-clients-with-low-back-pain-an-observational-study.pdf)
- [COMPANY/affiliated, UK] Squeezy was designed by chartered pelvic health physiotherapists working in the NHS (clinical lead Myra Robson, Lewisham and Greenwich NHS Trust); the developer describes it as NHS-endorsed and recommended by professional journals; an early developer interview cites over 45,000 downloads. A 2017 user survey by the app's own clinical lead found over 90% of respondents increased pelvic floor exercise frequency after starting the app. — [Bladder & Bowel Community](https://www.bladderandbowel.org/news/squeezy-the-nhs-physiotherapy-app-for-pelvic-floor-muscle-exercises/); [POGP journal PDF](https://thepogp.co.uk/_userfiles/pages/files/11_14301024_0.pdf)
- [VENDOR case study, UK] Squeezy has a clinician-facing companion ("Living With Pelvic Health") through which clinicians monitor patients; a Barts NHS Trust trial reported average exercise adherence of 92.4% and plans to adopt it as standard care for some patients. — [Living With, Barts case study](https://livingwith.health/barts-nhs-trust/)
- [MEASURED/industry] Manufacturers already use the diary as a clinician-distributed asset: Coloplast Spain's printable "diario miccional" PDF for professionals includes links to download its iOS and Android app. — [Coloplast Spain PDF](https://prod-professional-es.coloplast.com/globalassets/hcp/pdf-file/v2/spain/cc/diariomiccional_460n0d2992.pdf)

### Inferences
- Squeezy's structure is the closest precedent for Option A: a single-purpose, clinician-authored patient app with a neutral name, plus a separate clinician-side product. Its adoption came through professional networks (POGP, NHS trusts), not store search. It is a paid-app/licensing business, not a funnel into a marketplace, so it proves the distribution channel but not the cross-sell.
- The Irish criteria (ease of use 96%, accuracy 88%, clear purpose 77%, privacy 76%) describe a single-purpose, no-account tool almost exactly. "Clearly stated purpose" in particular favours an app whose store listing and first screen are only the diary.
- Clinicians outside Eleva's network (hospital urologists, urogynaecologists, public-sector physiotherapists) have an obvious conflict in telling patients to install a competitor marketplace's app. A tool that works without an account and exports a standard PDF removes that objection; an "Eleva Care" app that opens on expert booking does not. This is reasoning, not measurement, but it is the strongest argument for Option A that is independent of ASO.
- For Eleva's own experts the reverse holds: they would rather send patients to the app where the patient can also rebook and message. Option B, or Option A with strong deep links, serves them equally.
- The Coloplast example shows the bladder diary is already treated as a branded lead-generation asset in Spain, and that paper/PDF remains the default clinical format. A PDF that mirrors the ICIQ-style layout clinicians expect is table stakes for clinician recommendation.

### Gaps
- No study or dataset comparing clinician recommendation rates for a neutral single-purpose tool versus a branded marketplace or provider app.
- No Portuguese or Spanish survey of physiotherapists' or urologists' app-prescribing behaviour found.
- No independent download or active-user figures for Squeezy, and no evidence on Physitrack or MyFitnessPal-with-dietitians as patient-acquisition loops was retrieved.
- No data on what share of patients install an app after a clinician recommends it.

## 7. Brand architecture: branded house, house of brands, or endorsed brand; naming, icon, and stigma

### Takeaway
Brand-architecture sources give only general trade-offs (a shared master brand concentrates equity and speeds launches; separate brands isolate risk and target distinct audiences), and stigma research confirms incontinence users value discretion; nothing I found tests app naming or icon discretion directly, so the endorsed form ("Eleva Diary by Eleva Care") is a reasoned compromise, not an evidenced winner.

### Cited Findings
- [OPINION, agency guides] Branded house: one master brand over every offer gives marketing efficiency and faster launches, but a problem in one product spreads to the rest. House of brands: independent brands protect each other from reputational damage and can target distinct audiences at higher cost. Hybrid/endorsed systems are common. Branded-house naming typically follows "parent + descriptor". — [Fabrik Brands](https://fabrikbrands.com/branding-matters/brand-strategy/brand-architecture-models-house-of-brands-vs-branded-house/); [Bigeye](https://www.bigeyeagency.com/insights/brand-architecture-masterbrand-endorsed-or-house-of-brands); [Branding Strategy Insider](https://brandingstrategyinsider.com/brand-architecture-for-competitive-advantage/)
- [MEASURED, vendor dataset, US 2025] Brand-term searches convert far better than generic ones in App Store search ads (73.5% vs 59.8% conversion; 25.3% vs 8.5% tap-through), i.e. a recognised name in the result row is worth a lot once people know it. — [AppTweak, Apple Ads benchmarks](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/apple-ads-benchmarks)
- [MEASURED, 2026, adults 50+] Focus groups and expert interviews on digital bladder health tools named stigma, low digital literacy and fear of doing exercises wrongly as barriers to app use. — [ICS 2026 abstract 175](https://www.ics.org/2026/abstract/175)
- [MEASURED, adolescents] The URApp continence app was designed around "discreet reminders" because young people with continence problems report stigma and fear of bullying. — [JMIR Pediatrics and Parenting 2021](https://pediatrics.jmir.org/2021/4/e26212)
- [MEASURED, qualitative] Women with urinary incontinence valued app-based treatment because it lowers the barrier to seeking treatment. — [JMIR 2020 (PMC7320303)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7320303/)
- [MEASURED, market observation] Existing apps in the niche mostly use literal descriptive names (Bladderly, UroBladderDiary, My Bladder, Bladder Diary Tracker, Minha Bexiga), while the clinician-recommended UK pelvic floor app uses a non-clinical name (Squeezy). — [App Store, My Bladder](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1372947470); [App Store, Bladder Diary Tracker](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1487355489); [App Store, Minha Bexiga](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1537991229); [Bladder & Bowel Community, Squeezy](https://www.bladderandbowel.org/news/squeezy-the-nhs-physiotherapy-app-for-pelvic-floor-muscle-exercises/)
- [OFFICIAL] On iOS the icon and name cannot vary by custom product page (only screenshots, promotional text and previews can), so one app has one home-screen identity. — [Apple Developer, Custom product pages](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/custom-product-pages/)

### Inferences
- There is a tension between ASO and discretion. The store title wants "Bladder Diary / Diário Miccional" for ranking; the home-screen label and icon want to say nothing about bladders. Both can be satisfied because the store name and the on-device display name are set separately: store listing "Eleva Diary: Diário Miccional", home-screen label "Eleva Diary" or "Diary", abstract icon. Notifications should likewise avoid condition words on the lock screen. This applies to Option A directly; in Option B the app is "Eleva Care" on the home screen, which is already discreet.
- Stigma cuts both ways on the one-vs-two question. A general "Eleva Care" women's health brand is arguably more discreet on a home screen than a dedicated bladder app; a dedicated app is more discreet as a download (no account, no marketplace profile, nothing tying identity to the condition). The "no account needed" property is the real discretion feature, and it can exist in either option.
- Endorsed naming ("Eleva Diary by Eleva Care") fits the dual audience: strangers see a tool, clinicians see a neutral-enough name, and the Eleva brand accrues recognition so later brand searches convert at brand-term rates. A fully separate brand would maximise neutrality for clinicians but forfeit that brand transfer, which is the point of the funnel. A pure branded house ("Eleva Care" only) is Option B.
- Risk isolation is a real branded-house cost for a small company: a ratings problem or store rejection on the diary would sit under the same developer name but not on the same listing in Option A; in Option B the diary's reviews and the marketplace's reviews share one rating.

### Gaps
- No study testing whether users with incontinence prefer a discreet single-purpose app or a general health brand on their home screen, and nothing on icon or name discretion for stigmatised conditions.
- No app-store-specific evidence on endorsed-brand naming performance (for example "X by Y" in titles) or on how developer-name association transfers ratings or trust between apps.
- Brand architecture sources are agency opinion pieces; none is specific to app portfolios.

## 8. Marketing cost context: cost per install, acquisition cost, Apple Search Ads, and long-tail value in Portuguese and Spanish

### Takeaway
Paid installs are cheap in Iberia relative to the US (median Apple Ads cost per install about $1.01 in Portugal and $1.43 in Spain versus $4.06 in the US, all categories, 2025), while paid acquisition of a care customer is expensive (Hims & Hers spends 39% of revenue on marketing); the organic value of a long-tail diary keyword in Portuguese is therefore small per install but meaningful if even a few percent of those installs become care customers.

### Cited Findings
- [VENDOR, stated sample] AppTweak Apple Ads benchmarks, data January-December 2025, nearly 3,500 apps, 50,000 campaigns, $1B spend, 38 countries, search-results placement. Median cost per install by country (all categories): United States $4.06; United Kingdom $2.60; Germany $2.14; France $1.78; Spain $1.43; Italy $1.32; Mexico $1.10; Brazil $1.05; Portugal $1.01. Median cost per tap: US $1.91; UK $1.35; Germany $1.11; France $0.92 (not given for Spain or Portugal). — [AppTweak, Apple Ads benchmarks (published 19 Aug 2026)](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/apple-ads-benchmarks)

- [VENDOR rule of thumb, US] A telehealth infrastructure vendor suggests $150 per new patient as a starting CAC assumption for most telehealth businesses. — [Bask Health](https://bask.health/blog/pricing-for-telemedicine-services)
- [OPINION] Apple's App Tracking Transparency made telehealth ads harder to target and measure, raising cost per acquired subscriber. — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/chat/h/7f510169-50ee-4dfe-996e-5c0bf849356c/)

### Inferences
- Worked value of an organic diary install, using only the figures above (illustrative, not a forecast): replacement cost of one install in Portugal is about $1 via Apple Ads. If 2% of diary installs become paying care customers (freemium median, section 3), each care customer acquired through the diary "costs" about $50 in install-equivalents, and about $13-20 at 5-8%. Against a $150 US telehealth CAC rule of thumb, the diary funnel is attractive on paper even at low conversion, but only if install volume exists.
- Because Portuguese installs are that cheap, the organic ranking is worth little in cash terms unless volume is meaningful: 200 organic installs a month is roughly $200 a month of paid-equivalent value. The strategic value of the standalone app is therefore less "free installs" and more (a) reaching people who would never search for or tap on a marketplace, and (b) clinician distribution (section 6).
- The same cheapness means Option B can buy diary-intent traffic directly: bid on "diário miccional" in Apple Ads and land on a diary-specific custom product page with a deep link into the diary. At about $1 an install this is a low-cost way to test real keyword volume and install-to-booking conversion before building and maintaining a second app.
- Spanish opens far larger long-tail volume (Spain plus Latin America, with Mexico and Brazil at about $1.05-1.10 per install), but Eleva can only monetise where it has experts, so installs outside served markets have brand value only.

### Gaps
- No Europe-specific or Iberia-specific Health & Fitness or Medical cost-per-install benchmark from a primary source (Adjust, AppsFlyer, Liftoff, Business of Apps) was retrieved; SplitMetrics' country and category data is gated.
- No published customer acquisition cost for European telehealth or marketplaces (Kry/Livi, Doctolib, and similar) was found.
- No Apple Search Ads popularity or bid data for bladder, incontinence or pelvic-floor keywords in any language.
- No keyword volume data for Portuguese or Spanish long-tail terms, so the "organic value" above is expressed per install, not in total.

## 9. Web alternative or complement: can a web diary/PDF tool plus SEO do the same top-of-funnel job?

### Takeaway
Partly. For this query type much demand is for a printable form (clinicians hand out PDFs, and the Portuguese and Spanish web results are dominated by PDFs and academic documents), so a web page with a free PDF and a simple web diary can capture search intent with no install friction, while a true multi-day diary still works better as an app because reminders drive completion. Web and app are complements; the web tool does not replace the store listing for people who search inside the stores.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- The European Portuguese web result page for this intent looks uncontested by consumer-grade pages. An eleva.care page titled around "diário miccional" with a free printable PDF, a short explainer from a pelvic physiotherapist, and a "do it on your phone" link is cheap to build, lives on the domain that already sells sessions (zero cross-app hop), and builds domain authority for adjacent pelvic health queries. This serves Option B directly and is the web equivalent of Option A's "find it without wanting Eleva".
- Web cannot fully substitute for the app on the core job: reliable multi-day logging depends on reminders and quick entry, which the diary studies tie to completion. A web diary without notifications will behave more like paper.
- Web is also the lowest-friction place to complete the paid step. Whichever app option is chosen, routing "book a specialist" to a web booking flow with the diary attached avoids the second-install loss identified in section 2.
- Web SEO and store search reach different people. Store searchers have already decided they want an app; web searchers often want a form or an explanation. The two top-of-funnel assets are additive, and the web one is far cheaper to test first.
- Clinician distribution (section 6) is format-agnostic: a QR code on a printed PDF can open the web page, which can then offer the app. That makes the web page the natural hub for clinician-led acquisition under either option.

### Gaps
- No search-volume data (Google Keyword Planner or similar) for "diário miccional", "diario miccional", "bladder diary" in Portugal or Spain; I could not quantify web versus store demand for this query.
- No current (2024-2026) survey on how people discover health apps (store search vs web search vs clinician vs social); the only health-specific numbers are from 2015.
- No direct comparison of a web diary versus an app diary on completion or on downstream booking.
- The SERP observation is from one query in one session through a US-based search tool; it should be re-run from a Portuguese IP with the actual target phrases before being relied on.

## Cross-cutting summary for scoring Options A and B (inference, built only from the findings above)

Acquisition
- Option A advantage, mechanism-based: title-level relevance and exact-match tap-through for diary keywords in every locale (section 1); a tool clinicians outside Eleva can recommend without endorsing a marketplace (section 6); no-account discretion at download (section 7).
- What reduces that advantage: since July 2025 Option B can show a diary-specific product page for diary keywords on iOS and a keyword-targeted listing on Google Play (section 1); the keyword niche is small by every signal available (section 1); installs in Portugal cost about $1 to buy (section 8); a web page plus PDF can capture the non-app intent for either option (section 9).
- Unknown that decides it: actual search volume for the diary terms in pt-PT and es-ES stores and on Google.

Conversion
- Option B advantage, measured only by proxy: no second install between diary result and booking; adjacent benchmarks suggest a second install hop loses 25-60% of users who tap (section 2); the end of a short diary episode is when a standalone app is most likely to be deleted (section 4).
- What reduces that advantage: Option A can route booking to the web with the diary attached, removing the install hop (sections 2 and 9); conversion in both options depends mainly on a result-based hand-off at diary completion (sections 3 and 5).
- Unknown that decides it: no one has published a cross-install or paid-conversion rate from a free health tracker to the same publisher's paid care app. The nearest company-reported hand-off rates (25-31%) come from US symptom checkers leading to same-day visits.

Cheapest way to replace the unknowns with data before committing: run Apple Ads on the diary keywords in Portugal and Spain against a diary-specific custom product page of a single app, and publish the web PDF page; both report real demand and real install-to-booking conversion for a few hundred euros.
